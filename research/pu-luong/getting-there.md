- S6 says bicycles are what visitors are offered, and that homestays often lend them without a charge. That is one page’s “often.” A hire sheet was not opened. Check on arrival.
- S6 says a local motorbike is occasional, not the usual hire.
- S13 also describes cycling between Bản Đôn and Bản Hiêu, and says many hamlets want cash. One commercial guide.
- S20 tells newcomers to walk with a group or a local guide. No guide fee on that page. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- No commune bus route was named on S1, S6, S7, or S20.
- The 2021 scheme’s tourist lines start at the administrative center in Cành Nàng (S3). They are scheme lines, not a measured transfer from a homestay.
- S10 prints about 25 km from Cành Nàng to Thác Hiêu. No second kilometer was opened.
- S13 prints about 6–8 km and 2–4 hours from Bản Đôn to Bản Hiêu and the waterfall, and about 15 km for Kho Mường–Hang Dơi–Bản Đôn. No second page prints those distances. Check on arrival.
- S8 prints walking times to the market: more than 10 km from Son–Bá–Mười, about 3 hours on foot from some Thành Sơn hamlets, and a 5:00 departure for a 7 km walk from old Cổ Lũng. Those are sellers’ walks in a 2022 article, not a visitor transfer.

Season limit on the journey:
- S1 and S6: late May to early June for green young rice; September and October for ripe gold. S6 also says the valley is visitable in any month, and that summer stays cooler than the lowland because of limestone, forest, and few people.
- S13 agrees with a new crop in May–June and ripe rice in September–October, and it tells readers to avoid July and August as storm months, with slippery roads. S1 and S6 do not say that.
- S20 calls both May–June and September–October ripe-rice months, golden, cool, and with little rain. That fights the green-versus-gold split on S1 and S6.
- S7 says Bản Đôn is busiest in February, March, September, and October.
