## What it is

- English name: Phi Yen temple
- Vietnamese name: An Sơn miếu; also Miếu bà Phi Yến
- Kicker (one line, what it is): The town shrine for Phi Yến
- Kind: sight

## What the visitor does

- The scene, in order: courtyard, yellow basin, tiled hall, incense burner at the three doors. Address on the newest special-zone page: khu dân cư số 3, đường Hoàng Phi Yến (S29). About 2 km southwest of the centre (S29, S30). The hall standing now was built in 1958 on the older shrine ground, after the earlier building collapsed when the French occupation of 1861 moved islanders to the mainland (S29). S31 says the first shrine was built in 1785 and rebuilt in 1958 after the French destroyed it in 1861. Both the 1785 first shrine and the 1958 hall stay. The public page uses the standing hall’s 1958 date.
- Worship: Phi Yến, local name Lê Thị Răm, plus Đô đốc Ngọc Lân and prince Hội An, called hoàng tử Cải (S29). S30 centers the shrine on Phi Yến.
- The local account on S29 says she died on 18/10 lunar 1785. The graphic telling (the butcher, the arm, the suicide) stays in the dossier. The public page says the local account places her death on that day and that the anniversary is held in this courtyard.
- Miếu Cậu, also Thiếu gia miếu, and the prince’s grave stay at Cỏ Ống near Đầm Trầu (S29). That is a different stop. Unpublished here: no separate page.
- How long on site: UNSOURCED. A provincial tourism card that printed an estimated 30 minutes returned HTTP 500 on 23 Oct 2026 and is not used.
- Mistake that wastes the visit: burning votive paper. S29 asks for a green offering basket and forbids votive burning at relics on the island.
- Notice: leave votive paper unburned.

## Season

- The anniversary. S29 says the giỗ is 18/10 lunar and, in the visit list, the festival runs 16–17–18/10 lunar. S30 and the 2020 Vietnamese relic note say 18/10 lunar. S17 (ministry, 26 Aug 2023) says 17 and 18 of the 10th lunar month at An Sơn miếu, and that the rite is national intangible heritage, with a fair of fruit trays, flower arranging, folk games, calligraphy, and đàn ca tài tử. The public page prints the newest span, the 16th through the 18th, and the heritage ranking. The 17–18 ministry span stays here.
- Solar dates move. Left as a lunar span. bestMonths unset.
