- Vietnamese name: bánh quy hạt bàng

### Ốc vú nàng
- Vietnamese name: ốc vú nàng
- What it is: A spire-shaped snail. Vietnam Tourism calls it the nipple snail, gray-black and knobby outside, smooth pink, white, or cream inside, found beside steep cliffs. Each block on that account says Source: Internet (S21). Báo Điện Biên Phủ calls it a specialty usually boiled or grilled for a natural sweet taste (S23).
- How to eat it: Boiled or grilled (S23). S21 also names sauté, salad, a steam in a little water, salt with pepper and lemon, and grilling with onion fat.
- When to order it: With the seafood meal. Check on arrival.
- Area, not a shop: Seaside restaurants and the fishing village (S23). No street.
- Meal: main
- Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Sources: S21, S23

### Tôm hùm đỏ
- Vietnamese name: tôm hùm đỏ; S23 says tôm hùm and does not say đỏ
- What it is: Vietnam Tourism says smaller than other lobsters, firmer and sweeter, nicknamed fire lobster. The same account says Côn Đảo is the country’s lobster-farming kingdom and, in the next lines, that these lobsters cannot be farmed and are only caught wild. Both lines stay. Each block says Source: Internet (S21).
- How to eat it: Sushi, salad, soup, or steamed whole. A female lobster in winter may carry golden roe along the spine (S21). S23 printed no method.
- When to order it: With the seafood meal. Check on arrival.
- Area, not a shop: Seaside restaurants (S23). No street.
- Meal: main
- Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Do not use the S24 range.
- Sources: S21, S23

Hạt bàng price, printed in S14 and not copied on the first pass: 250,000–400,000 đồng per kilogram, depending on the type. S24 prints 200,000–280,000 đồng per kilogram for the candied kernels and names no newspaper. The public guide uses the S14 range only. ## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Hạt bàng
- Vietnamese name: hạt bàng rang muối; mứt hạt bàng
- What it is: The kernel of trái bàng. The report says prisoners gathered leaves and fruit when they were kept without greens, and that the island now roasts the kernels with salt or makes candied kernels. It also says peak-season supply is bought in from the eastern and western provinces, so not every bag is fruit picked on the island (S14).
- How to eat it: As a gift pack from the market, not a sit-down dish. The report shows traders packing roasted kernels at chợ Côn Đảo (S14).
- When to order it: When leaving. It is the souvenir the report says visitors buy.
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Côn Đảo, in the town
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site
- Sources: S14

### Cua mặt trăng
- Vietnamese name: cua mặt trăng
- What it is: A crab the report says carries red spots on the shell like a moon. Locals told the reporter the meat is best in the week of the rising moon. Shells are also used as decoration (S14).
- How to eat it: As a seafood meal. The night market cooks crab and other seafood to order, steamed or grilled (S15). A recipe was not printed.
- When to order it: Evening, at the night market. The “rising moon” line is the newspaper’s local tip, not a tested calendar.
- Area, not a shop: Chợ đêm Côn Đảo
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S14, S15

## Food streets and markets

### Chợ đêm Côn Đảo
- Vietnamese name: Chợ đêm Côn Đảo
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats seafood and street snacks in the open yard in front of the daily market. The gate is on Phạm Văn Đồng. The streets named are Võ Thị Sáu and Phạm Văn Đồng. Visitors pick seafood and the stall cooks it. Grilled snacks, corn, sweet potato, bánh tráng nướng, and cơm cháy are also there (S15).
- When to go: From 17:00. Busiest 19:00–22:00 every day in that report. Peak season and weekends can run until before 02:00. No roof, so rain moves the tables (S15).
- Area: Town centre
- Suggested slug: con-dao-night-market
- Photo must show: the open yard at night, stalls, and people at tables, the street still visible. A plate with no market fails.
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S15

### Chợ Côn Đảo
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Côn Đảo; the daily market the night market sits in front of
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Buys hạt bàng and dried seafood to take away. The Đồng Nai report shows traders at this market packing roasted kernels (S14). A market-hall address and a daytime hour were not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: Daytime for the packs. The night market is the evening use of the same yard (S15).
- Area: Town centre, in front of which the night market opens
- Suggested slug: con-dao-market
- Photo must show: the market aisle and the goods, in daylight, not a single packet filling the frame
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S14, S15
