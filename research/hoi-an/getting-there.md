# getting there

Checked: 21 Oct 2026

- Nearest airport: Da Nang International Airport. AIP name VVDN — DA NANG/Da Nang International, administered by the Civil Aviation Authority of Viet Nam and Airports Corporation of Viet Nam. The AIP places it southwest of Da Nang city, 3.2 km from the city centre (S10). Vietnam Airlines uses IATA DAD, about 3 km southwest of the Da Nang city center, about 10–15 minutes by car to that center, with a domestic terminal and an international terminal (S11). Those two official lines agree on a short hop into Da Nang. They are not a Hoi An transfer time. The AIP lists aerodrome administration, customs, and air traffic services as H24 (S10).
- Nearest rail station: The Vietnam Railways SE board checked on 21 Oct 2026 lists Ga Đà Nẵng on every SE train in both directions. It does not list a Hoi An station. Tra Kieu appears on SE10 in the northbound table (km 901, between Tam Ky and Da Nang) and is absent from the southbound SE columns on that same page (S12). The street address of Ga Đà Nẵng is not on that timetable. Address: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Road time from the station to Hoi An: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Do not send someone to Tra Kieu until a sourced road time exists.
- From the usual previous stop: Da Nang, then the road south. Hue is the stop before that, by rail into Da Nang or by road over Hai Van.

- Getting around on site: Walk inside the ancient town. The streets are narrow and the evening is on foot (S1, S5). Phan Chau Trinh’s published pedestrian window is 17:30–21:30 in summer and until 21:00 in winter, every day, on the 2023 segment (S4). A bicycle or a short ride is the way to An Bang; the minutes are Check on arrival — UNSOURCED (S6 does not give them). Bus 02 is the public link from Da Nang and it continues to Cua Dai, so the stop for the old town is the Hoi An bus station on that route, not the beach end (S13). The walk from that station to the bridge is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Ride-hailing inside the town was not on the pages checked.
- Season limit on the journey: Vietnam Tourism, Hoi An section: September–January rainy, cool, cloudy; February–August warm to hot and clear; from September, storms hit central Vietnam; September–November means a lot of rain and a few small floods; May–August can be hot enough that people go to the beach; January is the coldest point in that write-up (S7). UNESCO says the adverse effects of annual flooding are being controlled (S1). Do not promise a dry road from Da Nang, or a walk on Bach Dang, in a flood week without checking that morning. No flood depth is recorded here.
