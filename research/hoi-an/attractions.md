# Research dossier — Hoi An

Slug: `hoi-an`
Province: Da Nang city. The Government portal’s ward list for the new Da Nang city puts the old core in Hoi An ward, Cửa Đại in Hoi An Đông ward, and Cẩm An in Hoi An Tây ward (S8). The conservation centre’s own footer uses Phường Hội An, TP. Đà Nẵng (S4). UNESCO’s list page and the national tourism page still print Quảng Nam (S1, S7). Search and book it as Hoi An.
Kind: town
Corridor: central-coast
Base a traveler sleeps in: the lanes just off the ancient-town core, or a villa or homestay at An Bang
Checked: 21 Oct 2026

## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: a tight row of tiled timber shophouses, the roofed wooden bridge, and the river the houses back onto. UNESCO’s list page places the town on the north bank near the mouth of the Thu Bồn, a 30 ha property with a 280 ha buffer, 1,107 timber-frame buildings, an open market, a ferry quay, and a street grid with one axis parallel to the river (S1). The heritage department gives the same picture in fewer words: moss-coloured old streets, 30 km southeast of Da Nang, and the Japanese bridge among the surviving works (S2). This is not a beach postcard and not the airport.
- Where they sleep, as an area: either the lanes just off the ancient-town core, because the evening is walked, or An Bang, where the June 2024 ministry article describes villa and homestay lodging as the stay foreign visitors use (S6). No hotel brand. The same article puts An Bang and Tân Thành in Cẩm An ward and says Cửa Đại’s erosion is serious, with Tân Thành and An Bang also threatened, so do not sell Cửa Đại as the intact swim. The whole of Cẩm An ward was folded into Hoi An Tây ward (S8). Cửa Đại ward was folded into Hoi An Đông (S8).
- Where the evening is: on foot in the ancient town. The creative-city visitor page prints the old-town pedestrian hours as 09:00–21:30 in summer and 09:00–21:00 in winter, and a separate clock for Phan Châu Trinh (S5). That page does not show a publication date. The heritage centre’s 15 May 2023 notice is the dated rule for the Phan Châu Trinh extension (S4).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): the Japanese bridge, Chùa Cầu, at the join of Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai and Trần Phú. The heritage centre’s Vietnamese monument page says Japanese merchants built it in the early 17th century, the temple was added in 1653, Lord Nguyễn Phúc Chu named it Lai Viễn Kiều in 1719, and the wooden bridge is about 18 m (S3). The same centre’s French page says 1593 and, later on that page, the early 17th century (S3b). UNESCO’s English list text calls it an 18th-century bridge with a pagoda (S1). Do not pick one date. Primary URL: https://www.hoianworldheritage.org.vn/vi/news/Diem-di-tich/Chua-Cau-8.hwh. Two evening pins from the centre’s 17 March 2025 ticket article, with no prices and no closing hour on that page: traditional music at 66 Bạch Đằng, and the Japanese culture house at 06 Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai (S10). They orient the walk. They are not a ranked list.
- Hero must include: the tiled timber row and either the bridge roof or the river in the same frame, subject through the centre. Colour. Not the airport, not a military frame, not a beach with no town.
- A crop that would fail: a lantern close-up with no street, a frame that is only sand or only water, the bridge with its roof cut off, a panorama whose centre is empty sky, a statue cropped through the chest.

### Ancient town night
- Vietnamese name: Khu phố cổ Hội An
- Kind: area
- Area: Ancient town, now inside Hoi An ward. That ward was formed from Minh An, Cẩm Phô, Sơn Phong, Cẩm Nam, and Cẩm Kim (S8). The conservation centre publishes its office at 10B Trần Hưng Đạo, Phường Hội An (S4). UNESCO gives the inscribed property as 30 ha, buffer 280 ha, coordinates N15 52 60 E108 19 60 (S1).
- What the visitor does: Walk the narrow timber streets while the shops are still open and before the pedestrian hours end. The creative-city page prints 09:00–21:30 in summer and 09:00–21:00 in winter for the old-town zone (S5). Phan Châu Trinh is the 2023 extension: from 15 May 2023, every day, 17:30–21:30 in summer and until 21:00 in winter, from the Nguyễn Huệ intersection to the Cao Hồng Lãnh junction (S4). A later stage, described as expected from 1 January 2024, would start at 15:00 and run from the Hoàng Diệu junction (S4). The visitor page checked on 21 Oct 2026 still prints the 17:30 start (S5). Whether the afternoon start happened is Check on arrival. The monthly lantern night is a separate event. This lane did not write it up.
- Time: An evening on foot. The old-town window on S5 runs from morning through the night. The dated Phan Châu Trinh window is the evening only (S4).
- Fee: The conservation centre treats entry to the heritage area as a ticket, and the May 2026 article says the town is an open neighbourhood, which is why many people walk in without one (S11). Two official counts disagree. The 17 March 2025 heritage article: 120,000 VND for five sights and 80,000 VND for three sights (S10). The creative-city page: 80,000 VND for a Vietnamese visitor (the general streetscape and three sights) and 120,000 VND for a foreign visitor (the streetscape and five sights); children under 16 free; students, people with revolutionary merit, and people over 60 at 50 percent, printed as “2 người/1 vé” (S5). The 5 May 2026 article, quoting the centre’s director: since 2012 the price has stayed 80,000 VND for four cultural works and 120,000 VND for six (S11). Record both counts. Do not pick one. Which rule is enforced that day: Check on arrival.
- From the base: Walk, if the night’s sleep is just off this core. A ride from An Bang. The 2024 coast article does not give minutes (S6). Ride length: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: Late afternoon into the published pedestrian hours. Not a flood peak. The Da Nang city climate page, updated 4 May 2026, puts the rainy season in October–December and says floods are usual in September–November (S12). A national news report on 29 Oct 2025 describes Bạch Đằng, Nguyễn Thái Học, and Lê Lợi under 1–1.5 m of water (S13). That is one flood, not the calendar.
- Mistake to avoid: Treating every night as the lantern festival, booking the beach and expecting to walk these streets, or telling someone the streets are free because the town is open. Also, do not turn the 2016 bus list into the current ride.
- Local interest: Old town at night
- Suggested slug: no separate page yet. This is the place. The published sights inside it stay `japanese-bridge` and `tan-ky`.
- Suggested dot: do not add a record in the copy pass, and do not change the place dot
- Photo must show: tiled timber houses in a tight row, with the bridge roof or the river, subject through the centre
