## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: a narrow street of tiled timber shophouses in one unbroken row, the wooden Japanese bridge with its roof, and the river the houses back onto. UNESCO places the town on the north bank near the mouth of the Thu Bon, 1,107 timber-frame buildings, an open market, a ferry quay, and a grid with one axis parallel to the river (S1). The heritage department gives the same picture: moss-coloured old streets, the Japanese bridge, assembly halls, and a living town, 30 km southeast of Da Nang (S2). This is not a beach postcard and not the airport.
- Where they sleep, as an area: either the lanes just off the ancient-town core, because the core is walked in the evening, or An Bang beach, where villa and homestay lodging is the stay the June 2024 ministry article describes for foreign visitors (S6). No hotel brand. Cua Dai is the other named beach on that page, and the same page says erosion there is serious, so do not sell Cua Dai as the intact swim. An Bang was in Cam An ward in that 2024 article. The July 2025 ward list does not put Cam An inside Hoi An ward or Hoi An Dong ward (S8, S9). The new ward name for the beach is Check on arrival.
- Where the evening is: on foot in the ancient town. The chairman of the old Hoi An city, quoted by ANTV on 16 May 2023, named Bach Dang, Tran Phu, and Nguyen Thai Hoc as the streets with no room to move, especially from 17:00 to 21:00 (S5). The formal pedestrian hours published for the 2023 extension are on Phan Chau Trinh, not a bar street (S4, S5).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): the wooden Japanese bridge, the pin at the river end of the street grid. UNESCO’s English text calls it an 18th-century bridge with a pagoda; the Japanese text on the same page dates the covered Japanese bridge to 1593 (S1). Do not pick one date here. The heritage department calls it cầu Nhật Bản (S2). Primary URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/948/. Phan Chau Trinh is the named pedestrian extension, not a venue: from 15 May 2023, every day, 17:30–21:30 in summer and until 21:00 in winter, on the segment from the Nguyen Hue intersection to the Cao Hong Lanh junction (S4). ANTV gives the same clock times (S5). Which calendar months are “summer” and “winter” is not on those pages. A later stage, described as expected from 1 January 2024, would start at 15:00 and run from the Hoang Dieu junction (S4). Whether that afternoon start happened is Check on arrival. No bar and no restaurant is a pin. The May 2023 program named performance stops, including a music group at 49 Phan Chu Trinh; those are not current picks, and the schedule is Check on arrival (S4).
- Hero must include: the tiled timber row and either the bridge roof or the river in the same frame, subject through the center. Colour. Not the airport, not a military frame, not a beach with no town.
- A crop that would fail: a lantern close-up with no street, a frame that is only sand or only water, the bridge with its roof cut off, a panorama whose center is empty sky, a statue cropped through the chest.

### Ancient town night
- Vietnamese name: Khu phố cổ Hội An
- Kind: area
- Area: Ancient town, now inside Hoi An ward. That ward was formed from Minh An, Cam Pho, Son Phong, Cam Nam, and Cam Kim. The published ward office is 9 Tran Hung Dao (S9). The inscribed property is 30 ha, buffer 280 ha, coordinates on the UNESCO page N15 52 60 E108 19 60 (S1).
- What the visitor does: Walk the narrow streets of timber houses after the shops are still open and before the pedestrian hours end. The evening the chairman described is Bach Dang, Tran Phu, and Nguyen Thai Hoc from about 17:00 to 21:00 (S5). Phan Chau Trinh is the added pedestrian segment with the clock times above (S4). The monthly lantern night is a separate event. This lane did not re-source its date.
- Time: An evening on foot. The published pedestrian window on Phan Chau Trinh is about four hours in summer and three and a half in winter (S4).
- Fee: Walking the streets is not given a price on these pages. UNESCO says entrance-ticket revenue is spent on the property, and it does not publish the figure (S1). Ticket price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Leave the house-ticket rules to the attractions lane.
- From the base: Walk, if the night’s sleep is just off this core. A ride from An Bang. The ride length is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The 2024 coast article does not give minutes (S6).
- When to go: Late afternoon into the published pedestrian hours. Not a flood peak. Vietnam Tourism says September–November brings a lot of rain and a few small floods (S7). UNESCO says annual flooding is being controlled, not that it has stopped (S1).
- Mistake to avoid: Treating every night as the lantern festival, or booking the beach and expecting to walk to these streets. Also, do not treat the 2016 city bus list as the current ride into town.
- Local interest: Old town at night
- Suggested slug: no separate page yet. This is the place. The published sights inside it stay `japanese-bridge` and `tan-ky`.
- Suggested dot: do not add a record in the copy pass, and do not change the place dot
- Photo must show: tiled timber houses in a tight row, with the bridge roof or the river, subject through the center
- Sources: S1, S2, S4, S5, S7, S9
