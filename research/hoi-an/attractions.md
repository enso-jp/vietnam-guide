# Research dossier — Hoi An

Slug: `hoi-an`
Province: Da Nang city. The Government portal’s ward list for the new Da Nang city puts the old core in Hoi An ward, Cửa Đại in Hoi An Đông ward, and Cẩm An in Hoi An Tây ward (S8). The conservation centre’s own footer uses Phường Hội An, TP. Đà Nẵng (S4). UNESCO’s list page and the national tourism page still print Quảng Nam (S1, S7). Search and book it as Hoi An.
Kind: town
Corridor: central-coast
Base a traveler sleeps in: the lanes just off the ancient-town core, or a villa or homestay at An Bang
Checked: 21 Oct 2026

## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: a tight row of tiled timber shophouses, the roofed wooden bridge, and the river the houses back onto. UNESCO’s list page places the town on the north bank near the mouth of the Thu Bồn, a 30 ha property with a 280 ha buffer, 1,107 timber-frame buildings, an open market, a ferry quay, and a street grid with one axis parallel to the river (S1). The heritage department gives the same picture in fewer words: moss-coloured old streets, 30 km southeast of Da Nang, and the Japanese bridge among the surviving works (S2). This is not a beach postcard and not the airport.
- Where they sleep, as an area: either the lanes just off the ancient-town core, because the evening is walked, or An Bang, where the June 2024 ministry article describes villa and homestay lodging as the stay foreign visitors use (S6). No hotel brand. The same article puts An Bang and Tân Thành in Cẩm An ward and says Cửa Đại’s erosion is serious, with Tân Thành and An Bang also threatened, so do not sell Cửa Đại as the intact swim. The whole of Cẩm An ward was folded into Hoi An Tây ward (S8). Cửa Đại ward was folded into Hoi An Đông (S8).
- Where the evening is: on foot in the ancient town. The heritage centre’s visitor notice of 26 Jan 2026 prints the old-town pedestrian hours as 09:00–21:30 in summer and 09:00–21:00 in winter, and Phan Châu Trinh as 17:30–21:30 in summer and 17:30–21:00 in winter (S20). The centre’s 15 May 2023 notice is the dated rule for the Phan Châu Trinh extension, and it still described a later stage, expected from 1 January 2024, that would start at 15:00 (S4). The January 2026 notice still prints 17:30 (S20). Whether the afternoon start is in force is Check on arrival.
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): the Japanese bridge, Chùa Cầu, at the join of Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai and Trần Phú. The heritage centre’s Vietnamese monument page says Japanese merchants built it in the early 17th century, the temple was added in 1653, Lord Nguyễn Phúc Chu named it Lai Viễn Kiều in 1719, and the wooden bridge is about 18 m (S3). The same centre’s French page says 1593 and, later on that page, the early 17th century (S3b). UNESCO’s English list text calls it an 18th-century bridge with a pagoda (S1). Do not pick one date. Primary URL: https://www.hoianworldheritage.org.vn/vi/news/Diem-di-tich/Chua-Cau-8.hwh. Two pins from the centre, with no prices: traditional music at 66 Bạch Đằng (S10), and the Japanese culture house at 06 Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai, where the January 2026 notice prints a tea ceremony at 09:30 and 16:30 (S20). They orient the walk. They are not a ranked list. No bar pin turned up on a 2026 primary page.
- Hero must include: the tiled timber row and either the bridge roof or the river in the same frame, subject through the centre. Colour. Not the airport, not a military frame, not a beach with no town, not a rice field.
- A crop that would fail: a lantern close-up with no street, a frame that is only sand or only water, the bridge with its roof cut off, a panorama whose centre is empty sky, a statue cropped through the chest.

### Ancient town night
- Vietnamese name: Khu phố cổ Hội An
- Kind: area
- Area: Ancient town, now inside Hoi An ward. That ward was formed from Minh An, Cẩm Phô, Sơn Phong, Cẩm Nam, and Cẩm Kim (S8). The conservation centre publishes its office at 10B Trần Hưng Đạo, Phường Hội An (S4). UNESCO gives the inscribed property as 30 ha, buffer 280 ha, coordinates N15 52 60 E108 19 60 (S1).
- What the visitor does: Walk the narrow timber streets while the shops are still open and before the pedestrian hours end. The January 2026 notice prints 09:00–21:30 in summer and 09:00–21:00 in winter for the old-town zone, and 17:30–21:30 in summer and 17:30–21:00 in winter for Phan Châu Trinh, from the Nguyễn Huệ intersection to the Cao Hồng Lãnh junction (S20). The 2023 notice described a later stage from the Hoàng Diệu junction starting at 15:00, expected from 1 January 2024 (S4). The January 2026 notice does not print that 15:00 start (S20). Whether it happened is Check on arrival. The monthly lantern night is a separate event.
- Time: An evening on foot. The old-town window on S20 runs from morning through the night. The dated Phan Châu Trinh window is the evening only (S4, S20).
- Fee: Two official systems are in print, and they do not match. From 10 August 2026, Da Nang city council Resolution 98/2026/NQ-HĐND of 31 July 2026 sets 20,000 VND per person per building, the same for a Vietnamese and a foreign visitor (S19). Nhân Dân on 7 August 2026 reports the same 20,000 VND figure (S22). The heritage centre’s own notices, later than the old nationality split and earlier than that resolution, still describe combination tickets: 80,000 VND for the streetscape, Chùa Cầu or Miếu Quan Công, and two other cultural works; 120,000 VND for the streetscape, one of those two landmarks, a museum, and three other cultural works (S20, 26 Jan 2026; S21, 16 Mar 2026). S21 says one ticket can be used through the stay, up to three days. The May 2026 article, quoting the centre’s director, says the town is an open neighbourhood, which is why many people walk in without a ticket, and gives a third count: 80,000 VND for four cultural works and 120,000 VND for six, unchanged since 2012 (S11). The creative-city page splits 80,000 and 120,000 by nationality and by three sights versus five (S5). Do not pick one price. Which desk is in force that day: Check on arrival. Free categories quoted from Resolution 02/2026 on the January notice: Vietnamese disabled people with the certificate, Vietnamese people covered by the 2003 cultural-access decision, children under 16 and pupils, Vietnamese citizens 60 and over, and diplomatic guests (S20).
- From the base: Walk, if the night’s sleep is just off this core. A ride from An Bang. The 2024 coast article does not give minutes (S6). Ride length: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: Late afternoon into the published pedestrian hours. Not a flood peak. The Da Nang city climate page, updated 4 May 2026, puts the rainy season in October–December and says floods are usual in September–November (S12). A national news report on 29 Oct 2025 describes Bạch Đằng, Nguyễn Thái Học, and Lê Lợi under 1–1.5 m of water (S13). That is one flood, not the calendar.
- Mistake to avoid: Treating every night as the lantern festival, booking the beach and expecting to walk these streets, or telling someone a single ticket price. Also, do not turn the 2016 bus list into the current ride.
- Local interest: Old town at night
- Suggested slug: no separate page. This is the place. The published sights inside it stay `japanese-bridge` and `tan-ky`.
- Suggested dot: do not add a record, and do not change the place dot
- Photo must show: tiled timber houses in a tight row, with the bridge roof or the river, subject through the centre
- Sources: S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S8, S10, S11, S12, S13, S19, S20, S21, S22

## Attractions (Sight, area of interests, zone, hub, etc.)

### Japanese Covered Bridge
- Vietnamese name: Chùa Cầu / Cầu Nhật Bản / Lai Viễn Kiều
- Kind: sight
- Area: West end of the ancient town, at Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai and Trần Phú (S3)
- What the visitor does: Walk the short covered wooden bridge. A small temple sits on it. Dogs at one end, monkeys at the other, yin-yang tiles (S3). On the January and March 2026 ticket notices it is one of the two landmark choices, with Miếu Quan Công, inside the 80,000 and 120,000 VND tickets (S20, S21). From 10 August 2026 the city fee is 20,000 VND per building (S19). Which of those is sold: Check on arrival.
- Time: 20 minutes. The bridge is short, about 18 m (S3). Do not budget a museum hour.
- Fee: See the ancient-town fee above. No separate bridge price was printed on the monument page (S3). Interior versus looking from the street: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- From the base: Walk, if the night is just off the old town.
- When to go: Morning, before groups, or at the edge of the pedestrian hours. The monument page does not print a door time.
- Mistake to avoid: Picking one build date. Vietnamese page: early 17th century, temple 1653, name Lai Viễn Kiều in 1719 (S3). French page on the same site: 1593 and also the early 17th century (S3b). UNESCO English list: 18th century (S1).
- Local interest: Old town
- Suggested slug: japanese-bridge
- Suggested dot: already 4 — do not change
- Photo must show: the covered bridge from the street, roof on, in colour, one frame. A second frame is the temple side or the dogs and monkeys, not the same view cropped tighter.
- Sources: S1, S3, S3b, S19, S20, S21

### Tan Ky House
- Vietnamese name: Nhà cổ Tấn Ký
- Kind: sight
- Area: Ancient town, 101 Nguyễn Thái Học. The creative-city page says the back of the street runs to the Thu Bồn at the Châu Thượng Văn junction (S23). That page’s byline is Traveloka. The address is used. The page’s 1741 date and its claim that UNESCO listed the house in 1990 are not used: the town was inscribed in 1999 (S1, S2), and the byline is not the centre’s monument notice.
- What the visitor does: Walk a tube house with no windows, light from the courts, Chinese characters on the columns, and a glass-cased cup the page calls a Confucius cup. The ground floor is the visit. The family still lives upstairs. Do not touch the objects (S23). The January 2026 ticket notice names old houses among the optional cultural works on the combination ticket (S20).
- Time: A short house visit. No minute count on S23. Plan on well under an hour. Exact minutes: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Fee: No separate house price on S23. It sits in the ancient-town fee disagreement (S19, S20, S21). Check on arrival.
- From the base: Walk. Nguyễn Thái Học is inside the pedestrian zone (S20, S23).
- When to go: The creative-city page prints Monday–Sunday 08:00–11:30 and 13:30–17:45 (S23). The ticket notice does not repeat those hours (S20). Because the byline is Traveloka, confirm the split day on arrival.
- Mistake to avoid: Talking through the visit. The upstairs is a home (S23). Also, do not treat the house as a night sight. The printed hours end in the afternoon.
- Local interest: Old merchant houses
- Suggested slug: tan-ky
- Suggested dot: already 4 — do not change
- Photo must show: the timber tube-house front on Nguyễn Thái Học, sign and tiles in one frame. A second frame is the inner court or the carved wood, not the street again.
- Sources: S20, S23

### Quan Cong Temple
- Vietnamese name: Chùa Ông / Miếu Quan Công
- Kind: sight
- Area: 24 Trần Phú, Hoi An ward (S25)
- What the visitor does: Enter the Chinese-style hall built for Quan Công (Quan Vũ) by Hoa merchants in the mid-17th century, about 1653. Tube-tile roof, carved dragons and phoenixes, the statue in the main hall (S25). On the 2026 combination tickets it is the other landmark choice beside Chùa Cầu (S20, S21).
- Time: A short hall. No minute count on S25. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Fee: The ancient-town fee. No separate temple price on the city page (S25). During the 2026 Nguyên tiêu window the centre said it would not sell a sightseeing ticket for Chùa Ông on those days (S34). That was a dated plan, not the ordinary rule.
- From the base: Walk along Trần Phú.
- When to go: Morning. Door hours are not on the city page (S25). Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The heavy day is the 16th of the first lunar month, when people come for Nguyên tiêu (S25).
- Mistake to avoid: Calling it a Buddhist pagoda because the name says chùa. The city page describes a temple to Quan Công (S25).
- Local interest: Assembly halls and temples
- Suggested slug: quan-cong
- Suggested dot: 4 — one of the two landmarks on the combination ticket, a stop inside the old-town walk, not a day by itself
- Photo must show: the temple front on Trần Phú, roof and gate in one colour frame. Not a document and not a military photograph.
- Sources: S20, S21, S25, S34

### Fujian Assembly Hall
- Vietnamese name: Hội quán Phúc Kiến / Chùa Phước Kiến / Kim Sơn Tự
- Kind: sight
- Area: 46 Trần Phú, even side, near the market. The centre’s monument file says the hall is about 120 m deep, through three courts, from Trần Phú back to the Phan Chu Trinh–Nguyễn Huệ crossing (S24).
- What the visitor does: Walk the long hall. It began in 1697 as Kim Sơn Tự. From 1757 the Fujian congregation rebuilt it as an assembly hall for worship, the earlier merchants, and meetings (S24). The main hall is Thiên Hậu. The January 2026 ticket notice names assembly halls among the optional cultural works (S20).
- Time: Longer than the bridge, because of the courts. No minute count on S24. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Fee: The ancient-town fee. No separate price and no door hours on S24. Check on arrival.
- From the base: Walk. The file gives the way in from the Hoi An bus station via Huỳnh Thúc Kháng and Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai, across the bridge onto Trần Phú, and also via Phan Chu Trinh or Trần Hưng Đạo to Nguyễn Huệ (S24).
- When to go: Morning. Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Mistake to avoid: Spending every optional ticket punch on assembly halls that repeat each other. This is the long one. The file does not rank the others.
- Local interest: Assembly halls
- Suggested slug: phuc-kien
- Suggested dot: 4 — the hall to enter if the walk includes one assembly hall
- Photo must show: the outer gate or the red hall and a court, subject through the centre, in colour. A portrait crop that cuts the roof fails.
- Sources: S20, S24

### An Bang Beach
- Vietnamese name: Bãi biển An Bàng
- Kind: sight
- Area: An Bang, Cẩm An ward in the June 2024 article, now inside Hoi An Tây (S6, S8). Recognized as a local tourism site in the province’s May 2018 list, together with Cửa Đại (S35).
- What the visitor does: Swim or walk the sand from a villa and homestay base. The ministry article describes that lodging as the stay foreign visitors use, and it says erosion also threatens An Bang and Tân Thành. Cửa Đại’s erosion is described as serious (S6). Do not send the swim to Cửa Đại.
- Time: Half a day if it is the beach, or a morning if the old town has the afternoon.
- Fee: No entry fee was printed on S6 or S35. Chair hire: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Do not write “free” without a tariff.
- From the base: A ride or a bicycle from the old town. Minutes and kilometres: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The 7 km figure on secondary pages was not on a page this pass could open.
- When to go: Morning, in the drier months. The city climate page calls January–September dry and puts floods in September–November, rain in October–December (S12). That is the city’s climate page, not a beach flag.
- Mistake to avoid: Sleeping here and expecting to walk the lantern streets, or sleeping in the old town and expecting the sand at the end of the lane.
- Local interest: An Bang
- Suggested slug: an-bang
- Suggested dot: already 3 — do not change
- Photo must show: the sand, the water, and the low beach buildings in one frame, subject through the centre. Not a resort logo and not an empty strip of sand.
- Sources: S6, S8, S12, S35

### Tra Que vegetable village
- Vietnamese name: Làng rau Trà Quế
- Kind: sight
- Area: About 2.5 km north of the ancient-town centre, between the Đế Võng river and the Trà Quế lagoon. The 2020 heritage page still says Cẩm Hà commune (S26). Cẩm Hà is now inside Hoi An Tây (S8).
- What the visitor does: Walk the herb beds the town’s bowls use, including the greens on a cao lầu (S28). The page offers a farmer hour, the Ngũ Hành and Thổ Thần shrines, a stone well, and basil-seed drink (S26). The national tourism page says the same village is about 2 km northeast and names more than 20 leaf and herb crops (S7b).
- Time: A morning. No duration on S26.
- Fee: No price on S26 or S7b. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- From the base: Bicycle or a short ride. The 2.5 km is the heritage page’s distance, not a clock time (S26). Minutes: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: Morning. The village festival Cầu Bông is the 7th of the first lunar month on the national tourism page (S7b). Hours of the visit: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Mistake to avoid: Treating it as a ticketed museum. It is a working village. The national intangible-heritage recognition is for the growing, dated 4 April 2022 (S26).
- Local interest: Tra Que herbs
- Suggested slug: tra-que
- Suggested dot: 3 — the morning outside the old town, not the reason for the night
- Photo must show: herb beds and a person or a path in the same frame, in colour. Not a single leaf filling the card.
- Sources: S7b, S8, S26, S28

### Cam Thanh coconut water
- Vietnamese name: Rừng dừa Bảy Mẫu / Rừng dừa Cẩm Thanh
- Kind: sight
- Area: Võng Nhi, Cẩm Thanh. Cẩm Thanh is now inside Hoi An Đông (S8). The heritage article of 14 Nov 2022 says about 3 km from the old-town centre and about 5 minutes by car (S27). One article. No second clock. The May 2018 heritage note says this water was already a recognized local tourism site before the seven sites added that month (S35).
- What the visitor does: Ride a basket boat through the nipa water. The article says the palms were brought from the Mekong delta about 200 years ago and that the name still says seven mẫu though the water is larger now (S27).
- Time: A boat hour. The wooden boat on that article is printed as 45 minutes (S27). One source.
- Fee: The same 14 Nov 2022 article prints 30,000 VND entry and 150,000 VND for a basket boat for two adults, and says that price included the ride, a basket-boat dance, singing, and crab fishing. A wooden boat is printed at 450,000 VND for up to six people for 45 minutes (S27). No second source. Public copy says Check on arrival. Summer hours on that article: 07:30–18:30. Winter: 08:00–17:00. Which months those words mean is not on the page (S27).
- From the base: About 3 km, about 5 minutes by car, on that one article (S27). Confirm the minutes on arrival.
- When to go: The article suggests early morning or after 15:30 (S27).
- Mistake to avoid: Quoting the 2022 prices as this week’s tariff.
- Local interest: Cam Thanh boats
- Suggested slug: cam-thanh
- Suggested dot: 3 — a boat hour, not the old town
- Photo must show: a basket boat in the palms, water in front, trees behind, subject through the centre.
- Sources: S8, S27, S35

## Nightlife and evening

### Ancient town evening
- Vietnamese name: Phố cổ về đêm
- Kind: nightlife
- Area: The pedestrian streets of the ancient town, plus Phan Châu Trinh in the evening window
- What the visitor does: Walk. Shops stay open into the pedestrian hours. The January 2026 notice prints 09:00–21:30 in summer and 09:00–21:00 in winter for the old town, and 17:30 until 21:30 or 21:00 for Phan Châu Trinh (S20). This is the night people book. It is not a bar grid.
- Time: The evening inside those hours
- Fee: Walking the streets is the open-neighbourhood argument in the May 2026 article (S11). The ticket notices still ask visitors to buy before they enter the heritage area (S20). Check which rule is enforced. No drink price is recorded.
- From the base: Walk from the lanes just off the core. A ride from An Bang, minutes Check on arrival (S6).
- When to go: Dusk through the published close. The lantern night, the 14th of the lunar month, is the separate event (S33).
- Mistake to avoid: Waiting for a club street that this pass did not find. Also, do not assume the 15:00 Phan Châu Trinh start. The January 2026 notice still says 17:30 (S20).
- Scene landmarks, if any, with a primary URL: Chùa Cầu, https://www.hoianworldheritage.org.vn/vi/news/Diem-di-tich/Chua-Cau-8.hwh (S3). Traditional music at 66 Bạch Đằng, named without a closing hour (S10). Japanese culture house at 06 Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai, tea at 09:30 and 16:30, which is daytime (S20). No bar.
- Suggested slug: ancient-town-evening
- Suggested dot: do not add a separate page. This is the place at night.
- Photo must show: the timber street with lanterns or shop light, the row still readable, subject through the centre. A lantern with no street fails.
- Sources: S3, S10, S11, S20, S33

- Area: Ancient town
