# Food

Checked: 21 Oct 2026

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Cao lau
- Vietnamese name: Cao lầu
- What it is: Hoi An’s noodle, which the heritage centre’s 11 Aug 2025 article says people here call bánh, not mì. Thick rice noodles from local Xuyệt rice, soaked with wood-ash water from tràm on Cù Lao Chàm, milled with water from the Bá Lễ well, steamed, rolled, and cut. Served with a little pork broth, char siu, crispy ram, and Trà Quế herbs: húng lủi, young mustard, rau đắng, diếp cá (S28). It looks related to mì Quảng and is not that bowl (S28).
- How to eat it: Toss it while it is hot. The broth wets the noodles. It is not a soup you drink (S28).
- When to order it: Hot. The article does not lock it to one hour. Lunch is the natural bowl. A cold takeaway is the miss the same article warns against (S28).
- Area, not a shop: The old town. A 7 Jun 2023 note on the heritage site names the food hall inside Chợ Hội An and the night market on Nguyễn Hoàng, Công nữ Ngọc Hoa, Trần Quý Cáp, and Bạch Đằng (S31). Shop names and the prices on that note stay under Notes, not pages.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: mì Quảng, the Da Nang and Quảng bowl, which this one is not (S28, S30)
- Sources: S28, S30, S31

### White rose dumpling
- Vietnamese name: Bánh bao bánh vạc / bánh bông hồng trắng
- What it is: A steamed dumpling the heritage centre traces to Hoa families in the port, from a Chinese há cảo, filled so the white bánh bao looks like a rose. Eaten with bánh vạc. The English name “white rose” is the story on that page: a foreign guest in the early 1990s could not remember the Vietnamese name and said white rose (S29). Vietnam Tourism says the wrapper is rice flour and the same well water as cao lầu, filled with minced pork, shrimp, and mushrooms, with fried scallion and shallot (S30). The heritage article describes a thin wrapper, shrimp-and-pork filling, and fried shallot, dipped in sweet-sour fish sauce, eaten warm (S29). Where the two pages disagree on the filling, keep both: S29 says tôm thịt; S30 adds mushrooms.
- How to eat it: One dumpling is one bite. Dip. Eat it warm. A cold plate misses the wrapper (S29).
- When to order it: A small plate, not the whole lunch. The heritage article calls it a refined dish, historically expensive, now on local-specialty menus (S29).
- Area, not a shop: The old streets where it was carried: Lê Lợi, Trần Phú, Nguyễn Thái Học, Bạch Đằng (S29). The family workshop named on that page is under Notes, not pages.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S29, S30

### Com ga
- Vietnamese name: Cơm gà
- What it is: Chicken rice. Vietnam Tourism says the Hoi An plate is believed to come from a Hainanese dish brought by Chinese immigrants, and that here the chicken is shredded, the rice is yellow with chicken broth and turmeric, and the sides are herbs, a bowl of chicken soup, and a sour papaya salad (S30).
- How to eat it: Eat the chicken with the rice and the salad. The soup is beside the plate (S30).
- When to order it: A lunch when you do not want noodles (S30).
- Area, not a shop: Hoi An. The shops named on S30 are not published as picks. The heritage food note also names cơm gà as a local bowl without a street (S31).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: the Da Nang com ga page already separates a Hong Kong-style plate from this Hoi An plate. Do not rewrite those Da Nang shops.
- Sources: S30, S31

### Banh mi
- Vietnamese name: Bánh mì
- What it is: The baguette sold from street stalls. Vietnam Tourism says Hoi An has it the way other cities do, and names two shops (S30). Those names are not picks.
- How to eat it: In the hand, the morning it is baked. Point at the fillings if there is a choice.
- When to order it: Morning, before the old-town walk.
- Area, not a shop: Street stalls in the old town (S30). No house number is copied.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: the shared banh mi page. Add only this town’s street, not a new origin story.
- Sources: S30

### Mi Quang
- Vietnamese name: Mì Quảng
- What it is: The neighbor’s bowl. Wide turmeric rice noodles, a shallow broth, herbs, and a cracker. Vietnam Tourism says versions are eaten across Quảng Nam, which is the old province this town sat in (S30). The cao lầu article says the two bowls look related and are not the same (S28).
- How to eat it: Toss. Do not drink it like phở.
- When to order it: Lunch, if you are not riding to Da Nang for it.
- Area, not a shop: Old town. Named shops on S30 are not picks.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: `mi-quang` on the Da Nang food guide. Do not rewrite that dish’s Da Nang sentences.
- Sources: S28, S30

### Mango cake
- Vietnamese name: Bánh xoài
- What it is: A chewy sweet named for its shape, not for mango. Vietnam Tourism says glutinous rice flour, crushed peanuts, and brown sugar, and compares the texture to mochi (S30).
- How to eat it: As a sweet, in the hand.
- When to order it: After the walk.
- Area, not a shop: Street stalls near the Japanese bridge, as that page prints it (S30). No stall name.
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S30

## Food streets and markets

### Hoi An market food hall
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Hội An
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eat in the market’s food hall. The 7 Jun 2023 heritage note names it for cao lầu and does not give a street number (S31). UNESCO’s list text includes an open market inside the property and does not name the street (S1).
- When to go: Daytime. Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area: Ancient town. Street: Check on arrival.
- Suggested slug: hoi-an-market
- Photo must show: the market hall or the stalls, not a single bowl cropped so tight the place disappears
- Sources: S1, S31

### Hoi An night market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ đêm Hội An
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Walk the night stalls for the local bowls. The 7 Jun 2023 note places the night market on Nguyễn Hoàng, Công nữ Ngọc Hoa, Trần Quý Cáp, and Bạch Đằng, close to the old-town centre (S31).
- When to go: Evening. The note does not print an open or close (S31). Check on arrival.
- Area: Streets just off the ancient-town core
- Suggested slug: hoi-an-night-market
- Photo must show: the night street of stalls, subject through the centre, not a menu board
- Sources: S31

### Tan Thanh fishing-village market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ phiên làng chài Tân Thành
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: A weekend market in the fishing village. The ministry article says it is usually held on Saturday and Sunday (S6). No stall list and no prices.
- When to go: Saturday and Sunday, as that article prints it. A later week can move. Check on arrival.
- Area: Tân Thành, Cẩm An ward in June 2024. The whole of Cẩm An ward is now inside Hoi An Tây (S8).
- Suggested slug: tan-thanh-market
- Photo must show: the market lane or the boats and the stalls in one frame, not a single dish cropped tight
- Sources: S6, S8
