## What the visitor does

- The scene, in order: The hall is at 157 Nguyễn Duy Hiệu. The monument page says Chaozhou merchants built it in 1845 as their meeting place and their own worship, and that people also call it chùa Âm Bổn. The gods here are the ones who govern wind and wave, so the voyages out of the port would go smoothly.

## Fee

- Basic price, and who pays it: Check on arrival. No separate price is printed for this building. From 10 August 2026, Da Nang Resolution 98/2026 sets 20,000 VND per person per building, the same for a Vietnamese and a foreign visitor. The heritage centre’s notices of 26 January 2026 and 16 March 2026 still describe an 80,000 VND ticket for the streetscape, Chùa Cầu or Miếu Quan Công, and two other works, and a 120,000 VND ticket when a museum and three other works are added. The March notice says one purchase can be used through the stay, up to three days. A May 2026 article says the town is an open neighbourhood and many people walk in without a ticket.
