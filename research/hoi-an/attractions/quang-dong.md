## What the visitor does

- The scene, in order: The centre’s Vietnamese monument page says Cantonese merchants built the hall in 1885, first for Thiên Hậu and Confucius. After 1911 the worship turned to Quan Công and the earlier merchants, the Tiền Hiền. The English page on the same site describes a pottery dragon fountain in the court. That is the frame you recognise before you reach the hall.

- Basic price, and who pays it: Check on arrival. No separate price is printed for this building. From 10 August 2026, Da Nang Resolution 98/2026 sets 20,000 VND per person per building, the same for a Vietnamese and a foreign visitor. The heritage centre’s notices of 26 January 2026 and 16 March 2026 still describe an 80,000 VND ticket for the streetscape, Chùa Cầu or Miếu Quan Công, and two other works, and a 120,000 VND ticket when a museum and three other works are added.
