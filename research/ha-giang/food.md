# Comprehensive list of local food to try — Ha Giang

Checked: 23 Oct 2026

Specialty dishes below: 32, plus phở as the shared breakfast bowl. A specialty is a dish. Streets and restaurants are not dishes. This is every specialty the opened pages named. It is not padded to 40. Where a method disagrees, both versions stay. No stall price is printed. A shop name is not a page.

Phở on the four-day page is the ordinary breakfast bowl (S1). It is recorded because the page names it. It is not counted as a highland specialty. Phở chua, phở ngô, buckwheat pho, and phở Tráng Kìm are different named bowls.

### Five-colour sticky rice
- Vietnamese name: Xôi ngũ sắc (S13, S14). The English page says five-colour sticky rice (S3, S6).
- What it is: Sticky rice coloured with leaves, not a chemical dye (S6). The five colours are the five elements: yellow earth, green plant, red fire, white metal, black water (S3, S6). Dân Việt says each colour comes from forest leaves and that the dish is eaten across the northwest and northeast as well as in Đồng Văn (S14).
- How to eat it: As a plate of five colours (S6, S14). A serving ritual was not printed.
- When to order it: With a highland meal. No clock.
- Area, not a shop: The plateau, and Đồng Văn on S14
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site
- Sources: S3, S6, S13, S14

### Thang co
- Vietnamese name: Thắng cố (S13, S14). S6 says Thang Co.
- What it is: A Mong meat soup (S6, S13). S6: traditionally horse; for visitors the meat is now buffalo, cow, or pork; broth of bones and organs with 12 spices, including star anise, cardamom, and lemon leaves; meat lightly fried, then simmered. S13: horse, buffalo, beef, and pork, originally horse, now mostly beef, buffalo, or pork because horse is rare and expensive; heart, liver, lung, blood, intestine, meat, and bone, simmered with 12 forest herbs including thảo quả, quế, and hồi. S14: horse or beef offal, and “cooked properly, without spices, to keep the fragrance.” All three stay. Do not pick one pot.
- How to eat it: S6 says with mèn mén, grilled corn cakes, and corn wine. S13 describes eating it from the pot in cold weather. S14 says with green vegetables.
- When to order it: Cold days, feast days, and market days (S13). No clock.
- Area, not a shop: The plateau. S13’s photograph line places a thắng cố stall at chợ phiên Mèo Vạc.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site. Sa Pa’s dossier has the same name for a northwest pot. Do not merge the two write-ups.
- Sources: S6, S13, S14

### Au tau porridge
- Vietnamese name: Cháo ấu tẩu (S13, S14). S6 says Au Tau porridge.
- What it is: Porridge from the ấu tẩu tuber (S6, S13, S14). The methods disagree. S6: the Mong soak the tuber in rice water, simmer about 4–5 hours, puree, cook with bone broth and yellow-flower sticky rice mixed with a little ordinary sticky rice; eat with minced lean meat, salt, pepper, and scallions; fatty, with a little bitterness; the page says it is used for colds and aches. S13: soak the peeled tuber in rice water overnight, simmer until soft, cook with good ordinary rice and pounded sticky rice and a stewed pork hock; a large pot uses only a few tubers; bitter like tam thất, then sweet; egg, scallion, and perilla at the table; the page calls it a cold remedy that became a specialty. S14: after the toxin is removed, stew with fragrant upland sticky rice and minced meat or pork hock; slightly bitter, then a sweet finish; scallion, coriander, and pepper. All three stay. Do not pick one pot.
- How to eat it: Hot, in a bowl, with the herbs that page names (S6, S13, S14)
- When to order it: Cold days in the rocky mountains (S6). S14 says cool weather.
- Area, not a shop: The plateau, and Đồng Văn on S14
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S3, S6, S13, S14

### Lon cap nach
- Vietnamese name: Lợn cắp nách. The prepared meat is thịt lợn cắp nách (S13).

Cooked whole as quay, or as grilled, steamed, or stir-fried meat. The page says the favored pieces are lòng dồi and belly still on the rib, steamed, with salt pounded with green chili and mắc khén. Some places grill the meat in a bamboo tube. Skin crisp, little fat, lean meat that is firm.
- How to eat it: Those ways, at a stall or a market, sometimes with rượu ngô or rượu táo mèo (S13)
- When to order it: At a highland market or an eatery (S13). No clock.
- Area, not a shop: Highland markets (S13)

### Stone cake
- Vietnamese name: Bánh đá. Also bánh Lơ khoải (S14).
- What it is: A long cake of rice, corn, and sticky rice, yellow or purple, of the Dao and the Nùng (S14). It is left in the stream for months until moss grows, then scraped, and cut in strips about as wide as a finger. Some people eat it in place of noodles, some as a cool bite, some boiled until soft with palm sugar and roasted peanuts. It appears at Dao áo dài festivals and Tết, on that page.
- How to eat it: Those three ways (S14). Do not pick one.
- When to order it: Festival and Tết on S14. A 2026 civil date: Check on arrival.
- Area, not a shop: The high communes of Đồng Văn (S14)
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S14
