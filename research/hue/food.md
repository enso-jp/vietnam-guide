## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Bun bo Hue
- Vietnamese name: Bún bò Huế
- What it is: Rice noodles in a beef-and-pork broth. Vietnam Tourism says the bowl has beef brisket and also pork hock, trotter, congealed blood, and loin, seasoned with garlic, shallots, chili, shrimp paste, lemongrass, herbs, and lime (S18). The city’s culture department, quoting a 2014 CNN segment, describes pork and beef bone, beef, pork loaf, hock, banana flower, and herbs at Dong Ba market (S21). Heavier and hotter than pho is the comparison the national page draws (S18).
- How to eat it: Herbs and lime come with the bowl. The national page does not give a spoon gesture. Do not invent one. The 2014 culture note says the Dong Ba bowl is the one that visitor filmed (S21).
- When to order it: The city’s bowl, any meal. A clock hour is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Nothing on these pages says it is only breakfast.
- Area, not a shop: Dong Ba market (S21). A house number for the market is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The city portal’s “16 Le Loi” line on a thin Dong Ba page is the portal’s own contact block, not a market door this pass could confirm.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none. It is Hue’s bowl. Do not send the reader to Da Nang for it.
- Sources: S18, S21

### Com hen
- Vietnamese name: Cơm hến
- What it is: Cold rice with tiny clams from the Perfume River, herbs, bean sprouts, shrimp paste, roasted peanuts, and pork rind. Vietnam Tourism calls it a peasant dish built on leftover rice (S18). The culture department notes the same dish in the 2014 film, without a recipe (S21).
- How to eat it: Mix the rice with the clam, herbs, and the shrimp paste. The national page does not name a tool.
- When to order it: A lunch plate. A clock hour is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Hen Island (Cồn Hến). Cross Ưng Bình bridge. The island is small, under half an hour to walk, and Vietnam Tourism says it disappears when the city floods (S20). Stalls there sell cơm hến and bún hến. Chi Lăng and Bạch Đằng, on the far side, are the afternoon streets the same itinerary names (S20). The 2026 tourism plan also names Chi Lăng – Bạch Đằng as a night-anchor target, not as a finished food street (S4).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S4, S18, S20, S21

### Banh beo
- Vietnamese name: Bánh bèo
- What it is: Small steamed rice cakes in little saucers, topped with dried shrimp, pork crackling, shallots, and scallion oil (S19). Vietnam Tourism’s Hue page lists it with bánh khoái as the snack after Dong Ba (S17).
- How to eat it: A few drops of slightly sweetened fish sauce, then the cake from the dish (S19).
- When to order it: A snack, not a dinner. Afternoon is the hour the national page implies by calling it a snack. A clock is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED as a street. The national “how to eat” page names one shop. That name is under Notes, not pages (S19).
- Meal: sweet is wrong. It is a savory snack. Meal: main, as a snack plate.
- Neighbor dish to link: banh beo is already shared with Da Nang. The Hue cake is the saucer described here. Do not copy Da Nang’s shop onto this line.
- Sources: S17, S19

### Banh nam
- Vietnamese name: Bánh nậm
- What it is: A flat steamed rice dumpling in a banana leaf, filled with minced pork and shrimp. Vietnam Tourism ties it to the court kitchen (S18). The “how to eat” page describes a thin rice layer, fresh shrimp, and shrimp fried with garlic and scallion, folded in the leaf (S19).
- How to eat it: Dip in fish sauce (S18, S19).
- When to order it: With the other small cakes, daytime. A clock is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The shop name is a note.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: banh beo
- Sources: S18, S19

### Banh khoai
- Vietnamese name: Bánh khoái
- What it is: A turmeric rice-flour crepe with shrimp, quail egg, bean sprouts, and pork belly, folded, with a salad of fig, starfruit, and cucumber, and a thick peanut-and-pork-liver sauce (S19).
- How to eat it: Folded, with the salad and the sauce (S19).
- When to order it: A snack or a light meal. Clock: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Kim Long is where the national page places one shop (S19). The shop is a note.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19

### Bun thit nuong
- Vietnamese name: Bún thịt nướng
- What it is: Cold rice noodles with grilled pork, herbs, green papaya, carrot, cucumber, and lettuce. Vietnam Tourism says the peanut sauce is the Hue version and that the royal kitchen is the claimed origin (S18, S19).
- How to eat it: Pork on the noodles, sweet-sour fish sauce, toss (S19).
- When to order it: Lunch (S19).
- Area, not a shop: Kim Long. The shop name is a note (S19).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S18, S19

- How to eat it: Wrap in rice paper with herbs, pineapple, bean sprouts, green banana, and chili, then a creamy sauce (S18).
- When to order it: Street snack. Clock: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: “Vendors on the street” is all the national page gives (S18). A street name is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.

## Food streets and markets

### Dong Ba Market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Đông Ba
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Starts here for produce, then the snacks. Vietnam Tourism sends the reader to Dong Ba before bánh khoái and bánh bèo (S17). The culture department places a bún bò Huế bowl inside the market (S21).
- When to go: Daytime. A door hour is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area: Check on arrival as a street number — UNSOURCED. It is the city’s market, not a south-bank hotel restaurant.
- Suggested slug: dong-ba
- Photo must show: the market aisle and a bowl or a stall, not the citadel gate
- Sources: S17, S21

### Hen Island
- Vietnamese name: Cồn Hến
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Crosses Ưng Bình bridge and eats cơm hến or bún hến from the stalls. The walk is under half an hour. The island floods out of sight in high water (S20).
- When to go: Daytime, and not in a flood week (S5, S20).
- Area: The island in the Perfume River, then Chi Lăng and Bạch Đằng if the afternoon continues (S20)
- Suggested slug: con-hen
- Photo must show: the island lane or a clam-rice plate, not a generic river panorama
- Sources: S5, S20

### Kim Long snack street
- Vietnamese name: Kim Long
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats bánh khoái or bún thịt nướng along the north-bank road that also leads to Thien Mu. The national page names shops. Those names are notes (S19).
- When to go: On the pagoda ride, daytime
- Area: Kim Long ward (S28)
- Suggested slug: kim-long-food
- Photo must show: the street of shops with the river or the tower, not a menu board
- Sources: S19, S28
