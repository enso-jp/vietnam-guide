# getting there

Checked: 21 Oct 2026

- Nearest airport: Phu Bai International Airport, published in the Vietnam AIP as HUE/PHU BAI INTL, ICAO VVPB. The chart places it southeast of Hue city center, 13 km, at the intersection of runway 09/27 and taxiway E1. The operator block names Airports Corporation of Vietnam at Phu Bai. The aerodrome, customs, and the briefing office are H24. Passenger transport listed on the chart is bus, taxi, and hire car (S7). The chart copy opened here is the 28 Feb 2024 AIP folder. A later amendment was not opened. Vietnam Airlines’ airport guide uses the same 13 km southeast and prints the IATA code HUI, with passenger flights at terminal T2 since 28 Apr 2023. It lists domestic legs to Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Phu Quoc, Hai Phong, and Da Lat, plus international flying from Beijing, Osaka, Bangkok, Taipei, and Bali (S8). A direct reopen of that guide URL failed later the same day, so treat the airline list as check-before-you-fly. The ride in minutes from the airport to the south bank is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Flight block times are Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Nearest rail station: Ga Huế, on the north–south line. Vietnam Railways’ board puts Huế at km 688 from Hanoi, and Đà Nẵng at km 791, so the published gap is 103 km (S9). The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, reporting Decision 339/QĐ-SDL of 9 Oct 2025, gives the station as số 1, Bùi Thị Xuân, phường Thuận Hóa, and says the French-built station was finished in 1906 on the Đông Hà–Đà Nẵng line of 171 km (S6). Use số 1 from that decision. A ticket-site door number was not used.
- From the usual previous stop: Hanoi, by the overnight SE train. Da Nang is the usual next stop, by a short SE train or by the Hai Van road. Hoi An is beyond Da Nang. This pass did not open a coach-station page, so the bus is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.

- Getting around on site: Walk Phu Hoi and cross Truong Tien Bridge. While the three-street walking area is on, vehicles are banned there (S2). The citadel is the other bank (S1). The emperors’ tombs are further upstream along the Perfume River (S1), so they are a ride from the south-bank base. That ride’s minutes are Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. From the airport, the AIP lists bus, taxi, and hire car, and no fare (S7).
- Season limit on the journey: UNESCO’s management note says recurrent floods are a risk to the monuments, and that dams were being built upstream on the Perfume River to reduce them (S1). It does not give a month. Vietnam Airlines tells riders to take Hai Van in February–May, when the page calls the weather drier, and to avoid October–December for heavy rain, thick fog, and a slippery road. It also flags rain from September through December (S10). Do not send someone over the pass in a flood week without checking that morning. No hotel rate and no hire-bike rate from that page belong in this dossier.
