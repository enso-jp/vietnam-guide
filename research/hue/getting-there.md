- Getting around on site: Walk the south bank. Cross to the north-bank imperial city; Phu Xuan Bridge is the link the culture department names (S2). The river path from Truong Tien Bridge to Nghinh Luong Dinh is a walk. The parks center has asked the ward police to stop electric bikes using it (S3). UNESCO puts the emperors’ tombs upstream along the Perfume River, outside the Capital City (S1). A bicycle or taxi minute to a tomb is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The 2026 tourism plan includes drafting a resolution on free buses for visitors, and it talks about electric boats on the Perfume River and the Ngu Ha (S4). Both are plans. This pass does not record a free bus, a fare, or a timetable.
- Season limit on the journey: The city’s disaster plan, Decision 3279/QĐ-UBND of 6 Oct 2026, splits the year in two. Hot season May–September, 35°C to 40°C, under the southwest foehn. Cool season October–March, 20°C to 22°C on the plain, under the northeast monsoon (S5). Rain averages above 2,700 mm a year, over 4,000 mm at Bach Ma and Thua Luu, and is concentrated from September to February. November holds about 70 percent of the year’s rain. That concentration is what the plan ties to extreme floods and erosion (S5). On the Perfume River the early flood is May–June and the main flood is September–December, especially October and November. The plain downstream floods in the rainy season (S5). The coast takes a direct or indirect hit from about 3–5 storms or tropical depressions a year (S5). UNESCO already flags recurrent floods at the monuments (S1). Do not send someone over Hai Van, or count on the river path, in a flood week without checking that morning. A written rule for when the pass or the railway closes was not on these pages.
