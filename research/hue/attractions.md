## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: Morning on the Perfume River. UNESCO places the old capital in that river setting: the Perfume River runs through the Capital City, the Imperial City, the Forbidden Purple City, and the Inner City, with Ngu Binh (the Royal Screen) in front of the monuments (S1). The city’s culture department, writing up the 2026 bicycle stage, puts the modern city on the south bank and the old imperial city on the north bank, joined at Phu Xuan Bridge (S2). The flag tower, Kỳ Đài, is the pin on that north bank. The same page says it was built in 1807 under Gia Long, three stacked tiers for heaven, earth, and man, and calls it the city’s zero marker (S2).
- Where they sleep, as an area: A room on the south bank, the modern side, close enough to walk to Phu Xuan Bridge or to the river path. No official page names a hotel street or a brand. Which block faces the water is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED as a lodging map.
- Where the evening is: The walking path along the Perfume River from Truong Tien Bridge to Nghinh Luong Dinh. A complaint at 20:30 on 14 Mar 2026 describes that path, and the city’s parks center, replying on 19 Mar 2026, treats it as a walk on both banks and says it has no power to fine the electric bikes using it (S3). The 2026 tourism plan, 66/KH-UBND of 3 Feb 2026, tells the wards to keep completing three night streets: the Imperial City (Hoàng Thành), the street it calls phố “Tây”, and Hai Bà Trưng (S4). The plan still gives no closing hour. Field reports on the city desk do name the streets people call phố Tây: a 2019 complaint says “khu phố tây (28 Đường Phạm Ngũ Lão)”, and the urban-management reply of 7 Jan 2022 treats Chu Văn An, Phạm Ngũ Lão, and Võ Thị Sáu as the three walking-street roads (S24). A 14 Feb 2024 ward reply, answering a “Phố Tây” complaint, places the photo on Võ Thị Sáu and says the walking street is not organized on lunar-new-year days (S24). Hai Bà Trưng is still only the name in the plan. No door number. The same plan’s product line is to shape one or two night anchors at Hoàng Thành – Chi Lăng – Bạch Đằng – Bao Vinh, tied to the Perfume River, the Ngu Ha, and An Cuu (S4). That line is a target, not a finished street list.
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): Kỳ Đài, the flag tower the culture department calls the city’s zero marker, north bank, built 1807 (S2). Nghinh Lương Đình, on the river bank: the old royal landing, where the king stopped in summer and where the royal boats tied up (S2). The river walk runs from Truong Tien Bridge to that pavilion (S3). Ca Huế on the Perfume River is named in the 2026 plan as a product to keep improving through 2026–2030, with a chamber theatre still to be built on Bui Thi Xuan (S4). The plan gives no boat, no wharf, and no hour. Check on arrival. These are pins. They are not a ranked list, and this pass records no drink price and no restaurant.
- Hero must include: the river, the north-bank wall or the flag tower, and the south-bank front in one frame, subject through the center. Ngu Binh belongs in the distance if the frame can hold it (S1). Not a military photograph, not a document, not a field of empty water.
- A crop that would fail: a gate cut through the roof, a panorama whose center is only brown water, a fog bank with no wall, a statue cropped through the chest, the same bridge with no riverbank.

## Attractions (Sight, area of interests, zone, hub, etc.)

The visit that matches the ticket is the Imperial City plus the tombs of Minh Mang, Tu Duc, and Khai Dinh. Resolution 55/2025/NQ-HĐND, in force 4 Jan 2026, prices those four as one ticket at 530,000 VND for an adult and 100,000 VND for a child from 7 to 12, usable for up to two days from the first visit (S11). The same adult price applies to an international visitor and a Vietnamese visitor. A child under 7 is free. Hue residents and the other half-price groups in the resolution pay half. The centre’s old 2023 price page is replaced by this resolution (S11, Article 4).

Hours for the centre’s visiting points are on its notice of 12 Mar 2024 (S12). Open 16 Mar–15 Oct from 06:30 to 18:00, and 16 Oct–15 Mar from 07:00 to 17:30. Ticket sales stop at 17:30 in the first season and at 17:00 in the second. A direct reload of that page timed out on 21 Oct 2026, so the public line says confirm on arrival. The notice does not name Thien Mu. No weekly closing day is on it.

On-site minutes and the ride from the south bank are Check on arrival — UNSOURCED, except where a line below has a source. No coordinates are recorded. Do not geocode them.

### Hue Citadel (Imperial City)
- Vietnamese name: Đại Nội / Hoàng thành Huế
- Kind: sight
- Area: North bank, inside the citadel walls
- What the visitor does: Enters at the ticketed Imperial City. The centre describes Kinh Thành as the square outer city, about 10 km around, walls about 6.6 m high, with the Imperial City (Hoàng Thành, also called Đại Nội) inside it, each side about 600 m. Ngọ Môn was the king’s gate. Inside are the courts from Ngọ Môn to the Thái Hòa palace, the ancestral temples, and the Forbidden Purple City (Tử Cấm Thành) behind Thái Hòa, built in 1804 for the king and his household, with the Duyệt Thị Đường theatre (S13). UNESCO’s frame is the same set: Capital City, Imperial City, Forbidden Purple City, Inner City, Perfume River, Ngu Binh (S1).
- Time: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The centre’s own description is a walled city of more than 100 buildings, not a gate photo.
- Fee: Adult 200,000 VND. Child 7–12: 40,000 VND. Under 7 free. Same adult price for international and Vietnamese visitors (S11). This ticket is the Imperial City only. The tombs, the royal antiquities museum, An Dinh, Hon Chen, and Nam Giao are separate lines on the same resolution.
- From the base: Walk from the south bank. Phu Xuan Bridge is the link the culture department names (S2). Truong Tien Bridge is the other river crossing at the start of the evening path (S3). Minutes: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: Morning, at opening. Summer opening on the 12 Mar 2024 notice is 06:30; winter opening is 07:00 (S12). Confirm that board.
- Mistake to avoid: Buying only the Imperial City ticket and expecting Minh Mang, Tu Duc, and Khai Dinh to be on it. They are a different line, or the four-site ticket (S11).
- Local interest: Imperial city
- Suggested slug: citadel
- Suggested dot: 5 already on the record. Do not change it.
- Photo must show: Ngọ Môn from the bridge in front, gate and roofs whole, in color. A second frame should be a different court inside, not the same gate.
- Sources: S1, S11, S12, S13

### Tomb of Tu Duc
- Vietnamese name: Lăng Tự Đức (Khiêm Lăng)
- Kind: sight
- Area: Upstream. The centre’s ticket page places it in a narrow valley at what is now thôn Thượng Ba, xã Thủy Xuân (S14). The centre’s heritage page says phường Thủy Xuân (S29). Write both ward names. Do not pick one.
- What the visitor does: Walks a garden tomb, not a palace axis. The centre describes two parallel axes, Khiêm Cung where the king rested, and the tomb itself, with almost 50 buildings whose names use the word Khiêm, and Lưu Khiêm lake in front (S14). It was started as Vạn Niên Cơ and renamed Khiêm Cung after the Chày Vôi revolt (S14).
- Time: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Fee: Adult 150,000 VND. Child 7–12: 30,000 VND. Under 7 free (S11). The four-site ticket that includes this tomb, the Imperial City, Minh Mang, and Khai Dinh is 530,000 / 100,000 and lasts up to two days (S11).
- From the base: South-bank start, then upstream. UNESCO puts the emperors’ tombs upstream along the Perfume River (S1). Bicycle or taxi. Minutes: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The old public line “south of the citadel” is not how these pages place it.
- When to go: The same day as Thien Mu if the ride is already upstream. Not in the half hour before the winter ticket desk closes (S12).
- Mistake to avoid: Photographing the incense stalls on the approach and leaving. Those stalls are not the tomb.
- Local interest: Royal tombs
- Suggested slug: khiem
- Suggested dot: 4 already on the record. Do not change it.
- Photo must show: the tomb garden, the pavilion, or the lake, with the building recognizable. Not a stall of incense, not a bus.
- Sources: S1, S11, S12, S14, S29
