Accessed 21 Oct 2026. The UNESCO MAB URL was requested and returned a bot challenge, so it is not a source. Species counts and the designation day are taken only from the pages below.

### S1
- URL: https://en.nbca.gov.vn/khu-bao-ve-canh-quan-cu-lao-cham-quang-nam-2/
- Publisher: Nature and Biodiversity Conservation Agency, national conservation site. The page credits the Department of Natural Resources and Environment. No article date is printed.
- Accessed: 21 Oct 2026
- Supports: Tân Hiệp, 18 km east of Hội An, about 15 km², eight islands, Hòn Lao the largest and the only inhabited island. Coordinates of the marine area 15°52'30" to 16°00'00" N and 108°24'30" to 108°34'30" E. UNESCO date printed as 29 May 2009, Jeju, 21st MAB session. 947 aquatic species. Swiftlet nests as a livelihood. The page still says Quảng Nam.

### S2

- Accessed: 21 Oct 2026

Hòn Lao about 1,147 hectares and the inhabited island. Forest plant counts are on the page and are not repeated as visitor facts.

### S7

### S12
- URL: https://hoianheritage.net/vi/trao-doi-chuyen-nganh/chuyen-de-nghien-cuu-trao-doi/thoi-quen-am-thuc-cua-cu-dan-cu-lao-cham-1310.html
- Publisher: Hội An World Heritage centre, Thái Thị Liễu Chi. The page stamp is 16 Sep 2025. An earlier dossier line said 15 Sep 2025.
- Accessed: 23 Oct 2026
- Supports: Page stamp 16 Sep 2025. An earlier pass wrote 15 Sep. Meal-time shift to lunch 11:00–12:00 and evening meal 17:00–18:00. Older pattern: no breakfast, first meal about 10:00, evening 16:00–17:00, night fishing to about 23:00. Breakfast stalls on the Bãi Làng coast road and by Giếng Xóm Cấm. Daily plates: canh rau, rau luộc, canh cá, cá kho phớt, cá kho mặn, cá chiên, cá cháy, cá khô nướng, cá khô kho rim. Catch named first: fish, shrimp, squid, swimming crab, then greens, then meat. Dried seafood called fragrant. Chả cá from small fish, fried, in soup, or with tomato. Bánh canh chả cá and bánh xèo chả cá. Afternoon list: bánh xèo chả cá, bánh canh, bánh bèo, mì Quảng. Occasional meat with no method: thịt heo kho, luộc, canh xương heo, sườn rim mặn, thịt xào củ quả, and bún and mì with no recipe. Mắm households named and not published. Mắm cái among foods now rare. Lobster, ốc vú nàng, abalone, and stone crab off the daily table. Stone crab described as a visitor forest catch at a high price with no figure.
