# Getting there — Cham Islands

Slug: `cham-islands`
Checked: 21 Oct 2026

The islands are xã Tân Hiệp, an island commune of Đà Nẵng. The Government portal of 4 Aug 2025 says Tam Hải and Tân Hiệp were the two commune-level units of the new Đà Nẵng that were not rearranged (S10). The city temple page of 1 May 2026 and the visit-fee notices of 2026 still call it xã Tân Hiệp, thành phố Đà Nẵng (S4, S8).

- Nearest airport: Da Nang International. The civil-aviation AIP names it VVDN — DA NANG/Da Nang International, southwest of Đà Nẵng, 3.2 km from the city centre. Administration is the Civil Aviation Authority of Viet Nam and Airports Corporation of Viet Nam. Aerodrome administration, customs, and immigration are listed H24 (S16). The cells after the 3.2 km figure run together on the HTML page and are not used as a second distance. The IATA code was not printed on that AIP page. Road minutes from the airport to the Cửa Đại pier: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Nearest rail station: There is no station on the island. The mainland rail stop for this crossing was not named on the pages read. Treat Ga Đà Nẵng as the usual city station only as a label travelers already use, and mark the station address and the minutes to the pier Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. No Vietnam Railways timetable was opened.
- Usual previous stop: Hội An, then the boat. The pier named by the Border Guard on 30 Aug 2023 is bến thủy nội địa Cửa Đại – Cù Lao Chàm, with the Cửa Đại border checkpoint and the inland-waterway berth team of the then Quảng Nam transport inspectorate (S11).
- How far the water is: the city temple page says Hải Tạng is about 20 km from the Hội An mainland shore (S4). The biodiversity agency says the commune is 18 km from Hội An city toward the East Sea (S1). Both figures stay. Do not average them. Neither page prints a crossing time.

Getting around on site:
- Land at Bãi Làng. Hải Tạng, Đình Tiền Hiền, and Lăng Ông Ngư are in that hamlet (S5). Giếng Xóm Cấm and Miếu Âm Linh are in Bãi Ông. Miếu Tổ nghề yến is in Bãi Hương, beside khe Cây Cừa (S5).
- Tourism boats, under the 17 Jul 2020 marine-reserve regulation, must pick up and drop passengers at the designated place and must not operate or anchor on coral or seagrass (S7). Whether that sentence is still the text in force after the 2025 nature-reserve decision and the 2026 biosphere regulation was not checked against the new regulation’s own PDF. The 30 Jun 2026 city article describes the newer biosphere rules and does not reprint the coordinates (S3).
- The visit fee is not the boat fare. See attractions for the 70,000 VND.

Season limit on the journey:
- The 2023 Border Guard report ties the crowd to summer, holidays, and Tết (S11). A month when the pier closes was not on that page. Do not copy a commercial “no boats from October to December” line. Weather stop: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- The city leadership note of 9 Jun 2026, reported on 30 Jun 2026, says the island still needs the pier, clean water, waste, fuel, health, and school works brought up to a standard, and asks for a longer stay rather than a higher headcount (S3). That is a works list, not a closure calendar.
