## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Seafood with rice
- Vietnamese name: Cá, tôm, mực, ghẹ
- What it is: The heritage centre’s 15 Sep 2025 study says the daily plate is still the catch: fish, shrimp, squid, and swimming crab, then greens, then meat. The simple island versions it names are fish soup, fish braised lightly, fish braised salty, fried fish, grilled dried fish, and dried fish simmered again (S12).
- How to eat it: With rice, at the hamlet meal. Little spice, the study says (S12).
- When to order it: Lunch about 11:00–12:00, evening meal about 17:00–18:00, on that 2025 study (S12).
- Area, not a shop: Bãi Làng, and the other hamlets. Not a restaurant name.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this plate. Mì Quảng is the afternoon bowl below.
- Sources: S12

### Chả cá
- Vietnamese name: Chả cá
- What it is: The island’s everyday fish paste. The study says the women keep the large fish to sell and pound the small fish. It is fried, put in soup, or cooked with tomato, and it is also the filling of bánh canh chả cá and bánh xèo chả cá. Some households now sell the paste to visitors (S12).
- How to eat it: In the rice meal, or in the noodle soup and the pancake when the cook is changing the meal (S12).
- When to order it: Lunch or the evening meal. The study also lists bánh xèo chả cá, bánh canh, bánh bèo, and mì Quảng as afternoon food for visitors and islanders (S12).
- Area, not a shop: Bãi Làng coast road and the stalls by Giếng Xóm Cấm (S12)
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: `mi-quang` for the afternoon bowl the study names. Cao lầu is a Hội An dish. This pass did not re-open the cao lầu article, so the ash-water line is not repeated here.
- Sources: S12

### Mực một nắng
- Vietnamese name: Mực một nắng
- What it is: Tube squid (mực ống), split, cleaned of the gut and the ink sac, rinsed in seawater, pinned at the fins, and dried one sun on a grid so the outside dries and the inside stays raw. The heritage centre’s 7 Jul 2014 note says other squid (mực lá, mực cơm, mực ghim) are not the ones they dry, and that the smallest grade is eaten fresh rather than dried. Main seasons then: vụ Nam, June to September; vụ Bắc, December to April. Winter drying used squid bought off Quảng Ngãi boats and was not as good (S13). The 2014 headcount of dryers is not treated as a current census.
- How to eat it: Grilled or as the gift the 2014 page says visitors eat on the island and carry home (S13). The 2025 study still lists dried squid among the island’s dried seafood (S12).
- When to order it: The sunny months, if the 2014 seasons still hold. Confirm on the boat day.
- Area, not a shop: Bãi Làng. The dryer named in 2014 is under Notes, not a pick.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S13, S12

### Rau rừng and mắm
- Vietnamese name: Rau rừng, mắm
- What it is: Forest greens, boiled or in soup, and fish sauce the islanders like. The 2025 study says many households have stopped keeping a jar through the storm season, and that only a few still make mắm to sell to visitors (S12). The same page says lobster, ốc vú sao / vú nàng, abalone, and stone crab have left the daily table. Stone crab became a forest catch for visitors, and the price is described as high, with no figure printed in the portion read (S12). Do not list those four as the everyday order.
- How to eat it: Greens with the seafood rice. Mắm on the side, if a household is still selling it.
- When to order it: With the midday or evening rice (S12).
- Area, not a shop: The hamlets. Household names stay in Notes.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S12

### Nước lá Lao
- Vietnamese name: Nước lá Lao
- What it is: A leaf drink the 7 Jul 2014 heritage note says is particular to Cù Lao Chàm (S13). It does not print a recipe or a price.
- How to eat it: As a drink, on the island.
- When to order it: With the day. No hour was printed.
- Area, not a shop: The island. No stall named.
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S13

### Bánh ít and bánh su sê
- Vietnamese name: Bánh ít, bánh su sê
- What it is: The sweets the same 2014 note lists as the island’s cakes, beside the squid (S13). No method and no price on that page.
- How to eat it: As a sweet, on the island.
- When to order it: Check on arrival — the page does not give an hour.
- Area, not a shop: Tân Hiệp. No shop named.
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S13

### Yến sào
- Vietnamese name: Yến sào Cù Lao Chàm – Hội An
- What it is: The nest of Aerodramus fuciphagus, worked into a steamed dish, not a street bowl. The city portal, 28 May 2026, says the geographic indication was registered on 23 Nov 2020, and that the grades yến quang, yến thiên, and yến bài were then quoted from nearly 100 million to nearly 180 million VND a kilogram (S14). That is a product range, not a snack price. Do not turn it into a bowl fee.
- How to eat it: Steamed, if someone is buying the product. The visitor sight is the temple in Bãi Hương, not the cliff (S5, S14).
- When to order it: Not a meal hour. The harvest windows are in events.md.
- Area, not a shop: The trade of Tân Hiệp. No shop is a pick.
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S14, S5

## Food streets and markets

### Bãi Làng coast road
- Vietnamese name: Đường ven biển Bãi Làng
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats at the breakfast stalls the 15 Sep 2025 study places along this road, and at the stalls by Giếng Xóm Cấm. Afternoon choices named there: bánh xèo chả cá, bánh canh, bánh bèo, mì Quảng (S12). A market hall with the name Chợ Tân Hiệp appears on tour pages and was not on the heritage or city pages read. It is not a second entry.
- When to go: Morning for the breakfast stalls. Lunch 11:00–12:00 and the evening meal 17:00–18:00 on that study (S12).
- Area: Thôn Bãi Làng, and the well in Bãi Ông as the other pin (S5, S12)
- Suggested slug: bai-lang-breakfast
- Photo must show: the coast road, the sea, and the bowls in one frame. Not a menu board cropped tight.
- Sources: S12, S5
