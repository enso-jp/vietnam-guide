# Research dossier — Cao Bang

Slug: `cao-bang`
Province: Cao Bang (Cao Bằng). National Assembly Resolution 202/2025/QH15 left Cao Bang unmerged. It is one of 11 provinces and cities that were not rearranged. New merged provinces elsewhere started work on 1 Jul 2025 (S10). The government briefing gives the province 6,700.4 km², a long border with China, and about 95 percent of the population in ethnic minority communities (S11). Older pages still say districts (Trung Khanh, Ha Quang). Geopark pages from 2025 say communes (Dam Thuy, Tra Linh) and Thuc Phan ward (S3, S20).
Kind: area
Corridor: northern-midlands
Base a traveler sleeps in: Cao Bang city, on the Bang River, for the arrival night and for Pac Bo or Phia Oac days. Homestays and guesthouses in Dam Thuy, on the Quay Son, for a morning at the falls. Never a hotel brand.
Checked: 21 Oct 2026

## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: Ban Gioc, a wide cascade on the Quay Son at the China border, with limestone around the river. Not the bus yard and not a city square (S1, S3, S7, S17).
- Where they sleep, as an area: City hotels and guesthouses by the Bang River for the hub night (S7). Dam Thuy homestays and guesthouses when the falls are the morning (S3, S22). No hotel brand as a pick.
- Where the evening is: Kim Dong walking street and the Bang riverside in the city, described as a weekend space with food counters and a water-wheel stage (S16). Dam Thuy after dark is the homestay and the river, not a sourced bar row (S3, S22).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): Ban Gioc from the river (S3); Truc Lam Ban Gioc pagoda on Phia Nhan, looking back at the falls and the fields (S3); the Kim Dong walking street and its water-wheel stage (S16).
- Hero must include: the cascade, the river at its foot, and the limestone in one color frame, subject through the center. If the frame is the pagoda viewpoint, keep the temple roof and the falls together.
- A crop that would fail: spray with no cliff and no river; a slice of one thread that loses the width; empty mist; a portrait crop that cuts the pagoda roof or the far limestone.
