# Food — Cao Bang

Checked: 21 Oct 2026

Highland Tay, Nung, Hmong, and Dao cooking, plus a city bowl that is not Hanoi pho. No stall prices.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Pho chua
- Vietnamese name: Phở chua
- What it is: A cold sour pho. Vietnam Tourism names it as a city specialty beside banh cuon (S7, reloaded 23 Oct 2026). Báo Cao Bằng on 21 May 2026 (S24) describes vinegar thickened with canna starch, sugar, salt, garlic, and chili; fried sweet-potato threads, crushed peanut, cucumber, herbs, roast duck, crisp pork belly, and smoked sausage, plus a little duck broth. The 1 Sep 2026 article (S26) does not mention the duck broth. The 3 May 2026 article (S27) says morning service in Thục Phán is limited and does not print a clock. The old tourism URL was not the page that reloaded.
- How to eat it: As its own bowl. S24 prints the assembly order and calls the sauce mildly sour and lightly sweet. Do not invent a legend
- When to order it: Breakfast or midday in town. S27 says morning service in Thục Phán is limited. Exact stall hours — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: Cao Bang city (S7)
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: `pho` — the Hanoi beef bowl is a different dish
- Sources: S7, S24, S26, S27

### Banh cuon
- Vietnamese name: Bánh cuốn
- What it is: Steamed rice rolls. Vietnam Tourism names them with pho chua (S7). Báo Cao Bằng on 4 Jul 2012 (S25): local ordinary rice, pork minced and fried with dried shallot, eaten hot in pork-bone broth with gio, cha, and egg. Fish sauce, mắc mật chili, and lime are optional on that article. The 1 Sep 2026 article (S26) says it is not eaten with the familiar fish sauce. Both stay. Egg roll is the same article’s version, not a second dish. Ministry 22 Aug 2024 (S28) says it was chosen for a top 100 dish list.
- How to eat it: Hot, in the bone broth (S25, S26)
- When to order it: Breakfast. S25 says stalls open from 4–5 in the morning. A closing time — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: Cao Bang city
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none that is this city’s roll. Do not reuse a central-coast rice-roll slug
- Sources: S7, S25, S26, S28

### Trung Khanh chestnut
- Vietnamese name: Hạt dẻ Trùng Khánh
- What it is: A round chestnut, dark glossy shell, yellow kernel. Bao Cao Bang says the roasted nut is béo, sweet, and aromatic, and that the same nut goes into ham hock and into chestnut wine (S9)
- How to eat it: Roasted or boiled, or mixed into com. Score the tip before roasting so the shell splits — that step is on an indexed tourism note this pass could not reload, so treat the knife nick as Check on arrival
- When to order it: Autumn, on the eastern route through Trung Khanh (S9). Month numbers — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED on the page that loaded
- Area, not a shop: Trung Khanh / the eastern route (S9)
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S9

### Chestnut green rice
- Vietnamese name: Cốm hạt dẻ
- What it is: Young Pi Pat glutinous rice, boiled, dried, and pounded to a translucent green, then rubbed with pounded chestnut (S9)
- How to eat it: By the handful, as a seasonal sweet
- When to order it: Autumn, when the rice is still milky and the chestnut is in (S9)
- Area, not a shop: Eastern route, Trung Khanh (S9)
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S9

### Five-colour sticky rice
- Vietnamese name: Xôi ngũ sắc
- What it is: Pi Pat or Ong glutinous rice colored with plants, five colors, also served as xoi and banh day (S9). The same rice appears on Long Tong offering trays (S13)
- How to eat it: With the fingers or a spoon, at a market or a festival tray
- When to order it: Festivals and ordinary highland meals — Check on arrival for a daily stall
- Area, not a shop: Tay and Nung villages on the routes in S9
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S9, S13

### Roast duck with mac mat
- Vietnamese name: Vịt quay tẩm lá mác mật
- What it is: Roast duck, and a related dew-dried grass duck stewed with mac mat leaf. The geopark names roast duck and smoked sausage as flavors of the eastern route (S3, S9)
- How to eat it: Shared, with rice or sticky rice. Leaf aroma — do not invent a tasting note beyond the leaf being the point of the name
- When to order it: Lunch on the eastern route (S3, S9)
- Area, not a shop: Eastern route and the city
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S3, S9

### Corn wine
- Vietnamese name: Rượu ngô
- What it is: Corn distilled with a yeast cake of more than 20 forest leaves, rested about a month, described by Bao Cao Bang as the most famous drink of the northern kitchen (S9)
- How to eat it: A small cup with a meal. The paper calls a modest pour a tonic. Do not publish a drinking dare
- When to order it: Evening at a village meal
- Area, not a shop: Northern route (S9)
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S9

- How to eat it: As a side with meat

### Dong glass noodles
- Vietnamese name: Miến dong

- How to eat it: As the noodle of a western-route lunch
- When to order it: Autumn in the article’s frame (S9)
- Area, not a shop: Nguyen Binh and the western route
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S9
