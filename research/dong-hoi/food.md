## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Chao canh
- Vietnamese name: Cháo canh
- What it is: The everyday Quảng Bình bowl. A 15 Mar 2023 visitor article says it is also called bánh canh, and that a bowl has rice or wheat noodles, lean pork, snakehead (cá lóc), and ram (fried spring roll), in a broth simmered from marrow bones (S16). The same article says some shops add pork offal or crab and still call it cháo canh (S16). The provincial portal’s bánh canh card describes a different broth emphasis: pork rib and fresh shrimp, with rice-flour noodles cut from dough that has hung overnight, and fish as an option. It names cá quả, a firm green-black fish, and says the broth is pork bone plus shrimp and fish so it stays light (S17). Print both. Do not collapse them into one recipe.
- How to eat it: A bowl, with the ram. The portal says people keep a regular shop rather than one ranked address (S17). Greens and chili are on the portal’s seasoning note, not as a rule for every shop (S17).
- When to order it: A full bowl, morning or midday. The market page says stalls are busy early and in the afternoon (S4). The portal card prints 07:00–18:00 and the email `email@gmail.com`, so that clock looks like a CMS default. Hours: Check on arrival.
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Đồng Hới on Mẹ Suốt, and the shop clusters on Lê Thành Đồng, Đồng Hải, Nguyễn Trãi, and Lý Thường Kiệt. Shop names are under Notes, not pages.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: `banh-canh` is the Da Nang snakehead bowl and must not be reused as this page. Lệ Thủy’s “cháo bánh canh” is a third bowl, named on a 2022 page this pass did not need for the city recipe. Do not paste it onto Đồng Hới.
- Sources: S4, S16, S17

### Banh khoai Dong Hoi
- Vietnamese name: Bánh khoái
- What it is: A palm-sized crisp rice-batter cake. The 15 Mar 2023 article says it is basically like a southern bánh xèo and a little like a northern bánh gối, with a crisp shell. The batter is milled rice, sometimes with a little corn flour, egg, or turmeric. The filling is seasoned ground pork, shrimp, and bean sprouts. The sauce is the point: ground pork, tomato, pineapple, and peanut (S16). This is not the Hue crepe.
- How to eat it: Hot, with that sauce. The article does not describe a herb wrap. Do not copy the Hue method onto it.
- When to order it: A snack or a light meal. Hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Cô Tám and Lê Thành Đồng, from the ward’s shop list (S1).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: `banh-khoai` is the Hue crepe on Kim Long, with a quail egg and a peanut-and-liver sauce. Link it only to keep the pages apart. Suggested new slug if a page is ever accepted: `banh-khoai-dong-hoi`.
- Sources: S1, S16

### Banh bot loc
- Vietnamese name: Bánh bột lọc
- What it is: The 15 Mar 2023 article says it looks like the Hue cake and is made differently, from cassava flour (bột sắn), filled with shrimp, ground pork, mushroom, and bamboo shoot (S16). The ward page says Đồng Hới serves it in three forms: wrapped in a leaf, plain (trần), and fried (rán) (S1). The 2023 article names the plain cake and the banana-leaf steamed cake, and says to eat it hot, with the banana-leaf smell and the sour bamboo (S16). It does not mention the fried form. Print both.
- How to eat it: Hot. The article does not give a dipping method beyond that. Check on arrival for the sauce.
- When to order it: A snack, any daytime hour the shop is open. Hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Lê Thành Đồng and Đồng Hải (S1, S16).
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: Hue bánh bột lọc, if copy already mentions it. Do not reuse a Hue dish page. There is no published Đồng Hới cake page.
- Sources: S1, S16

### Banh xeo with red rice
- Vietnamese name: Bánh xèo
- What it is: The 7 Dec 2015 visitor article describes a Đồng Hới bánh xèo made with red rice. Eaten with snakehead, a salad of bean sprouts, lettuce, and sesame, rice crackers (bánh đa), and a dipping sauce. The method it gives: roll the crepe with herbs, salad, and snakehead, then pinch it in the cracker (S6). The 2023 fair listed “bánh xèo Ba Đồn” as its own stall, so a Ba Đồn crepe was being shown in the city and is not automatically the red-rice cake (S12).
- How to eat it: Rolled, as the 2015 article says, just after it comes off the pan (S6).
- When to order it: When it is just cooked (S6). Hour of day: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: The 2015 article says “in the city” and does not name a street (S6). The fair was on Bảo Ninh (S12).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: `banh-xeo` is the southern and Da Nang crepe. Link it to keep the red-rice roll separate. Suggested slug: `banh-xeo-dong-hoi`.
- Sources: S6, S12

### Khoai deo
- Vietnamese name: Khoai deo
- What it is: A gift sweet from red sweet potato. The 15 Mar 2023 article says the potato is boiled, sliced, and sun-dried 9–12 days on sand over a layer of rười grass until the slice is chestnut brown and leathery (S16). The 2023 fair sold it with the dried seafood (S12).
- How to eat it: As a thing to take home. The article does not describe a café serving.
- When to order it: Buy it in the morning market run. Not a plated meal.
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Đồng Hới and chợ Nam Lý (S16). Nam Lý is only a buy-it-here line on that page, not a second food street.
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S12, S16

### Chao ca ngheo
- Vietnamese name: Cháo cá nghéo
- What it is: A bowl the 7 Dec 2015 article places at Đá Nhảy, not in the Đồng Hới market. It says cá nghéo is a local cartilaginous fish, related to sharks, that bears live young, with lean meat and rough skin. The porridge is finished with oyster mushroom, straw mushroom, and button mushroom, and served over a bed of finely cut green mustard, with Đồng Hới fish sauce and hot chili (S6). One source, and it is a feature story.
- How to eat it: A hot bowl at the beach, then the article’s writer rented a hammock in the casuarina (S6). That hammock is a scene in the story, not a bookable service.
- When to order it: Lunch, in the story (S6). Hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Đá Nhảy, under Lý Hòa pass. Not a Đồng Hới street.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none. Suggested slug if a page is accepted: `chao-ca-ngheo`.
- Sources: S6

### So huyet song Roon
- Vietnamese name: Sò huyết sông Roòn
- What it is: Cockles from the Roòn River in old Quảng Trạch, where the 2023 article says salt and fresh water meet. Eaten steamed or grilled, or raw with lime, after a soak in rice water or light salt water so they spit the sand (S16). Not a Đồng Hới market bowl unless a stall happens to have them.
- How to eat it: Open the shell and dip in chili sauce or salt, pepper, and lime (S16).
- When to order it: When the trip is already in Quảng Trạch. Not a reason to leave Nhật Lệ by itself. Ride time from Đồng Hới: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Sông Roòn, old Quảng Trạch.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none published
- Sources: S16

## Food streets and markets

### Dong Hoi Market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Đồng Hới
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Shops the city’s main wholesale market on Mẹ Suốt, beside the river and the Mother Suốt memorial, with a fish landing next to it (S1, S4). The portal says the hall is ordinary and worth the walk for bánh xèo, cháo canh, bánh canh, and kẹo cu đơ, and that most counters sell in the early morning and the afternoon (S4).
- When to go: Morning, and again in the afternoon (S4). The same card prints open 04:00 and closed 18:00, a 120-minute visit, “Giá: Đang cập nhật”, and on the English mirror a placeholder email. One CMS card. Hours and any entrance fee: Check on arrival. The address line says Đường Mẹ Suốt, phường Đồng Hới, tỉnh Quảng Trị. The prose still says the old Hải Đình ward (S4).
- Area: Mẹ Suốt, left bank of the Nhật Lệ
- Suggested slug: `dong-hoi-market`
- Photo must show: the market hall or the landing with boats, the river in the frame if it fits, people at the counters, not a single plate
- Sources: S1, S4

### Le Thanh Dong
- Vietnamese name: Đường Lê Thành Đồng
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats the small cakes and the cháo canh. The ward page and the 2023 specialty article pile several shops onto this one street: bánh bột lọc, bánh bèo, and bánh khoái (S1, S16). The portal’s bánh canh card puts another cháo canh shop here (S17). It is an eating street because the official lists cluster here, not because a sign calls it a food street.
- When to go: Daytime. No street-level hour was printed. Hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area: Center, inland of the river
- Suggested slug: `le-thanh-dong`
- Photo must show: the street with more than one open shop front, in daylight, not a menu close-up
- Sources: S1, S16, S17

### Dong Hai by day
- Vietnamese name: Đường Đồng Hải
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats bánh bột lọc, bánh bèo, and cháo canh at the shops the ward and portal lists put on Đồng Hải (S1, S17). The same street is half of the weekend walking street after 18:00 (S1). Daytime it is a food street. After dark on Friday, Saturday, and Sunday it is the night walk in attractions.md.
- When to go: Daytime for the bowls. Hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area: Center, the citadel side
- Suggested slug: `dong-hai`
- Photo must show: the street in daylight with shop fronts, not the night pedestrian photo used for the walking street
- Sources: S1, S17
