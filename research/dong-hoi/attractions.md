# Research dossier — Dong Hoi

Slug: `dong-hoi`
Province: Former Quảng Bình. Resolution 202/2025/QH15 merged that province into Quảng Trị, effective 12 June 2025, with the new provincial government operating from 1 July 2025 (S10). The ward office prints 88 Phạm Văn Đồng, phường Đồng Hới, tỉnh Quảng Trị (S1). Visitor pages still say Quảng Bình and thành phố Đồng Hới (S1, S4).
Kind: city
Corridor: north-central
Base a traveler sleeps in: Nhật Lệ beachfront, Bảo Ninh across the river, or a riverside room by the market and Quảng Bình Quan. The airport is 6 km north and is not the picture (S8).
Checked: 21 Oct 2026

## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: yellow sand, a low line of casuarina, the Nhật Lệ mouth, and the city on the near bank. Across the water is Bảo Ninh, a coconut peninsula. At night the single tower of Nhật Lệ 2 is the lit shape over the river (S1). The caves are a day away. The airport is not the picture.
- Where they sleep, as an area: beachfront rooms on Nhật Lệ, or the peninsula at Bảo Ninh, or the river streets by the market and the old gate. Never a hotel brand.
- Where the evening is: Phan Bội Châu and Đồng Hải, the walking street the ward prints for Friday, Saturday, Sunday, and holidays, about 1 km, 18:00–24:00 (S1). On other nights the ward page sends people to the river path and the sunset on the sand, and it does not name a bar street (S1).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): Nhật Lệ 2, described on the ward tourism page (S1). Quảng Bình Quan, the old south gate, on the provincial tourism portal (S3). Chợ Đồng Hới on Mẹ Suốt, the market beside the river (S4). They are pins. No scores.
- Hero must include: the sand, the small surf, the casuarina line, and either the city bank or the Nhật Lệ 2 tower in the same frame, subject through the center, in color, in daylight or at the sunset the ward page names (S1).
- A crop that would fail: empty sand with no trees and no bank; a tight portrait of the Mother Suốt statue that loses the head or the oar; a tight crop of the Tam Toa shaft that cuts it off against empty sky; the airport fence; a cave interior, which is Phong Nha and not this night.

### Tam Toa bell tower

The ward tourism page puts the tower in the central park (S1). No street number on either page. Address: Check on arrival.

The same page says some documents put a baptism of the poet Hàn Mặc Tử here around 1912. That is the page’s hedge, not a second source. Do not state the baptism as settled.

- From the base: Walk from the river park (S1).
- When to go: Daylight, so the broken masonry reads. The ward page calls the park quiet (S1).
- Mistake to avoid: Budgeting a church service or a museum hour. The building is a ruin in a park.
- Local interest: The river war relics
- Suggested slug: `tam-toa`
- Suggested dot: 4 — the war relic people can see without leaving the center
- Photo must show: the full remaining tower from the park ground, headroom above the shaft, not a tight crop that cuts the top
- Sources: S1, S2

### Quang Binh Gate
- Vietnamese name: Quảng Bình Quan
- Kind: sight
- Area: Đồng Hải on the 2024 heritage list (S2). The provincial portal calls it the south gate, Cổng Hạ, of the Lũy Thầy line on the old north–south road (S3). No street number in the attraction text. A “Số 01 Lý Thường Kiệt” line on that portal page was not clearly the gate’s door. Address: Check on arrival.
- What the visitor does: Looks at the restored brick gate. The ward page says Đào Duy Từ had the rampart built in 1631, the gate rebuilt in brick and stone in 1825, and repaired in 1961 (S1). The portal says the line dates from 1631, the repair of 1961 was close to the original, American bombs nearly destroyed it in 1965, and it has been restored again (S3). A shorter visitor note says the Nguyễn lords built it from 1630 (S15). Print 1630 and 1631. The portal also records the later name Định Bắc trường thành, given in the text when Thiệu Trị passed the rampart (S3).
- Time: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Fee: The tour card prints “Miễn phí” (S5). No separate gate tariff was opened. Fee: Check on arrival.
- From the base: Walk from the river and the market. The ward page treats the gate as the city’s emblem, in the center (S1).
- When to go: Daylight for the brick. It is also a pin for the evening walk (S1, S3).
- Mistake to avoid: Treating it as a Hue-scale citadel tour. It is a gate.
- Local interest: The gate and the short walls
- Suggested slug: `quang-binh-gate`
- Suggested dot: 4 — the landmark in a walk around the center, not a day by itself
- Photo must show: the whole gate from the street, in color, with enough wall to show it is a gate and not a monument plinth
- Sources: S1, S2, S3, S5, S15

### Dong Hoi Citadel
- Vietnamese name: Thành Đồng Hới
- Kind: sight
- Area: The ward page places the remaining walls in the old Hải Định ward, with a moat, and an east gate toward the Nhật Lệ reached by an arched bridge (S1). The 2024 heritage list places Thành Đồng Hới in Đồng Hải and ranks it history and architecture, a ministry decision (S2). Ward names changed. Use both and do not draw a new map.
- What the visitor does: Walks the short restored sections. The ward page says this was a Nguyễn defensive work in the Trịnh–Nguyễn wars, that more than 200 years of wall remain only in pieces, and that after restoration the east gate and the arched bridge became the part visitors study (S1). It is not a complete imperial city.
- Time: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Fee: Check on arrival. No ticket page. The tour card’s free label is not a citadel tariff (S5).
- From the base: Walk. The ward page puts the Ho Chi Minh square inside the same citadel area, on Hùng Vương (S1).
- When to go: With the gate and the square, in daylight.
- Mistake to avoid: Promising a Hue citadel. The page says only short sections remain (S1).
- Local interest: The gate and the short walls
- Suggested slug: `dong-hoi-citadel`
- Suggested dot: 3 — the walls explain the gate, and they are not a separate day
- Photo must show: a stretch of wall and the moat, or the east gate and the arched bridge, with a person or the river for scale
- Sources: S1, S2

### Mother Suot
- Vietnamese name: Bến đò và tượng đài Mẹ Suốt
- Kind: sight
- Area: Both banks of the Nhật Lệ. The ministry page puts the national historic site at thôn Trung Bính, Bảo Ninh (S13). The biography page puts the landing at Trung Bính on the right bank and near the Đồng Hới fish market on the left bank, and says the town built the stele in 1980 (S14). A visitor note puts the statue about 1 km from Nhật Lệ bridge, on Quách Xuân Kỳ (S15). The ward page puts the statue beside the river and the market on Mẹ Suốt (S1).
- What the visitor does: Stands at the ferry landing where Nguyễn Thị Suốt rowed soldiers, ammunition, and messages across the Nhật Lệ under American bombing. The ministry page calls her a hero of Bảo Ninh who was killed on a crossing (S13). The biography page says she was named Hero of Labor on 1 January 1967 and quotes Tố Hữu’s poem (S14). The statue is the pin. The ward page also sends visitors to the market on the same walk (S1).
- Time: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Fee: The tour card prints “Miễn phí” on the statue (S5). The ministry page does not print a fee (S13). Fee: Check on arrival.
- From the base: Walk along the river from the market or the bridge (S1, S15).
- When to go: Daylight, with the market.
- Mistake to avoid: Looking for a working ferry as the visit. The pages describe a memorial of the wartime ferry, not a current boat service (S13, S14).
- Local interest: The river war relics
- Suggested slug: `mother-suot`
- Suggested dot: 4 — the figure on the river walk, beside the market
- Photo must show: the full statue and the river in one frame, headroom above the figure, not a crop of the torso
- Sources: S1, S2, S5, S13, S14, S15
