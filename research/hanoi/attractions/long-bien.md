## What it is

- English name: Long Bien Bridge
- Vietnamese name: Cầu Long Biên
- Kicker (one line, what it is): Steel bridge across the Red River
- Kind: sight
- Separate visit: yes. The walk is the side path of the steel bridge between the Hoan Kiem end and Bo De. It is not a lane of the Old Quarter, and it is not a split of Hoan Kiem, West Lake, or the citadel.
- Suggested dot: none in the dossier. Public dot 3.

## What the visitor does

- The scene, in order: Reach the Hoan Kiem end beside the Old Quarter, walk the side path across the Red River, and keep the railway in the middle. Come back the same way, or continue to Bo De and return.
- How long on site: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Travel pages print 45 minutes to two hours. One secondary source is not a published duration.
- How long including the ride from the base: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. A walk minute from Hoan Kiem is not on an opened primary page.
- Mistake that wastes the visit: Walking the railway in the middle, or arriving while the side paths are closed for repairs.
- Notice, if one fact would waste the day: The side paths were shut for repairs from 26 March 2026 and reopened at 00:00 on 28 May 2026. Motorbikes, bicycles and other non-motorized vehicles, and people on foot use those side paths in both directions (A3).

## Sources

### A1
- URL: https://vqh.hanoi.gov.vn/index.php?language=vi&nv=news&op=tin-lien-ket%2FLong-Bien-Cay-cau-bac-qua-ba-the-ky-1004.html
- Publisher: Hanoi People’s Council site, reprint of tapchikientruc.com.vn, 2 Nov 2018
- Accessed: 23 Oct 2026
- Supports: First bridge across the Red River. Started 1899, finished 1902. Daydé & Pillé, Paris. Named the Doumer Bridge. Also called Cầu Sông Cái and Cầu Long Biên. Length 1,682 m, 19 spans alternating 75 m and 106 m. Railway width 4.75 m. Side paths described as wider than 3 m. West brick approach 896 m, 5 m high, 125 arches. Sits beside the Old Quarter. Steel tied with rivets on concrete piers.
- Second council reprint, same site, 24 Oct 2023: https://vqh.hanoi.gov.vn/index.php?language=vi&nv=news&op=tin-lien-ket%2Fcau-long-bien-sinh-ra-tu-mot-y-tuong-dien-ro-2368.html — sourced to VTV.vn. Steel structure 1,682 m. Daydé et Pillé won the 1897 competition against Levallois-Perret and four other firms. Cantilever. 19 spans, 20 piers more than 30 m below the lowest water. Height 61 m. Two end spans 78.70 m, nine spans 75 m, eight spans 106.20 m. Ground broken September 1898. Banks joined on 3 February (year not printed in that sentence). First official Hanoi–Haiphong train 8 April 1902. Originally rail with 1.3 m sidewalks; cars used the ferry. Road lanes widened from 1922, finished December 1923, inaugurated 23 April 1924. Renamed Long Bien in 1945. The span lengths and the start year disagree with the 2018 reprint. Both stay. Do not average.
