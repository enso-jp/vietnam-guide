## What it is

- English name: Quan Thanh Temple
- Vietnamese name: Đền Quán Thánh
- Kicker (one line, what it is): Northern guardian temple on West Lake
- Kind: sight
- Separate visit: yes. A ticketed temple at the corner of Thanh Nien and Quan Thanh street, looking onto West Lake. It is not Tran Quoc, and it is not a split of the West Lake page or the citadel.
- Suggested dot: none in the dossier. Public dot 3.

## What the visitor does

- The scene, in order: See the white gate from Thanh Nien, buy a ticket, walk the courtyard and the halls, and see the black-bronze Tran Vu figure in the rear sanctuary.
- How long on site: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. A travel page’s 20–40 minutes is one secondary source.
- How long including the ride from the base: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. No opened page prints minutes from Hoan Kiem.
- Mistake that wastes the visit: Treating it as a free shore pagoda, or as the same stop as Tran Quoc.
- Notice, if one fact would waste the day: Admission is charged (B2, B3). The amount and the day’s hours are Check on arrival.

## Fee

- Basic price, and who pays it: Check on arrival on the public page. B3 (VTV, 19 Feb 2024, quoting the temple management) prints 10,000 VND a visitor, 5,000 VND for students, older people, and disabled visitors, and free under 15. Travel pages repeat 10,000 VND and often copy one another. That is one news report plus secondary copies, not an operator price page. The amount stays off the public fee.
- Child, height, or senior rule: Under 15 free, and 5,000 VND for students, older people, and disabled visitors, on B3 only. Not printed in public.
- What the basic ticket includes: Entry to the temple. B3 says electronic tickets from September 2022, and that the fee follows a Hanoi People’s Council resolution and is paid into the state budget. The resolution text was not opened.
- What costs extra: UNSOURCED
- Source, or UNSOURCED: B2 confirms it is a fee-charging special national relic. B3 is the only opened figure for the amount. Public fee: Check on arrival.

## Sources

### B1
- URL: https://vietnamtourism.vn/index.php/tourism/items/2795
- Publisher: Vietnam National Authority of Tourism
- Accessed: 23 Oct 2026
- Supports: Corner of Co Ngu, now Thanh Nien, and Quan Thanh street, looking onto West Lake. Also called Tran Vu Quan. Worships Huyen Thien Tran Vu, the northern guardian of Thang Long. Built in 1010 under Ly Thai To. One of the four Thang Long guardians: Tran Vu north, Bach Ma east, Linh Lang at Voi Phuc west, Cao Vuong at Kim Lien south. Black-bronze figure recast in 1677, height 3.07 m, circumference 8 m, weight 4 tons, in the rear sanctuary. Gate on the Thanh Nien pavement, four pillars. No hours and no fee on this page.

### B2

- Publisher: Ba Dinh ward / Hanoi, 19 Feb 2026
- Accessed: 23 Oct 2026
