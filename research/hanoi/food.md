# Food — Hanoi

Checked: 21 Oct 2026

The national tourism board is the source for this first pass. It says Hanoi food is light and traditional, and that black pepper, not chili, is the heat (S24). No stall price is copied. A shop name that located a dish is under Notes, not pages, and is not a pick.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Phở
- Vietnamese name: Phở
- What it is: Rice noodles in a clear broth. The board says beef bone is simmered with star anise, cinnamon, ginger, and black cardamom, and the bowl is finished with green onion and beef steak, flank, or brisket. Chicken is the other common bowl (S24). A second board page says to start beef phở with chili sauce and garlic vinegar, and chicken phở with lime (S23).
- How to eat it: Taste the broth first. The Hanoi bowl is plainer than a southern one. The board does not describe a plate of herbs as part of the Hanoi bowl (S23, S24).
- When to order it: Breakfast, lunch, or a cold-weather supper. The board says stalls serve it at all hours (S24).
- Area, not a shop: Old Quarter stalls. A named shop is not a pick.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: the southern bowl, already on the shared phở record
- Sources: S23, S24

### Bún chả
- Vietnamese name: Bún chả
- What it is: Chargrilled pork belly and patties with rice vermicelli, herbs, and a bowl of diluted vinegar fish sauce. The same publisher’s other page calls that bowl a hot, sweet broth. Both lines stand (S23, S24).
- How to eat it: Put the noodles and herbs into the bowl, and take a little of everything in one bite. Fried spring rolls, nem, are the board’s optional addition (S23).
- When to order it: Lunch. The board calls it almost exclusively a lunch dish (S23, S24).
- Area, not a shop: Old Quarter and the lakeside streets. A named shop is not a pick.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on these pages
- Sources: S23, S24

### Bún đậu
- Vietnamese name: Bún đậu mắm tôm
- What it is: Crispy fried tofu and fresh rice noodles. The board says the original dip is mắm tôm, fermented shrimp paste, and that fish sauce is the lighter choice. Young rice patties, fried spring rolls, and herbs come with it (S23).
- How to eat it: Dip the tofu and noodles. Shrimp paste is the strong version.
- When to order it: Lunch (S23).
- Area, not a shop: Old Quarter. A named shop is not a pick.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S23

### Chả cá
- Vietnamese name: Chả cá
- What it is: Fish marinated, grilled, then finished at the table with spring onion and dill, eaten with rice vermicelli, peanuts, and mắm tôm. One page says fatty catfish. The other says snakehead, turmeric, and dill, and that Chả Cá street is named for the dish (S23, S24). Both fish names stand. Do not pick one.
- How to eat it: At the table, with the herbs and the shrimp paste. The board says the paste belongs in the dish (S24).
- When to order it: A sit-down meal, not a breakfast bowl. Hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: Chả Cá street, in the Old Quarter. The board places the dish on Đường Thành as well (S23, S24). A named restaurant is not a pick.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S23, S24

### Bánh cuốn
- Vietnamese name: Bánh cuốn
- What it is: A thin steamed rice-batter sheet rolled around minced pork, mushroom, and shallot, with a side of pork sausage and herbs, dipped in nước mắm (S24).
- How to eat it: As a light roll, not a heavy noodle bowl.
- When to order it: Breakfast. The board calls it a popular breakfast everywhere in the city (S24).
- Area, not a shop: The city, not one street on this page.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S24

### Bánh tôm
- Vietnamese name: Bánh tôm Hồ Tây
- What it is: A fried cake of shredded sweet potato with a whole shrimp in it. The board says the cakes were first made with shrimp taken from West Lake at night, and that the shrimp now comes from other water. Wrap in lettuce and dip in fish sauce (S24).
- How to eat it: With lettuce and fish sauce, as a snack.
- When to order it: The board does not print an hour.
- Area, not a shop: Associated with West Lake. No shop is named on this page.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S24

### Bún thang
- Vietnamese name: Bún thang
- What it is: A clear broth with Vietnamese ham, shredded chicken, and omelette strips. The board calls it a breakfast that grew out of leftover Tết food (S23).
- How to eat it: As a finished bowl. A garnish list beyond that was not on the page.
- When to order it: Breakfast (S23).
- Area, not a shop: A named shop is not a pick. The board places one address on Cầu Gỗ. The dish is not that address.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S23

### Cà phê trứng
- Vietnamese name: Cà phê trứng
- What it is: Strong robusta under a sweet egg foam. The weekend page says the drink came from using whipped egg in place of fresh milk (S25). The dish list says the foam sits on the coffee (S23).
- How to eat it: Sit down with it. It is not a takeaway soup.
- When to order it: Any time after breakfast. The weekend page uses it as a morning start (S25).
- Area, not a shop: The Old Quarter. A named cafe is not a pick.
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: the shared cà phê record. This pass adds the egg version for Hanoi only.
- Sources: S23, S25

### Nộm bò khô
- Vietnamese name: Nộm bò khô
- What it is: Green papaya and carrot with beef jerky and fish sauce. The board calls it an afternoon snack. Beef spleen and tripe are the optional version (S23). The Old Quarter page also places it on Hoàn Kiếm street (S1).
- How to eat it: As a salad, on a stool.
- When to order it: Afternoon (S23).
- Area, not a shop: Hoàn Kiếm street. A named stall is not a pick.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S23

## Food streets and markets

- Vietnamese name: Chợ Đồng Xuân
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: The tourism board’s Old Quarter walk sends a visitor down Hàng Đào toward the market, then on toward Long Biên (S3). What is sold inside, and the hour, were on a national-tourism destination page that did not load. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED

- Sources: S3

### Hang Dao weekend night market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ đêm Hàng Đào
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: The Old Quarter page says that after dark on weekends, Hàng Đào turns into a night market for street food, and it names snail meatballs, grilled quail, and cured pork sausage as examples (S1). No stall is a pick.
- When to go: Weekend evenings (S1). A clock was not printed.
- Area: Hàng Đào, Old Quarter
- Suggested slug: hang-dao-night-market
- Photo must show: the lane at night with stalls and people, subject through the center. A dark frame with no stall fails.
- Sources: S1

### Cha Ca street
- Vietnamese name: Phố Chả Cá
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats the turmeric-and-dill fish the street is named for. The board says the street took its name from the dish, and that a family has cooked it there since 1871 (S24). A second page places a different shop at 6B Đường Thành (S23). The street is the destination. The shops are not.
- When to go: A sit-down meal. Hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area: Old Quarter, the Chả Cá / Đường Thành lanes
- Suggested slug: cha-ca-street
- Photo must show: the street sign or the lane, and a table with the fish and dill if the frame can hold both. A plated close-up with no street fails.
- Sources: S23, S24
