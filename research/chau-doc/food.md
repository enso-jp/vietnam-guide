## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Fish noodle soup with dien dien
- Vietnamese name: Bún cá (bún cá bông điên điển)
- What it is: Rice-noodle soup of freshwater fish, turmeric, and crunchy điên điển flowers. Vietnam Tourism calls it the classic Châu Đốc breakfast and the iconic bowl (S1, S3). VnExpress describes a deep broth with shrimp paste, lemongrass, chili, garlic, and fresh turmeric, eaten with điên điển, water lily, bitter herb, banana blossom, shredded morning glory, and beansprouts (S16).
- How to eat it: Season with chili and lime at the table, the way the charms chapter tells visitors to (S3).
- When to order it: Breakfast, at the edge of the market where bowls and coffee are sold (S3).
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Châu Đốc
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: Can Tho’s published `banh-xeo` is a different delta breakfast. Do not rename this bowl as bánh xèo.
- Sources: S1, S3, S16

### Fermented fish
- Vietnamese name: Mắm
- What it is: Fermented fish, the smell that marks the market. The charms chapter describes fish pickled with palm sugar, salt, chili, and garlic, stacked in the open (S3).
- How to eat it: Buy it as the local specialty, or meet it in the hotpot below. A recipe was not copied here.
- When to order it: While walking the market, daytime (S3).
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Châu Đốc
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on a Chau Doc page yet
- Sources: S3

### Fermented-fish hotpot
- Vietnamese name: Lẩu mắm
- What it is: The dinner Vietnam Tourism says not to leave without (S1). The charms chapter’s shared evening pot is vegetables, mushrooms, noodles, and herbs in a hot sour soup, in the same cuisine chapter (S3). This pass did not find a second page that prints a Châu Đốc recipe line by line. Do not invent the broth.
- How to eat it: Shared, at dinner (S1, S3).
- When to order it: Evening (S1).
- Area, not a shop: Town restaurants along the river. Not a named shop.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S3

### Claypot caramel fish
- Vietnamese name: Cá kho tộ
- What it is: The charms chapter calls this a Mekong Delta dish cooked for hours in a clay pot until the fish flakes, eaten with rice and greens (S3). It is in the Châu Đốc food chapter, and it is labeled as a delta dish, not a town-only recipe.
- How to eat it: With hot rice and greens (S3).
- When to order it: A main meal (S3).
- Area, not a shop: Town kitchens, not a single street
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S3

### Cham palm-sugar cake
- Vietnamese name: Bánh bò Chăm
- What it is: A soft, crisp cake of palm sugar and coconut milk. The charms chapter says each cake is cooked under a hot clay lid and eaten warm (S3). The province describes folk cakes in the Cham villages without giving this name a second time (S14). One source for the clay-lid method.
- How to eat it: Warm, soon after it leaves the lid (S3).
- When to order it: When visiting Châu Phong or Đa Phước (S3, S14).
- Area, not a shop: The Cham villages, not a brand
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S3, S14

## Food streets and markets

### Chau Doc Market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Châu Đốc
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Walks a large market. On the outskirts, fish-noodle bowls and strong coffee. On the waterfront, boats unload produce. Flowers, fruit, and vegetables sit on the sidewalk. Mắm ferments in the open. Tamarind, jellied sweets, and fish fill the aisles. Old shop-sign lettering is part of what Vietnam Tourism tells you to look up at (S3).
- When to go: Morning, for breakfast and the landing (S3). A closing hour was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area: Town center, phường Châu Đốc. A street number was not on the Vietnam Tourism page. Check on arrival for the gate.
- Suggested slug: `chau-doc-market`
- Photo must show: Produce on the ground, a noodle bowl or a mắm crock, and shop signs in one frame, subject through the center
- Sources: S3

The province said a company in Đa Phước had started a product called Chợ quê làng Chăm in early 2024 (S14). That is a project notice, not a second market this pass could walk. It is not a food street here.
