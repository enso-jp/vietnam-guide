## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Fish noodle soup with dien dien
- Vietnamese name: Bún cá (bún cá bông điên điển)
- What it is: Rice-noodle soup of freshwater fish. Vietnam Tourism calls it the classic Châu Đốc breakfast and the iconic bowl, with turmeric and crunchy điên điển (S1, S3). VnExpress says the bowl came from Cambodia (S31). The 26 Apr 2024 handbook says the older broth used cá linh, dried shrimp, ngải bún, mắm ruốc, lemongrass, chili, garlic, and fresh turmeric, and that today’s bowl is cá lóc, broth, and fresh noodles, the fish boiled with lemongrass and crushed turmeric, then stir-fried with turmeric (S32). The 20 Oct 2023 day calls the broth deep, with shrimp paste, lemongrass, chili, garlic, and fresh turmeric, and lists điên điển, bông súng, bitter herb, banana blossom, shredded morning glory, and beansprouts (S16). The 2019 article says the soul is mắm cá linh and mắm ruốc, pale yellow from crushed turmeric, and that some stalls add ống bones and heo quay (S31). The fish and the aromatics disagree across these accounts. All of them stay.
- How to eat it: Chili and lime, the way the charms chapter tells visitors to (S3). Roast pork or chả is common on S16. A fish head can be ordered alone (S16).
- When to order it: Breakfast, at the edge of the market (S3), and in the town center (S31).
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Châu Đốc and the town center
- Meal: breakfast
- Price: S31 prints an average 20,000 VND, and 10,000 VND away from the center. S16 prints 25,000–30,000 VND, not counting sides. Both stay. Public price: Check on arrival.
- Neighbor dish to link: Can Tho’s published `banh-xeo` is a different delta breakfast.
- Sources: S1, S3, S16, S31, S32

### Fermented fish
- Vietnamese name: Mắm Châu Đốc
- What it is: Fermented fish. The charms chapter describes fish pickled with palm sugar, salt, chili, and garlic, stacked in the open (S3). The dining chapter calls Châu Đốc the kingdom of pickled fish (S33). The ward names mắm Châu Đốc as a specialty and mắm-making as a trade (S34). The province, 6 Dec 2023, records two collective trademarks: đặc sản mắm Châu Đốc and đặc sản mắm thái Châu Đốc (S35).
- How to eat it: With rice, on the spot, or carried home (S31). The province says visitors buy mắm cá lóc, cá linh, cá trèn, cá sặc, and mắm thái (S35). S31 lists mắm Thái, cá lóc, cá linh, ba khía, cá sặc, and cá trèn. S32 names mắm from cá trèn, cá lóc, cá linh, and cá sọc. Cá sặc and cá sọc are both printed. Mắm cá mè vinh is Tân Châu’s specialty on S35, not a Châu Đốc jar.
- When to order it: While walking the market, daytime (S3). The province says the mắm lanes are busiest from December through July, when pilgrims come (S35).
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Châu Đốc, and the foot of Núi Sam on S31. Workshops cluster at Núi Sam and the old Châu Phú A (S35).
- Meal: main
- Price: S31 prints from a few tens of thousands of dong to more than 100,000 VND a kilogram. S35 says each stall posts a price and prints no figure. Both stay. Public price: Check on arrival.
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S3, S31, S32, S33, S34, S35

### Cham beef sausage
- Vietnamese name: Tung lò mò
- What it is: A Cham beef sausage. VnExpress reads the name from Cham tung lamow, cow gut. It looks like a lap xuong and hangs to dry on the outskirts. Cham Muslims here abstain from pork, so the sausage is beef, used at religious ceremonies (S31, S38).
- How to eat it: Cut rounds, grill on charcoal, cut smaller, eat with rice. Sour and not-sour are both sold. The English edition says dip in soy sauce, with basil and pepper in the taste (S31, S38).
- When to order it: When it is grilled. A clock was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Outskirts streets, drying in front of houses
- Meal: main
- Price: S31 prints about 150,000–250,000 VND a kilogram. Public price: Check on arrival.
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S31, S38

### Fermented-fish noodles
- Vietnamese name: Bún mắm
- What it is: A brown, clear, sweet fish broth. VnExpress says it is eaten across the Mekong, adapted from a Cambodian bowl that used prohok, and cooked in the west with cá linh. Sides are roast pork, balut, and often shrimp, fish cake, and pork. Herbs: water-spinach tips, banana blossom, beansprouts, fish mint (S31).
- How to eat it: As a noodle bowl (S31).
- When to order it: Breakfast on the landing and at the market (S16). Also inside the market with the other Mekong bowls (S32).
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Châu Đốc and the Hậu landing
- Meal: breakfast
- Price: S31 prints about 25,000–35,000 VND. Public price: Check on arrival.
- Neighbor dish to link: Da Nang’s `bun-mam` is a different bowl. This file is `bun-mam-chau-doc`.
- Sources: S16, S31, S32

### Shrimp-cake noodles
- Vietnamese name: Bún suông

- Area, not a shop: Hậu landing and Chợ Châu Đốc
- Meal: breakfast
- Price: S37 prints 15,000–25,000 VND for the Trà Vinh bowl. No Châu Đốc price was printed. Public price: Check on arrival.
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S16, S37

### Palm-sugar steamed cake
- Vietnamese name: Bánh bò thốt nốt

VnExpress says it is made from palm sugar and palm-fruit-shell flour, rice flour, and coconut milk, usually yellow, and that the Bảy Núi cake tastes most like itself in Châu Đốc (S32). The province ties palm juice, palm sugar, and this cake to the Bảy Núi (S36).
- How to eat it: As a snack, a dessert, or with thịt khìa or roast pork (S32). A texture beyond the name was not on the place page (S1).
- When to order it: Afternoon, or mid-morning (S1, S32).
- Area, not a shop: Town and the Sam Mountain approaches, where the charms chapter puts the palms (S3).
- Meal: sweet
- Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S3, S32, S36

## Food streets and markets

### Chau Doc Market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Châu Đốc
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Walks a large market. On the outskirts, fish-noodle bowls and strong coffee. On the waterfront, boats unload produce. Flowers, fruit, and vegetables sit on the sidewalk. Mắm ferments in the open. Tamarind, jellied sweets, and fish fill the aisles. Sugarcane juice is on the dining chapter (S33). Old shop-sign lettering is part of what Vietnam Tourism tells you to look up at (S3).
- When to go: Morning, for breakfast and the landing (S3). VnExpress on 20 Oct 2023 prints 05:30–18:00 every day (S16). Vietnam Tourism does not print a clock. Both stay.
- Area: Town center, phường Châu Đốc. A street number was not on the Vietnam Tourism page. Check on arrival for the gate.
- Suggested slug: `chau-doc-market`
- Photo must show: Produce on the ground, a noodle bowl or a mắm crock, and shop signs in one frame, subject through the center
- Sources: S3, S16, S33

### Chau Doc floating market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ nổi Châu Đốc
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Sees several dozen boats in the early morning, laden with fruit and vegetables. Samples hang on long poles. There is no hawking (S33). The charms chapter says wake before dawn for the painted boats of fruit (S3).
- When to go: Early morning, before dawn on S3. A clock was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area: The river. A landing name was not printed. Check on arrival.
- Suggested slug: `chau-doc-floating-market`
- Photo must show: Boats, produce on poles, and the river in one frame, subject through the center
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S3, S33

The province said a company in Đa Phước had started a product called Chợ quê làng Chăm in early 2024 (S14). That is a project notice, not a second market this pass could walk. It is not a food street here.
