## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Nem nướng
- Vietnamese name: Nem nướng Nha Trang (S20)
- What it is: Grilled pork paste from Ninh Hòa. Pork is ground, seasoned, put on bamboo skewers, and grilled over charcoal. The outside is slightly charred and the inside stays soft, on that page (S20).
- How to eat it: A sheet of bánh tráng phơi sương, herbs, pickles, fried ram, and half a skewer, rolled and dipped. The page lists xà lách, diếp cá, hẹ, and other herbs, plus sour starfruit, tart green banana, and pickles (S20). The sauce is cooked from lean pork, liver, ground shrimp, sticky rice, and fermented bean. The page calls the sauce yellow-orange (S20). Taste words on the page, kept as that page’s words: sweet, fatty, sour, tart, and crisp. They are not a second tasting.
- When to order it: The page does not print a clock. It calls the dish the one visitors come for (S20).
- Area, not a shop: Ninh Hòa is the origin. Nha Trang is where the page tells the visitor to eat it (S20). Shop names are under Notes.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: None on this plate. Bún cá is the other Khánh Hòa bowl (S21).
- Photo must show: The roll, the skewers, the herbs, and the orange sauce in one frame.

### Bún cá
- Vietnamese name: Bún cá Ninh Hòa (S21)
- What it is: A noodle bowl from Ninh Hòa, now also a Khánh Hòa specialty eaten in Nha Trang (S21). Fish is steamed, bones removed, and pulled into pieces the page calls cá dầm. The body text says the fish is cá thu, cá ngừ, and cá cờ. The caption says cá dầm is usually made from cá bò and cá ngừ (S21). Both lists stay. The broth is fish bone, simmered for hours, with a little pineapple and tomato (S21). The fish cake is called the soul of the bowl. The page says it is made from cá đỏ, cá nhồng, and cá thu ảo, pounded until it holds, then fried or steamed (S21).
- How to eat it: Herbs cut thin, rau thơm, bean sprouts, and banana blossom. The page says a squeeze of lime, a little chili fish sauce, and a few slices of chili (S21).
- When to order it: From early morning, and again in the evening. The Ninh Hòa speaker on the page says morning or evening (S21). In Nha Trang the page says visitors seek it out and does not name a street (S21).
- Area, not a shop: Ninh Hòa for the origin. Nha Trang for the visitor bowl. No shop address on the page (S21).
- Meal: breakfast, and also a main bowl later in the day (S21)
- Neighbor dish to link: Nem nướng, the other Ninh Hòa dish on the provincial food pages (S20)
- Photo must show: The bowl, with the white fish, the fish cake, and the clear broth.
- unpublished: no licensed photo of the bowl
- Sources: S21

### Bánh căn
- Vietnamese name: Bánh căn (S10, S27, S28)
- What it is: Named as a stall food on the night-market page, next to bún chả and chả cá (S10). The bánh căn article says the cake looks like bánh khọt but is cooked in a clay mold with no oil, so it stays dry, with a light charred smell. Rice soaked overnight is milled fine, poured thin into the hot mold, then topped with a quail egg, fresh squid, or a whole shrimp (S28). Plates named on that article: bánh căn with chicken or quail egg, bánh căn mực lá, bánh căn tôm, bánh căn bò băm, plus fried shallot and scallion oil (S28). Those plates are toppings of this cake, not four more dishes. The street-snack article says the coastal cake, unlike Đà Lạt bánh căn eaten with xíu mại broth, is topped with fresh squid, shrimp, or egg (S27). Both accounts stay.
- How to eat it: S28 names three dips: sweet-sour fish sauce, mắm nêm, or salty-sweet nước mắm cá kho. Each bowl gets scallion oil, pork xíu mại, and chopped mango. S27 names the dip as sweet-sour fish sauce, nước cá kho tót, and scallion oil. Both stay. Taste words on S28, kept as that page’s words: dry, a light char, crisp, sweet seafood, and rich xíu mại. They are not a second tasting.
- When to order it: At the night market (S10). No clock there. S27 says late afternoon along Tháp Bà in Nha Trang, and also names the Phan Rang market. Phan Rang is not a Nha Trang address.
- Area, not a shop: The 137 m walking street behind 46 Trần Phú (S10). Tháp Bà for the late-afternoon stalls (S27). Shop names are under Notes.
- Meal: main, as a stall food. The night-market page does not class it further. S28 files it under street food.
- Neighbor dish to link: None
- Photo must show: The small cakes on the clay mold or the plate, with the seafood or egg visible. A shop sign is not the dish.

- Sources: S10, S27, S28

### Bún sứa
- Vietnamese name: Bún sứa Nha Trang (S29). Also named at Chợ Xóm Mới (S30) and as a street bowl with bún cá (S27).
- What it is: A jellyfish noodle bowl. S29 says the broth is not pork bone. It is bone of cá thu, cá cờ, or cá liệt, with ripe tomato, for a light sour and a clean sweet, and the page says it is not fishy. In the bowl: offshore jellyfish the page calls white, thick, and crisp; fried fish cake and steamed fish cake; cá dầm; lettuce, chopped banana blossom, and bean sprouts; nước mắm ớt xanh (S29). S27 says a breakfast bowl with a clean sweet broth from cá cờ bone and cá dầm. Both broth accounts stay. S29 contrasts it with bún cá, which that page says uses more seasoning, and calls bún sứa clean and not fatty.
- How to eat it: S29 says add a little mắm tôm or mắm ruốc, depending on the shop, a squeeze of lime, and a lot of the cut herbs. Taste words on S29, kept as that page’s words: clean sweet, not fatty, crisp and cool. They are not a second tasting.
- When to order it: Morning or afternoon (S29). S27 names it as a breakfast bowl. No clock.
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Xóm Mới, 49 Ngô Gia Tự, from the tourism center (S30). S29 does not name a street. Shop names are under Notes.
- Meal: breakfast, and also later in the day (S29)
- Neighbor dish to link: Bún cá Ninh Hòa, the other fish bowl (S21, S29)
- Photo must show: The bowl, with the jellyfish, the fish cake, and the clear broth.
- unpublished: no licensed photo of the bowl
- Sources: S27, S29, S30

### Yến sào
- Vietnamese name: Yến sào (S3)
- What it is: Swiftlet nests from islands in the bay. The bureau says Hòn Nội is the famous nest island, with a temple to the trade, and that the island restaurant serves dishes made from the nests (S3). Vietnam Tourism says Yến Island, also called Yen Sao Island, holds thousands of swiftlets (S2). The two island names stay. Products named on the bureau page: raw nests, cleaned nests, and nest drinks, including Sanest (S3). Sanest is a product name on that page, not a café pick.
- How to eat it: The page says dishes at the island restaurant, and drinks to take home (S3). It does not describe a texture. S26, citing unnamed old books, says a person lies down around 7 in the evening, and around 8 to 9 is brought a cup of nest cooked thoroughly and left to cool, then rinses with warm water. The books are not named. That clock is the reprint’s, not a current restaurant hour.
- When to order it: On the island visit (S3). No clock on the bureau page. The old-book hour above is not a shop hour (S26).
- Area, not a shop: Hòn Nội on the bureau page (S3).
- Meal: Not classed. A specialty of the island, not a breakfast street.
- Neighbor dish to link: None
- Photo must show: The nest or the bowl made from it, not a city beach.
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S2, S3, S26
