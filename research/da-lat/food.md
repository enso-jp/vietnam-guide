# Food — Da Lat

Checked: 21 Oct 2026. Specialty pass checked: 23 Oct 2026.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Vietnamese coffee
- Vietnamese name: Cà phê
- What it is: Arabica country around Cau Dat and farm tours on the slopes (S1, S23).
- How to eat it: Hot or iced at a cafe after the lake walk.
- When to order it: Mid-morning
- Area, not a shop: Cau Dat hills or in-town cafes; K'Ho named on Vietnam Tourism without a stall price (S1).
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: shared `ca-phe` page
- Sources: S1, S23

### Avocado ice cream
- Vietnamese name: Kem bơ
- What it is: Blended avocado dessert sold around the market (S1).
- How to eat it: Eat it cold, standing at a counter.
- When to order it: Afternoon snack
- Area, not a shop: Market slope and night market (S5, S25)
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S25

### Grilled rice paper
- Vietnamese name: Bánh tráng nướng
- What it is: Crisp rice sheet grilled with toppings, a Da Lat street snack (S25).
- How to eat it: Fold and eat from the paper.
- When to order it: Night market evening
- Area, not a shop: Night market lanes (S25)
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S25

### Hot soy milk
- Vietnamese name: Sữa đậu nành nóng
- What it is: Sweet hot soy milk on cool evenings (S1).
- How to eat it: Cup in hand while walking.
- When to order it: After dark when the air cools
- Area, not a shop: Market and night market (S25)
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S25

### Strawberry with condensed milk
- Vietnamese name: Dâu sữa
- What it is: Fresh strawberries with sweet milk, a market souvenir (S1, S5).
- How to eat it: Cup or bag; wash fruit if you are cautious.
- When to order it: Daytime at the market
- Area, not a shop: Da Lat Market (S5)
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S5

### Artichoke tea
- Vietnamese name: Trà atiso
- What it is: Herbal tea from local artichoke, sold as a Lam Dong specialty on provincial desks.
- How to eat it: Hot or bottled to take home.
- When to order it: Any time; souvenir shops cluster near the market.
- Area, not a shop: Market area
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S3

### Banh mi
- Vietnamese name: Bánh mì
- What it is: Baguette sandwiches in the morning like any Vietnamese city; no Da Lat-only filling list on Vietnam Tourism (S1).
- How to eat it: In the hand at breakfast.
- When to order it: Morning
- Area, not a shop: Street counters near the market
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: `banh-mi`
- Sources: S1

- Where: 26 Hoàng Diệu and 26 Phạm Nhật Duật (S28). S26’s named door is not published as a pick.
- Price: about 10,000–15,000 VND a bowl (S28). Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.

### Persimmon
- `hong-treo-gio`: famous Da Lat specialty (S35). OCOP strength “hồng treo gió Đà Lạt” (S39). Innovation center: hung in controlled temperature, humidity, and wind; very sweet, chewy, fragrant. Price line is liên hệ, no đồng figure (S40). Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Season: garden article, late October and November (S35). September article, crunchy season September through December (S36). Both printed.
- Where: Trần Hưng Đạo, Khe Sanh, end of Mimosa Pass. Lê Vân garden named (S35). September article also names roads around Cau Dat (S36).
- `hong-gion`: gift fruit, sweet and aromatic (S35). September article also says freshly picked fruit is sweet, soft, and juicy (S36). Both printed. Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- `hong-say-deo`: Vietnam Tourism lists hồng sấy (S35). Innovation center describes hồng sấy dẻo, air-dried or cold-dried, favored in highlands such as Da Lat (S40). Rural desk: fragrant dried persimmon at a Cam Ly resort-yard fair (S37). Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- `hong-chin-mem` and `hong-ep`: named with the other persimmon specialties (S35). Method and price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.

### Jars, wine, fruit, artichoke extract
- `mut-trai-cay`: famous preserved fruits at the market fringe, with candied ginger, coffee, and artichoke tea. Early morning (S27). Wikipedia city article names mứt trái cây with the wine and the tea (S41). Other fruits in the jars not listed. Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- `mut-gung`: candied ginger, same market row (S27). Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- `mut-dau`: mildly sweet, fragrant strawberry jam at a seasonal fair in a resort yard in Cam Ly. Fairs quarterly, monthly, or weekly depending on the host (S37). Standing shop, jar size, price, hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- `vang-da-lat`: wine from Da Lat grapes and local specialty fruit. First product 1999, called the first grape wine made by Vietnamese producers. More than 2 million litres sold 1999–2007. Source of those sentences is Wikipedia citing Hồng Thắm, Diễn đàn Doanh nghiệp, 5 Aug 2009 (S38). Trademark year and later awards on that article are not copied; the citation marker sits on the 1999 sentences. Shop and price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- `dau-tay-da-lat`: fresh fruit. OCOP name dâu tây Đà Lạt (S39). Vietnam Tourism lists strawberries among farm crops (S26, S27). Separate from dâu sữa. Kilogram price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- `cao-atiso`: bulletin lists it among processed products from local ingredients (S39). How to take it, shop, price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Separate from trà atiso (S3).
