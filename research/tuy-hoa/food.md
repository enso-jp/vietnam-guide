## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Chive noodle soup
- Vietnamese name: Bánh canh hẹ
- What it is: The bowl the provincial pages treat as the local noodle. Soft strands, not mushy, with sea-fish cake and a lot of chives. The roundup says the strand can be bột lọc or bột gạo (S19). The handbook says it is sold all day (S14).
- How to eat it: A hot bowl, chives and chả cá in the same spoon (S14, S19).
- When to order it: Morning or midday. The roundup singles out a cold day (S19). A season for chives was not printed.
- Area, not a shop: Chợ Tuy Hòa, and the Trần Hưng Đạo side of that market. The handbook also points at the Ngô Quyền and Trần Hưng Đạo corner (S14, S19).
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S14, S19

### Ocean tuna
- Vietnamese name: Cá ngừ đại dương
- What it is: The fish the tourism office says Phú Yên led the country in catching. Also called cá bò gù on that page. The eye, steamed, is the dish visitors are pointed to. In 2017 the page says mắt cá ngừ đại dương hầm thuốc bắc was named in a Vietnam-records food list (S17). The handbook adds gỏi bao tử and cá ngừ cuốn cải xanh (S14).
- How to eat it: Thin cold slices rolled in mustard greens with wasabi, chilli sauce, soy, herbs, green banana, and ginger. The eye is steamed with spices, or with thuốc bắc (S14, S17).
- When to order it: A main meal. No season was printed.
- Area, not a shop: Lê Duẩn, and bờ kè Bạch Đằng (S14). The night page also points at the Điện Biên Phủ and Lê Duẩn corner as a tuna area (S13).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S13, S14, S17

### O Loan blood cockle
- Vietnamese name: Sò huyết đầm Ô Loan
- What it is: The lagoon’s cockle. The dish page says the meat is pink with yellow roe (S18). The lagoon page says the taste is why the lagoon has a name, and that the shells were used by the Quảng Đức pottery (S15).
- How to eat it: Grilled on charcoal until the liquor runs, then a little salt, pepper, and lime, eaten hot (S18). The handbook also names nướng tái, xào me, hấp, gỏi, and rang muối ớt (S14). The night boat on the lagoon is the other way (S15).
- When to order it: With the lagoon, day or the night boat. A closed season was not printed.
- Area, not a shop: Đầm Ô Loan, and the floating kitchens at cầu An Hải (S15).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S14, S15, S18

### Emperor crab
- Vietnamese name: Cua Huỳnh Đế
- What it is: A lagoon crab the tourism page says is red or yellow on the shell while still alive, with a tuft of short yellow hair, and walks forward because the claws sit at the front (S15).
- How to eat it: The page names it as a lagoon specialty and does not print a recipe.
- When to order it: With the lagoon meal (S15).
- Area, not a shop: Đầm Ô Loan (S15).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S15

### Rice-sheet rolls with pork
- Vietnamese name: Bánh hỏi lòng heo
- What it is: Rice sheets the roundup ties to xứ Nẫu feasts: weddings, anniversaries, đình and pagoda days (S19). The handbook serves them with scallion fat, rice paper, herbs, garlic-chilli fish sauce, and a bowl of cháo lòng (S14).
- How to eat it: A sheet with roast or grilled pork and offal, herbs, and the sauce (S14, S19).
- When to order it: A feast day, and also in town when a shop is open. Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: The handbook names Duy Tân and Hùng Vương as streets where people send visitors (S14). The roundup names An Mỹ on QL1A and Nguyễn Văn Linh (S19). Streets, not picks.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S14, S19

### One-day beef
- Vietnamese name: Bò một nắng
- What it is: Thigh and loin, seasoned and dried for one day, then grilled, pounded soft, eaten with herbs, cucumber, and muối kiến vàng (S14). Also on the gift list (S14).
- How to eat it: Grilled, torn, with herbs and the ant salt (S14).
- When to order it: A meal or a gift. No season was printed.
- Area, not a shop: The handbook does not pin one street for the meal. The gift is something to carry (S14).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S14

### Phu Yen chicken rice
- Vietnamese name: Cơm gà Phú Yên
- What it is: Chicken the handbook calls lean, yellow, and a little chewy, with cucumber, pickled onion, and đồ chua. The same page says it is less famous than the chicken rice of Hội An or Tam Kỳ (S14).
- How to eat it: A plate of rice and chicken with the pickles (S14).
- When to order it: Midday. Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: The handbook’s clue streets are Lê Thánh Tôn, Lê Trung Kiên, and Phan Đình Phùng (S14).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: Hội An chicken rice, place `hoi-an`. A dish slug was not opened in this pass.
- Sources: S14

### Steamed rice cakes
- Vietnamese name: Bánh bèo
- What it is: Small cups, fragrant and chewy, topped with fried bread, pork floss, and scallion fat, finished with sweet-sour fish sauce. The handbook says they are not the same as bánh bèo elsewhere (S14).
- How to eat it: A spoon from the hot cup (S14).
- When to order it: A snack. Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: The foot of Núi Nhạn. The handbook’s clue is Lê Trung Kiên and Tản Đà (S14).
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S14

### Quan De liquor
- Vietnamese name: Rượu Quán Đế
- What it is: The local spirit the gift page ties to Xuân Lộc and Xuân Bình in Sông Cầu, lighter and sweeter than ordinary rice liquor. The page says the seahorse bottle is the one people ask for (S28). The lagoon page names a glass of it with the night boat (S15).
- How to eat it: A small glass. The gift page says in moderation (S28). This dossier does not state a health claim beyond that sentence.
- When to order it: With the lagoon night, or as a bottle to carry (S15, S28).
- Area, not a shop: Sông Cầu for the stills. The Tuy Hòa shop address on the gift page is a clue only (S28).
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S15, S28

### White-stone mango
- Vietnamese name: Xoài Đá Trắng
- What it is: The mango garden around Chùa Từ Quang. The bureau says the fruit went to the court, so it is also called xoài Ngự (S29). The proverb on that page is “Xoài Đá Trắng, sắn phường Lụa”.
- How to eat it: As fruit. A season was not printed.
- When to order it: In season. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED for the month.
- Area, not a shop: The pagoda garden at Xuân Đài (S29).
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S29

## Food streets and markets
