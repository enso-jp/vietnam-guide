# Food — Cat Ba

Slug: `cat-ba`
Checked: 23 Oct 2026

A specialty here is a dish or a named island food, not a street and not a restaurant. Streets are under food streets. Restaurant names are under notes. No stall price is copied. The 20 June 2013 district posting is a price board. The đồng figures on it are not repeated.

Count: 52 dish headings below. That clears 20. The special-zone page itself names nine foods. The rest are drinks, the titled grill, and lines on the 20 June 2013 board. Several headings hold more than one printed name. A cooking method is printed for nước mắm, and is inside the name of gà nướng mật ong, ngao hấp xả, cháo ngao, cháo cá, and cá thu kho một nắng. The other seafood is ordered by the kilogram, the piece, the pair, or the plate on the 20 June 2013 board, or named on the 28 July 2025 special-zone list, with no method. Taste is printed only where a page printed it. Current doors are not known. The 2013 addresses are source clues. English animal names are not added where the page did not print them.

Left out of the dish list, on purpose: size grades and type numbers of the same printed animal; the 2013 prices; the unit cell “Bỏt” beside phở, cháo, and bánh đa cua, which is not its own dish line. Also left out as generic plates, not island specialties: khoai tây chiên, rau muống + cải xào, rau muống + cải luộc, trứng rán, and thịt lợn kho, printed at Thành Long, vịnh Bến Bèo, and at Viễn Dương, số 12 đường Núi Ngọc (S21). Phở is recorded, because it is its own line. It is not on the special-zone specialty sentence.

### Cat Hai fish sauce
- Vietnamese name: nước mắm Cát Hải; the page also uses the older name nước mắm Vạn Vân
- What it is: A condiment. The district page says the smell meets you on đảo Cát Hải, that the Vạn Vân workshop dates from 1916, and that the sauce runs from 15 to 40 degrees of protein. Fish named: cá nhâm, cá nục, cá quẩn, cá mực, cá cơm, mainly cá nhâm. Salted fish is sunned 12 to 24 months. The page says no chemical is used for smell or color (S20). The honey page tells a Cát Bà visitor they can also buy this sauce (S19). The special-zone list calls it a twentieth-century brand (S17).
- How to eat it: As the dipping sauce. The page does not print a dish built on it.
- When to order it: On the table, or as a bottle to take home. A shop door on Cát Bà was not printed.
- Area, not a shop: The story is đảo Cát Hải. It is the neighbor island’s sauce, eaten and bought from Cát Bà on the honey page (S19, S20).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none. It is not a Hải Phòng bún.
- Sources: S17, S19, S20
