## Why a night here

### The picture they expect
- Vietnamese name: Khu phố cổ Hà Nội / Phố cổ
- What they wake up to: a narrow commercial lane of tube-house shopfronts, not the airport and not the lake by itself. Hàng Đào is the spine the management board describes: about 260 m, north–south, from Đông Kinh Nghĩa Thục square at the head of Hàng Gai up to Hàng Ngang (S1). Vietnam Tourism calls the lanes a maze of shopfronts, incense, and street kitchens, and dates those streets to the 14th century (S8). The national tourism index text says the quarter formed in the 11th century (S12). The century is not settled. Both pages are describing the same grid of guild streets, the “36 phố phường.”
- The government portal calls it a national historic-cultural relic and a living museum of Thăng Long’s commercial town (S4). It is not described as a UNESCO site on any page opened for this pass.

### Where they sleep
- Area, not a hotel: inside the grid. No brand.
- Lake end: the south end of Hàng Đào, where the street meets Đông Kinh Nghĩa Thục and Hàng Gai, which is the lake side of this spine (S1). A night here is a short walk to the lake path. The lake itself is the morning and sunset walk beside the quarter, not a substitute for the lanes (S8).
- Deeper lanes: north from Hàng Đào through Hàng Ngang toward the Đồng Xuân market gate. That is the line of the weekend night market (S1). The same grid, further from the lake.
- Which block is quieter: Check on arrival. No page opened for this pass measures noise. S1 only says Hàng Đào is a busy shopping street.
- The indexed boundary (north Hàng Đậu, west Phùng Hưng, south Hàng Bông / Hàng Gai / Cầu Gỗ / Hàng Thùng, east Trần Quang Khải and Trần Nhật Duật, about 100 ha and 76 streets) is S12 only, and that page did not open. Do not print those edges as settled until it does.

### Where the evening is
- Weekend nights, the walk is the Hàng Đào – Hàng Ngang – Đồng Xuân line, from the lake-end square to the market gate (S1, S4).
- Clocks do not agree. S1 says 19:00–23:00 on Friday, Saturday, and Sunday, and dates the night market to 2003. S2, a 2020 city notice, puts that same Hàng Đào – Hàng Giấy – Đồng Xuân line, plus six streets in the level-I area, on “three weekend evenings,” and does not print a clock. The lake space in that notice is the longer one: 19:00 Friday through 24:00 Sunday. S3 is one weekend in March 2025, when the quarter streets were 19:00–24:00 Friday to Sunday and part of Hàng Đào paused its stalls on the Sunday evening. S4 confirms the spaces exist and does not give today’s hours.
- Standing hours: Check on arrival. Treat a weekday night as ordinary traffic. The vehicle-free evening is a weekend notice, and holiday weekends get their own notice (S3).
- The lake-edge walk (Đinh Tiên Hoàng and the streets in the 2020 lake space) is the evening next to the quarter, not a second Old Quarter (S2).

### Scene landmarks
- Hàng Đào night-market walking street. Primary page: S1. It is a street pin, not a bar and not a ranked list. No price on that page. The market sells crafts and Hanoi specialities; stall prices are not copied.
- No restaurant and no bar is a pin in this pass. A beer-street name can wait for the nightlife lane, and only with a primary URL.

### Hero must include
- Both rows of tube-house shopfronts, the lane between them, and enough of the street that a reader can see it is a market street. Subject through the center. Color. Hàng Đào from the street, or the same kind of lane, is the frame. A weekend frame can include the walking crowd if the facades stay readable.
- Not Hoàn Kiếm Lake alone, not the airport, not a military frame, not a document.

### A crop that would fail
- A tight crop of one sign, with no street.
- A lake panorama. That is the lake, not the quarter.
- A person or a statue cut through the chest, or a frame that is mostly sky.
- Night bokeh with no facade. The shopfronts are the subject.

## Attractions (Sight, area of interests, zone, hub, etc.)

A planning walk can use this list. It is not every communal house in the grid. Door hours that the relic page does not print stay `Check on arrival`. A “0h–24h” line under some Hoàn Kiếm 360 articles is a page widget, not a published clock.

### Bach Ma Temple
- Vietnamese name: Đền Bạch Mã
- Kind: sight
- Area: Hàng Buồm, east side of the grid
- What the visitor does: Step into the east guardian temple of the old Thăng Long citadel. The management board places it at 76 Hàng Buồm and calls it one of the Thăng Long tứ trấn, the temple of the spirit Long Đỗ. The compound faces south: tam quan, phương đình, đại bái, thiêu hương, cung cấm, and a meeting house behind. The tượng of the spirit is kept covered. The same board ranks it a national historic and architectural relic in 1986 (S17). The festival page dates the temple’s founding story to 865 and the capital move to 1010 (S21). Those are the board’s own dates, not a second archaeology report.
- Time: A short stop on the walk. Duration — UNSOURCED
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED as a current ticket. Resolution 44/2024 sets 20,000 VND at 87 Mã Mây and 22 Hàng Buồm and does not name Bạch Mã (S14). A November 2025 draft proposed 20,000 VND here. A draft is not a fee (S22).
- From the base: Walk. 76 Hàng Buồm (S17)
- When to go: The relic page does not name an hour. Weekend evenings the level-I streets, including Hàng Buồm, are part of the walking space (S20). The festival is the 12th and 13th of the second lunar month, which moves (S21). That date is the event, not the door hours.
- Mistake to avoid: Treating the four guardian temples as one visit. Voi Phục, Quán Thánh, and Kim Liên are other parts of the city. This dossier does not research them.
- Local interest: Old-quarter temples
- Suggested slug: bach-ma
- Suggested dot: already published — do not change
- Photo must show: the temple gate or the phương đình from the street, in color, one frame, roof included. A tight crop of a carving will lose the building.
- Sources: S17, S21, S14, S22

### Dong Xuan Market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Đồng Xuân
- Kind: sight
- Area: North end of the grid
- What the visitor does: Walk the covered market hall. The management board places it between phố Đồng Xuân (west), Hàng Khoai (north), Cầu Đông (south), and ngõ chợ Đồng Xuân (east). The hall the French built in the 1890s was rebuilt around 1990 as three floors, then burned in 1994 and rebuilt again. Ground floor: clothes, shoes, bags, small electronics, much of it described as goods from China. Floor 2: adult clothes and cloth. Floor 3: baby goods. Food stalls are mostly at chợ Bắc Qua behind the hall, and night kitchens sit on the north side. The same page says most visitors come to look, not to do the real shopping, and that this is a wholesale market (S18).
- Time: Long enough to cross the hall. Duration — UNSOURCED
- Fee: No entrance fee is printed on the management-board page. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- From the base: Walk north through the Hàng Đào – Đồng Xuân line (S1, S18)
- When to go: Door hours are not on S18. Travel blogs that name 06:00–18:00 were not used. The night market that connects the hall to Hàng Đào is the weekend evening in Why a night here (S1, S18).
- Mistake to avoid: Coming for souvenirs and finding wholesale racks. Also, do not treat the 2010 electric visitor car on S18 as still running. That sentence is a launch, not a 2026 timetable.
- Local interest: Market hall
- Suggested slug: dong-xuan
- Suggested dot: already published — do not change
- Photo must show: the market front, the arched roof or the columned gate, and the street in front, one frame. A rack of clothes with no hall does not say where you are.
- Sources: S18, S1

### Ma May Heritage House
- Vietnamese name: Ngôi nhà di sản 87 Mã Mây
- Kind: sight
- Area: Mã Mây, level-I streets
- What the visitor does: Walk a tube house kept as the example of an old Hanoi shophouse. Built about the end of the 19th century, 157.6 m², wood structure and household objects left in place. National heritage certificate 16 Feb 2004. Restored from the end of 1998 to 27 Oct 1999 with Toulouse (S16, S15).
- Time: A short indoor visit. Duration — UNSOURCED
- Fee: 20,000 VND per visitor per visit, from 1 Jan 2025. Free under 16, and for people with especially severe disabilities. Half price for the groups named in the resolution: severe disability, Vietnamese citizens 60 and over with the proof the resolution lists, students 16 and over with a Vietnamese student card, and the cultural-policy groups. Someone who qualifies twice still pays half, not a quarter. Free on 23 November, Vietnam Cultural Heritage Day. The collector is the Hoàn Kiếm Lake and Old Quarter Management Board (S14, S15). Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. S16 describes the e-ticket pilot from 2 Jan 2025 and the official system from 19 Jan 2025, and does not print a clock or the 20,000 figure.
- From the base: Walk. 87 Mã Mây (S16)
- When to go: Morning, before the weekend walking street fills Mã Mây. The street has been in the level-I walking set since 2014 (S20).
- Mistake to avoid: Expecting a museum wing. It is one house.
- Local interest: Old houses
- Suggested slug: ma-may
- Suggested dot: 4 — the house you can walk through on a day that is otherwise streets
- Photo must show: the narrow wooden front on Mã Mây, or the timber interior with the court still readable, one frame. A crop of one carved panel loses the tube house.
- Sources: S14, S15, S16, S20

### Guangdong Assembly Hall
- Vietnamese name: Hội quán Quảng Đông, di tích số 22 Hàng Buồm
- Kind: sight
- Area: Hàng Buồm
- What the visitor does: Walk a Chinese assembly hall on the same street as Bạch Mã. Kinh tế & Đô thị, citing the management board, says it was recognized as a national architectural and artistic relic by Decision 01/2007/QĐ-BVHTTDL on 27 Aug 2007, worships Quan Công and Thiên Hậu, and was built in 1803. Three axes, 1,670 m², including side corridors, a courtyard, tiền đường, phương đình, trung đường, and hậu cung (S15). The government portal describes the same address as a mix of Western concrete masses and East Asian carving, also 1,670 m², and does not repeat the 1803 date (S16).
- Time: A short indoor visit. Duration — UNSOURCED
- Fee: 20,000 VND per visitor per visit, same resolution, same free and half-price cases, free on 23 November, as Mã Mây (S14, S15). Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- From the base: Walk. 22 Hàng Buồm (S16)
- When to go: With Bạch Mã and Mã Mây. They are a few streets apart. Do not invent a combined ticket. S16 says the e-ticket can be bought once and used at linked sites “according to the rules,” and does not say these two doors are one price.
- Mistake to avoid: Calling it a second Bạch Mã. One is the east guardian temple. This is the assembly hall.
- Local interest: Assembly halls
- Suggested slug: hang-buom-22
- Suggested dot: 3 — on the same walk as Bạch Mã, not a separate day
- Photo must show: the hall front at 22 Hàng Buồm, facade and street together. An interior altar with no building is the wrong frame.
- Sources: S14, S15, S16

### Kim Ngan Communal House
- Vietnamese name: Đình Kim Ngân
- Kind: sight
- Area: Hàng Bạc
- What the visitor does: See the communal house of the silver street. The management board places it at 44 Hàng Bạc. The building is traced to the mid-15th century, taken up by Châu Khê silversmiths in the early 19th century, and used as their guild hall. The French name for the street was the money-changing street. Floor area on the page: 575 m². Layout: nghi môn, courtyard, tiền tế, hậu cung, in a công plan. Ranked a national historic and artistic relic on 24 Oct 2012. Households were moved out in 2004 and the hall was restored with Toulouse (S19). The south walking-space note puts performances at 42–44 Hàng Bạc (S20). Use 44 as the door the relic page prints, and treat 42–44 as the performance frontage.
- Time: A short stop. Duration — UNSOURCED
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Not in Resolution 44/2024 (S14). The November 2025 draft proposed 20,000 VND for 42–44 Hàng Bạc. A draft is not a fee (S22).
- From the base: Walk to Hàng Bạc (S19)
- When to go: Daytime for the hall. The relic page does not print hours. On weekend evenings the south walking space includes Hàng Bạc, and the page says relics on that route open while the space is on (S20). Summer on that page: 19:00–24:00 Friday, Saturday, Sunday. Winter: 18:00–24:00. That clock is for the street space, not a proved door hour for the hall.
- Mistake to avoid: Shopping the silver street and missing the hall, which sits behind a shopfront scale of frontage.
- Local interest: Guild streets
- Suggested slug: kim-ngan
- Suggested dot: 3 — the one hall that explains Hàng Bạc
- Photo must show: the communal-house front on Hàng Bạc, gate and street in one frame. A jewelry-shop window is not the hall.
- Sources: S19, S20, S14, S22

### Quan Chuong Gate
- Vietnamese name: Ô Quan Chưởng
- Kind: sight
- Area: East edge, Hàng Chiếu – Thanh Hà
- What the visitor does: Use the old east gate as the landmark of the south walking space. The management board’s 31 Dec 2020 opening note names Ô Quan Chưởng, the street of that name and the Hàng Chiếu – Thanh Hà crossing, as the center for cultural events on that route. Đào Duy Từ from Chợ Gạo to the gate is part of the same space (S20). A dedicated relic page was not found; the guessed URL returned the site home, not a monument entry.
- Time: A few minutes from the street. Duration — UNSOURCED
- Fee: No ticket is printed on S20. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- From the base: Walk to the east edge (S20)
- When to go: Weekend evening if the point is the event space. Daytime the gate is still the street landmark. Architecture, the number of arches, and a restoration date were not on the page that was opened. Do not fill them from a travel blog.
- Mistake to avoid: Describing a gate you have not sourced. The photo can wait for a relic page.
- Local interest: The east gate
- Suggested slug: quan-chuong
- Suggested dot: 3 — a street landmark on the walk, thin until a relic page is opened
- Photo must show: the gate from the street, full arch and both sides of the road, one frame. A crop through the arch that drops the roof fails.
- Sources: S20

### Hang Bac
- Vietnamese name: Phố Hàng Bạc
- Kind: area
- Area: South of the level-I core, on the walking link toward the lake
- What the visitor does: Walk the silver and money-changing street. The guild hall is Đình Kim Ngân at 44 Hàng Bạc (S19). The street is one of the lines in the south walking space (S20). Shops on the street are not picks.
- Time: Part of the walk, not its own hour. Duration — UNSOURCED
- Fee: The street is not ticketed on any page opened. The hall’s fee is Check on arrival (see Kim Ngan).
- From the base: Walk
- When to go: Day for the shops and the hall. Weekend evening for the walking space: Friday–Sunday, 19:00–24:00 in summer and 18:00–24:00 in winter, as printed for this south space (S20). Other pedestrian notices in the base file use different clocks. Check that weekend.
- Mistake to avoid: Giving the street its own page. The place walk covers it. Kim Ngan is the sight.
- Local interest: Guild streets
- Suggested slug: hang-bac
- Suggested dot: do not add a separate record. The place and Kim Ngan carry it.
- Photo must show: the street with silver-shop signs and the hall front if it fits. Not a product macro.
- Sources: S19, S20

### Level-I conservation streets
- Vietnamese name: Khu bảo tồn cấp I
- Kind: zone
- Area: Hàng Buồm, Mã Mây, Đào Duy Từ, Hàng Giấy, Lương Ngọc Quyến, Tạ Hiện
- What the visitor does: Walk the six streets the district added to the weekend walking space in 2014 (S20). Bạch Mã, 22 Hàng Buồm, and 87 Mã Mây sit in this zone. Tạ Hiện’s evening trade is the nightlife lane, not a second sight list.
- Time: The walk itself
- Fee: No zone ticket is printed. Each relic has its own fee or a Check on arrival.
- From the base: You are already in it if the night is in the grid
- When to go: Day for the relics. Weekend evening for the walking space. The 2014 note does not print a separate clock for these six streets. The south-space clock above is a later, different route (S20).
- Mistake to avoid: Publishing six street pages. This zone is the place.
- Local interest: The old grid
- Suggested slug: conservation-core
- Suggested dot: do not add a separate record
- Photo must show: one of these streets with both shopfronts and the lane, the same hero rule as Why a night here
- Sources: S20
