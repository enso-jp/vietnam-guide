# Research dossier — Old Quarter

## Why a night here

### The picture they expect
- Vietnamese name: Khu phố cổ Hà Nội / Phố cổ
- What they wake up to: a narrow commercial lane of tube-house shopfronts, not the airport and not the lake by itself. Hàng Đào is the spine the management board describes: about 260 m, north–south, from Đông Kinh Nghĩa Thục square at the head of Hàng Gai up to Hàng Ngang (S1). Vietnam Tourism calls the lanes a maze of shopfronts, incense, and street kitchens, and dates those streets to the 14th century (S8). The national tourism index text says the quarter formed in the 11th century (S12). The century is not settled. Both pages are describing the same grid of guild streets, the “36 phố phường.”
- The government portal calls it a national historic-cultural relic and a living museum of Thăng Long’s commercial town (S4). It is not described as a UNESCO site on any page opened for this pass.

### Where they sleep
- Area, not a hotel: inside the grid. No brand.
- Lake end: the south end of Hàng Đào, where the street meets Đông Kinh Nghĩa Thục and Hàng Gai, which is the lake side of this spine (S1). A night here is a short walk to the lake path. The lake itself is the morning and sunset walk beside the quarter, not a substitute for the lanes (S8).
- Deeper lanes: north from Hàng Đào through Hàng Ngang toward the Đồng Xuân market gate. That is the line of the weekend night market (S1). The same grid, further from the lake.
- Which block is quieter: Check on arrival. No page opened for this pass measures noise. S1 only says Hàng Đào is a busy shopping street.
- The indexed boundary (north Hàng Đậu, west Phùng Hưng, south Hàng Bông / Hàng Gai / Cầu Gỗ / Hàng Thùng, east Trần Quang Khải and Trần Nhật Duật, about 100 ha and 76 streets) is S12 only, and that page did not open. Do not print those edges as settled until it does.

### Where the evening is
- Weekend nights, the walk is the Hàng Đào – Hàng Ngang – Đồng Xuân line, from the lake-end square to the market gate (S1, S4).
- Clocks do not agree. S1 says 19:00–23:00 on Friday, Saturday, and Sunday, and dates the night market to 2003. S2, a 2020 city notice, puts that same Hàng Đào – Hàng Giấy – Đồng Xuân line, plus six streets in the level-I area, on “three weekend evenings,” and does not print a clock. The lake space in that notice is the longer one: 19:00 Friday through 24:00 Sunday. S3 is one weekend in March 2025, when the quarter streets were 19:00–24:00 Friday to Sunday and part of Hàng Đào paused its stalls on the Sunday evening. S4 confirms the spaces exist and does not give today’s hours.
- Standing hours: Check on arrival. Treat a weekday night as ordinary traffic. The vehicle-free evening is a weekend notice, and holiday weekends get their own notice (S3).
- The lake-edge walk (Đinh Tiên Hoàng and the streets in the 2020 lake space) is the evening next to the quarter, not a second Old Quarter (S2).

### Scene landmarks
- Hàng Đào night-market walking street. Primary page: S1. It is a street pin, not a bar and not a ranked list. No price on that page. The market sells crafts and Hanoi specialities; stall prices are not copied.
- No restaurant and no bar is a pin in this pass. A beer-street name can wait for the nightlife lane, and only with a primary URL.

### Hero must include
- Both rows of tube-house shopfronts, the lane between them, and enough of the street that a reader can see it is a market street. Subject through the center. Color. Hàng Đào from the street, or the same kind of lane, is the frame. A weekend frame can include the walking crowd if the facades stay readable.
- Not Hoàn Kiếm Lake alone, not the airport, not a military frame, not a document.

### A crop that would fail
- A tight crop of one sign, with no street.
- A lake panorama. That is the lake, not the quarter.
- A person or a statue cut through the chest, or a frame that is mostly sky.
- Night bokeh with no facade. The shopfronts are the subject.
