# getting there

Checked: 21 Oct 2026

The Old Quarter is a district inside Hanoi. The night is in the lanes. The airport and the station serve the city, then a walk or a short ride finishes the trip. Durations below are the figures the cited page actually prints. Where two pages disagree, both figures are kept.

- Nearest airport: Nội Bài International. IATA HAN (S7). ICAO VVNB (S5). The AIP places it 28 km north of Hanoi city (S5). Vietnam Tourism’s airport guide says about 27 km and about an hour to downtown (S7). The same publisher’s Hanoi place page says about 45 km (S8). Use the AIP for the aerodrome distance, and do not collapse 27, 28, and 45 into one number. Road time: the only sourced figure is “about an hour” (S7). Treat that as approximate. The AIP and the airport bus page do not print a drive time.
- Terminals: T1 domestic, T2 international, 850 m apart. Free inter-terminal shuttle every 10–15 minutes from 06:00 to 01:00, T2 floor 1 on the west side to T1 departures floor 2 (S7).
- Nearest rail station: Ga Hà Nội, 120 Lê Duẩn, Hoàn Kiếm, Hanoi (S10). It is the city station, south of the lake. It is not a door inside the lanes. Walk time from the station to Hàng Đào: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. No page opened for this pass gives minutes or a step count.

- Getting around on site: Walk. Hàng Đào itself is about 260 m (S1). The lanes are the base. On a weekend evening the walking-street notices take vehicles off the named streets; the clock is Check on arrival because S1, S2, and S3 do not say the same thing. On other days the tourism board’s picture of the quarter is motorbikes and sidewalk kitchens in the same lanes (S8). A bicycle is possible. A ride-hailing car is for the airport, the station, and leaving the grid, not for hopping one street. No metro station inside the quarter was confirmed on a page opened this pass. Do not send someone to a metro door that was not sourced.
- Season limit on the journey: Hanoi, not a mountain pass. Vietnam Tourism’s weather page: December–March cool to cold and misty, lows of 10 degrees from late December to March; April–June warm to hot; June–August hot, humid, and rainy, with June described as feeling like 40 degrees; September–November cooler, about 25 degrees, and sunny (S9). The place page’s “best time” is September–November, plus May for the flame trees (S8). The weather page’s best time is April–June or September–December (S9). None of these pages close the Old Quarter lanes. They change the walk: a cold drizzle, or a heavy summer shower. Check the weekend pedestrian notice if the trip falls on a holiday (S3).
