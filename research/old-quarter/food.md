## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Pho
- Vietnamese name: Phở
- What it is: Beef or chicken noodle soup. Vietnam Tourism’s pho story says Nam Định is the geographical cradle and Hanoi is the spiritual home. By the 1930s, shoulder-pole pho kitchens were a common sight in the Old Quarter. Northern garnishes on that page are fresh chilli, lemon, and a few herbs. The south’s bowl is sweeter and carries more herbs, hoisin, and chilli sauce (S24). The “10 dishes” page tells a visitor to start with chilli sauce, then garlic vinegar for beef pho or lime for chicken pho (S23). Those two pages do not describe the same garnish plate. Do not collapse them.
- How to eat it: The cook assembles the bowl. Taste it before adding anything. The story’s opening also mentions basil and hoisin on a general bowl, then limits the northern plate later in the same article (S24). Follow the table you are at.
- When to order it: Breakfast. The story calls it Hanoi’s staple breakfast, with stalls up from the early hours (S24). The Hanoi place page puts steaming pho on Old Quarter corners (S8).
- Area, not a shop: Sidewalks in the quarter. One tourism-board example sits on Hàng Vải. The shop name is in Notes, not pages.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: the southern bowl, already on the shared pho record
- Sources: S24, S23, S8

### Bun cha
- Vietnamese name: Bún chả
- What it is: Chargrilled pork belly and pork patties with rice noodles, herbs, and a diluted vinegar fish sauce. The culture department describes pork minced or sliced, marinated with lemongrass, grilled over charcoal, eaten with noodles, fish sauce, and herbs (S25). Vietnam Tourism calls it almost exclusively lunch, and says fried spring rolls (nem) are the hearty addition (S23). The Hanoi place page names bún chả in the Old Quarter street kitchens (S8).
- How to eat it: Put noodles into the sauce bowl, add herbs, and take a bite with some of everything (S23).
- When to order it: Lunch (S23)
- Area, not a shop: Old Quarter kitchens (S8). The tourism board’s example address is on Cửa Đông, the west edge. Shop name in Notes.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this pass
- Sources: S23, S25, S8

### Vietnamese coffee
- Vietnamese name: Cà phê, and cà phê trứng for the egg cup
- What it is: The bundled dish is the phin coffee. The Hanoi cup on the tourism board is cà phê trứng: strong robusta under a sweet egg foam (S23). The culture department describes coffee mixed with the richness of egg and the sweetness of condensed milk, and does not name a shop (S25).
- How to eat it: As a sit-down cup, not a walking snack. The dishes page pictures it hot, under trees, watching the street (S23).
- When to order it: The dishes page puts it at the end of a meal (S23). No page opened here gives a single hour. Check on arrival for whether a given cafe serves it in the morning.
- Area, not a shop: The tourism board’s example is an alley off Nguyễn Hữu Huân. That street was not proved inside the quarter on this pass. The shop name is in Notes, not a pick, and it is not a scene landmark in Why a night here.
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: the highland cup and the Da Nang river cup already on the shared coffee record
- Sources: S23, S25

### Bun dau
- Vietnamese name: Bún đậu
- What it is: Fried tofu with fresh rice noodles. Vietnam Tourism says the original dip is mắm tôm, with fish sauce as the lighter choice, plus rice patties, fried spring rolls, and herbs (S23).
- How to eat it: Noodles and tofu with the dip the table chooses. Mắm tôm is the stronger one (S23).
- When to order it: The page calls it a Hanoi lunch (S23)
- Area, not a shop: The example address is on Mã Mây, inside the level-I streets. Shop name in Notes.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S23

### Bun ca
- Vietnamese name: Bún cá
- What it is: Fish noodle soup. Vietnam Tourism: rice noodles, Vietnamese celery, and fried tilapia in a sweet-sour broth of pineapple and tomato, with herbs and pickled bamboo (S23).
- How to eat it: As a bowl, herbs on the side (S23)
- When to order it: The page calls it a summer bowl. No clock.
- Area, not a shop: The example is an alley off Hàng Bạc (ngõ Trung Yên). Shop name in Notes.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S23

### Bun thang
- Vietnamese name: Bún thang
- What it is: A clear broth with Vietnamese ham, shredded chicken, and omelette strips. Vietnam Tourism calls it a breakfast made from leftover Tết food (S23).
- How to eat it: As a bowl. The page does not give a garnish ritual.
- When to order it: Breakfast, and the Tết association means the full version is a seasonal memory, not a proved year-round menu (S23)
- Area, not a shop: The example is on Cầu Gỗ, the southern edge. Shop name in Notes.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S23

### Mien
- Vietnamese name: Miến
- What it is: Mung-bean or sweet-potato vermicelli, with chicken, crab, or eel, in broth or stir-fried. Quẩy is dipped in the broth (S23).
- How to eat it: With the fried breadstick in the broth if it is the soup (S23)
- When to order it: Not stated. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: The example is on Phùng Hưng, the west boundary street. Shop name in Notes. Treat the street as the edge, not the middle of the grid.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S23

Neighbor dishes, not Old Quarter streets on a page opened here: chả cá, with the tourism board’s example on Đường Thành (S23), and the culture department’s description of grilled fish with dill, spring onion, and mắm tôm (S25). Bánh tôm is placed on the West Lake road, not in the quarter (S25). Bánh cuốn, bún riêu, bánh mì, phở cuốn, and xôi xéo are on the culture department’s Hanoi list with no Old Quarter street (S25). Copy may link them. They are not rows on this board.

## Food streets and markets

### Bac Qua and the north side of Dong Xuan
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Bắc Qua
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eat at the kitchens the management board places behind Đồng Xuân, at chợ Bắc Qua, and at the night kitchens on the north side of the hall. The hall itself is the sight. Food stalls are mostly not inside the three wholesale floors (S18).
- When to go: Door hours for the kitchens are not on S18. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The night kitchens are the evening side of the same note.
- Area: North end of the quarter, against the market
- Suggested slug: bac-qua
- Photo must show: the kitchen street behind or beside the market hall, stools and the hall in one frame. A plate with no street fails.
- Sources: S18

### Hang Dao weekend night market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ đêm Hàng Đào – Hàng Ngang – Đồng Xuân
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Walk the weekend line from Hàng Đào to the Đồng Xuân gate. The management board says the night market sells crafts and some Hanoi specialities (S1). It is the same pin as Why a night here, not a second restaurant list. What is food and what is a souvenir stall is not separated on that page.
- When to go: S1 says 19:00–23:00 Friday, Saturday, and Sunday. Other notices disagree. See Why a night here. Check on arrival.
- Area: The spine of the quarter
- Suggested slug: hang-dao-night-market
- Photo must show: the walking street at night with stalls and both shopfronts, subject through the center
- Sources: S1
