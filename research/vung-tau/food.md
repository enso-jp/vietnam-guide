# Food — Vung Tau

Slug: `vung-tau`
Checked: 21 Oct 2026. Widened: 23 Oct 2026.

Vietnam Tourism still prints no stall price. Vietnam Airlines (S21), opened 23 Oct 2026, prints reference ranges for the dishes it lists. Both stay. Where two accounts describe the same dish differently, both lines are kept. Shop names stay under Notes. A specialty is a dish. Streets are not dishes.

S2 and S3 were opened again on 23 Oct 2026. The provincial food article at ittpa.baria-vungtau.gov.vn did not resolve. Dishes known only from a search snippet of that article stay out.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Banh khot
- Vietnamese name: Bánh khọt
- What it is: The street snack Vietnam Tourism calls the one a Vung Tau trip is incomplete without (S3). S3: rice-flour batter fried in small cast-iron molds until crisp, topped with shrimp, scallions, and scallion oil, wrapped with herbs, dipped in sweet-sour fish sauce. S1: a mini pancake of flour and curry powder, topped with squid or shrimp, green onion, and shrimp powder, wrapped in lettuce and herbs, dipped in fish sauce with papaya and chili. The batter ingredients disagree. Do not collapse them.
- How to eat it: One cake at a time, herbs around it, sauce on the side (S1, S3)
- When to order it: The shop Vietnam Tourism names at 14 Nguyen Truong To opens 7:00–14:00 on weekdays and 7:00–20:00 on Sundays (S1). That shop is a location clue in Notes, not a pick.
- Area, not a shop: Nguyen Truong To (S1). Hoang Hoa Tham also has bánh khọt addresses; see that street.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: Do not link the shared `banh-xeo` page. This is the small molded cake, not a folded crepe.
- Sources: S1, S3, S21
- S21 adds: crisp outside and soft inside, a rice-flour batter it calls rich, shrimp or squid, scallion and scallion oil, greens, and sweet-sour fish sauce with mild chili. Reference price 40,000–70,000 VND. It names the same two kitchens already in Notes. S1’s curry powder stays.

### Stingray hotpot
- Vietnamese name: Lẩu cá đuối
- What it is: A shared pot. S1 says stingray and baby bamboo shoots. S3 says a tamarind and pineapple broth with herbs, eaten with morning glory and bean sprouts. Both are recorded.
- How to eat it: From the pot at the table, with the greens (S3)
- When to order it: A sit-down meal, not a walking snack (S3)
- Area, not a shop: Vietnam Tourism does not name a street for the pot (S1, S3). Check on arrival — UNSOURCED for the lane.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S3, S21
- S21 adds: sour-sweet broth, natural sweetness of fresh stingray, meat it calls soft and free of small bones, greens and bún, and the smell of bamboo shoots and herbs. Reference price 150,000–250,000 VND. Streets it names: Trần Hưng Đạo, Trần Phú, Hùng Vương. Shop names stay in Notes. S1’s baby bamboo shoots and S3’s tamarind and pineapple both stay.

- How to eat it: In a bowl, lime at the table (S3)
- When to order it: A meal. Hour not printed.
- Area, not a shop: Not named (S3). Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: do not edit the Da Nang `banh-canh` page. This bowl is the crab one. File it as `banh-canh-ghe`.
- Sources: S3, S21
- S21 adds: firm sweet crab meat, a lightly thick seafood broth, soft chewy noodles, sweetness it ties to bones and crab, pepper, scallion, and greens. It does not mention the coconut milk on S3. Keep the coconut milk. Reference price 60,000–120,000 VND. Streets: Tú Xương, Võ Thị Sáu, Lê Lai. One caption on S21 says cua while the heading says ghẹ. Keep both words. Shop names stay in Notes.

## Food streets and markets

### Nguyen Truong To
- Vietnamese name: Nguyễn Trường Tộ
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Comes for bánh khọt. Vietnam Tourism names one shop at number 14 and prints its hours, 7:00–14:00 on weekdays and 7:00–20:00 on Sundays (S1). The street is the destination. The shop is not a pick.
- When to go: Those hours if you want that kitchen (S1). Other kitchens on the street were not surveyed.
- Area: Near the Back Beach side of town, at the address printed on S1
- Suggested slug: nguyen-truong-to
- Photo must show: The molds and the herbs on a street table, not a shop logo
- Sources: S1

### Hoang Hoa Tham
- Vietnamese name: Hoàng Hoa Thám
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats along the street that also holds the whale-hall procession and the ranked communal house at number 104 (S6, S14). A bánh khọt operator’s own site prints a kitchen at 1 Hoang Hoa Tham, open 8:00–21:30 (S19). That name stays in Notes.
- When to go: Daytime into the evening for the kitchens (S19). Festival mornings are the procession, not a quiet meal (S14).
- Area: The inland leg between Front Beach and the whale hall (S14)
- Suggested slug: hoang-hoa-tham
- Photo must show: Tables along the street, with the food in frame
- Sources: S6, S14, S19

### Back Beach night seafood market
- Vietnamese name: Not printed. Vietnam Tourism says the night seafood market on Back Beach (S2).
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Picks fish, crab, or shellfish and a cooking method, and eats it there (S2)
- When to go: After dark (S2). A clock time was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area: Back Beach. A street number was not on the page.
- Suggested slug: back-beach-seafood
- Photo must show: The night market, the catch, and the beach lights in one frame
- Sources: S2

The central market’s street address was left out. Two directories disagreed, and no provincial market page was opened.
