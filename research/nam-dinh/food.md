## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Pho bo
- Vietnamese name: Phở bò Nam Định
- What it is: Beef pho. Dân Việt says the noodle is especially small, thin, and soft, and that the broth stays clear because it is seasoned with traditional fish sauce rather than a harsh one (S18). The national tourism snapshot says the Nam Định bowl is a variation on beef pho, against a more varied Hanoi bowl (S19). That snapshot later returned HTTP 500, so only the retrieved opening is used. Dân trí describes áp chảo (beef seared with greens and tomato) and sốt vang (a larger cut) at one shop, and a broth of pork and beef bones (S17).
- How to eat it: In the morning, at a street table, with lime or kumquat and fresh chili if they are on the table. Dân trí says one famous shop closes at 09:00 (S17). Dân Việt says a bowl can be found at any hour (S18). Both stay. Do not pick one clock.
- When to order it: Breakfast (S18). The any-hour sentence is the conflict, not a second meal rule.
- Area, not a shop: Phố Hàng Tiện, around Chợ Rồng, and the center (S17, S18). The Nam Trực page title calls làng Vân Cù and Giao Cù the cradle of the trade (S26). The body did not open. No village method is taken from it.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: Hanoi pho, only so copy can say the noodle and the broth are described differently. Do not paste a Hanoi shop.
- Sources: S17, S18, S19, S26

### Banh cuon lang Kenh
- Vietnamese name: Bánh cuốn làng Kênh
- What it is: Rice sheets, thin and slightly elastic, with chopped wood-ear, fried shallot, a slice of chả, and a fish-sauce dip with lime or kumquat (S18).
- How to eat it: Off a banana leaf, at a small pavement set. The paper says the sheets are often carried in a basket, already steamed.
- When to order it: Morning, as the other breakfast after pho (S18).
- Area, not a shop: Phường Lộc Vượng (S18). A street number was not the point of the paragraph.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none
