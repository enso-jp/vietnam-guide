## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Hu tieu My Tho
- Vietnamese name: Hủ tiếu Mỹ Tho
- What it is: A rice-noodle bowl. The national tourism page says hủ tiếu came from Chinese cooking and was changed in Mỹ Tho, and that it is not the same as hủ tiếu Tàu, hủ tiếu Nam Vang, phở, or bún bò Huế. It prints the local way as fresh bean sprouts, lime, chili, and soy, and says the noodle is not eaten with lettuce, vinegar, and herb plates (S31). The old phường 9 page says a correct bowl has a lot of minced pork, and that the dry style is strands tossed with pork crackling, fried shallot, and soy, then topped with cooked minced pork. The soup style adds sliced pork, liver, intestines, shrimp, squid, and quail egg, then the broth, spring onion, and more crackling. The table holds soy, lime, sugar, fresh chili or satay, mixed by the guest (S30). The provincial tourism article says the strand is dry bánh made from local fragrant rice, names Gò Cát, Nàng Hương, and Nàng Út, and says a good strand smells of rice, stays soft without going mushy, and is not sour. Broth in that article is pork leg bone, grilled dried squid, cassava root, and spices, kept at a simmer (S16). The ward page’s broth list is bone, dried squid, dried shrimp, and daikon (S30). Record both broth lists.
- How to eat it: Dry, or with the broth poured over. The ward page says the strands are blanched briefly. Too long and they go soft (S30).
- When to order it: Breakfast, lunch, or supper. The ward page says all three (S30).
- Area, not a shop: Town streets, and the pier food court on 30 Tháng 4 (S22, S30). The workshop is ấp Hội Gia, phường Mỹ Phong, which is where you watch the strand made, not where the bowl is served (S6).
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: The ward page names hủ tiếu Sa Đéc and hủ tiếu Nam Vang as the other famous bowls, not as Mỹ Tho (S30). No page slug exists for them yet. Do not link `pho` or `banh-xeo` as this dish. The site slug `che` is not a Mỹ Tho recipe.
- History lines that disagree, so both stay: The provincial tourism article says the bowl appears at the start of the 1960s, from carts between Mỹ Tho and Gò Công, and that in 2014 it was recognized as a dish of Asian culinary value (S16). The national page also starts the fame in the 1960s and names the cooks Phánh Ký, Tuyền Ký, and Nam Sơn (S31). The city portal says the dish has “a hundred years” of cultural value, that a cooperative and the village formed in 2007, and that in December 2008 the Intellectual Property Office granted an exclusive certificate for the brand “Hủ tiếu Mỹ Tho” (S27). The ward page adds a “top 100” Asian list, a “top 10” Vietnamese list, and a WTO gold-quality line for the strand (S30). The provincial article does not print those lists. Do not choose a ranking.
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S6, S16, S22, S27, S30, S31

### Elephant-ear fish
- Vietnamese name: Cá tai tượng chiên
- What it is: TITC lists deep-fried elephant-ear fish among the islet dishes, with grilled shrimp, linh-fish hotpot with wild flowers, grilled snakehead, mussel pancakes, and pomelo salad (S8).
- How to eat it: As a countryside plate on the islet, with the fruit and the music (S8). A recipe was not printed.
- When to order it: At the orchard stop, midday
- Area, not a shop: Cù lao Thới Sơn
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S8

### Ginger honey tea
- Vietnamese name: Trà mật ong gừng
- What it is: TITC says Thới Sơn honey is an OCOP product and that guests drink ginger honey tea in the orchard while the music plays (S8). A Zing account says the hives on Lân and Phụng use longan blossom (S26).
- How to eat it: A cup at the garden, not a bottled souvenir list
- When to order it: With the morning orchard stop
- Area, not a shop: Cù lao Thới Sơn. Honey on Cồn Phụng is the Ben Tre side of the same river day (S9, S26).
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none. The site slug `ca-phe` is not this drink.
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S8, S9, S26

### Coconut candy
- Vietnamese name: Kẹo dừa
- What it is: TITC names handmade coconut candy with the honey as an OCOP product on Thới Sơn (S8). The national tourism article on Cồn Phụng says coconut candy and objects made from shell, husk, and leaf are the craft of that Ben Tre islet (S9).
- How to eat it: A piece at the garden. The Phụng article is about the craft, not a Mỹ Tho recipe.
- When to order it: On the islet stop
- Area, not a shop: Thới Sơn for the OCOP line (S8). The Ben Tre craft is a side trip, not a second place.
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none until a Ben Tre page exists
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S8, S9

## Food streets and markets

### Pier food court
- Vietnamese name: Khu ẩm thực đêm Mỹ Tho
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats at the 3,000 m² court the province placed inside the tourist-boat pier, beside the Tiền. The printed menu is hủ tiếu, bún, phở, cháo, bánh cuốn, nghêu, sò, dried seafood, sâm bổ lượng, chè, and sinh tố (S22).
- When to go: 18:30–02:00 on the 5 Jan 2019 article (S22). Verified as that article’s text on 21 Oct 2026. A 2026 confirmation was not opened. A travel page prints a night market at số 8 đường 30/4, from 18:00, busiest 19:00–22:00 (S28). Record both.
- Area: Đường 30 Tháng 4, old phường 1, now phường Mỹ Tho (S11, S22)
- Suggested slug: `pier-food-court`
- Photo must show: Stalls, the river or the pier, and people eating, in one night frame
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S11, S22, S28
