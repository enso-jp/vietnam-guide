# Getting there — Ba Be

Checked: 21 Oct 2026

The usual previous stop is Hanoi. The lake is the night. Cao Bang is a different road and a different place file.

- Nearest airport: Nội Bài. Lao Động, citing the Thai Nguyen portal, puts the lake about 220 km from that airport (S19). VnExpress tells southern travelers to fly to Hanoi and then take the road (S18). No airfield at the lake is named on the park pages or on the UNESCO summary (S1, S12).
- Nearest rail station: None named. The park pages, the UNESCO summary, and the VnExpress road note describe road and bus only (S1, S12, S18). This pass did not read a Vietnam Railways timetable. Do not publish a station name.
- From the usual previous stop:
  - Hanoi to the park | The about page says 250 km northeast of Hanoi (S1). The locations index says about 240 km northeast (S2). UNESCO says 250 km north of Hanoi, and 68 km northwest of Bac Kan city (S12). VnExpress says Bac Kan city is 165 km from Hanoi, then the lake is a further leg (S18). Record all of these. A single door-to-door kilometre — Check on arrival.
  - Hanoi to Bac Kan city, then the lake | VnExpress: old National Highway 3, about 3 hours, and motorbikes can use it; or the Hanoi–Thai Nguyen expressway and then National Highway 3, about 2 hours, cars only (S18). Those hours stop at the city, not the lake. From the city, provincial road 256 is about 60 km, or provincial road 257 via Chợ Đồn is about 70 km (S18). UNESCO’s city-to-park figure is 68 km (S12). Minutes on the last leg — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
  - Bus | VnExpress says coaches leave My Dinh in Hanoi on Hanoi–Bac Kan and Hanoi–Cao Bang lines, and that those lines often pass the lake (S18). It does not print a yard timetable. Operator clocks and fares — Check on arrival. Left out of this dossier.
  - From Nội Bài | About 220 km on the Thai Nguyen portal as quoted by Lao Động (S19). Minutes — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
  - Cho Ra | The park’s north edge is xã Chợ Rã (S1). The national tourism note puts Puông 5 km from thị trấn Chợ Rã (S20). That is the cave, not a measured walk from the market to Pác Ngòi. A last-mile motorbike from the park gate into the village is what VnExpress describes (S18). Gate-to-house minutes — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Getting around on site: The boat is the way across the water. The park sends boats from bến Buốc Lốm, about 1 km down the Năng to Puông (S3). VnExpress names two starts, the lake and the Năng, and prints 12 seats for a domestic group and 10 for an international group on a motorboat (S18). No park capacity notice was found, so the seat count — Check on arrival. A dugout is the older Tay boat: VnExpress says one tree, about 50 cm wide, 5–10 m long, one paddle, often 2–3 people (S18). On land, the village is a walk. The district is investing in a Bac Kan–Ba Be–Na Hang road and in an upgrade of the road around the lake (S14). Whether either is open as a bus loop — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Season limit on the journey: The park describes two seasons, a northeast monsoon, a year-round mean of 22°C, about 1,378 mm of rain, and humidity above 80 percent (S1). It does not print a road-closure calendar. VnExpress calls April–October the rainy season and November–February the dry season, says some places fall to 0°C with ice, and calls February–April the pleasant window at 25–30°C (S18). That pleasant range and the freezing claim sit beside the park’s 22°C mean. Print both. March and April, after the first rains, are when the park says fish run Đầu Đẳng (S4). Mountain roads in a storm — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
