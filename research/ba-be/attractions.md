# Research dossier — Ba Be

Slug: `ba-be`
Province: Thái Nguyên (Thái Nguyên). National Assembly Resolution 202/2025/QH15 merged the whole of Bắc Kạn into the new Thái Nguyên. The new province is 8,375.21 km² with a listed population of 1,799,489, and it borders Bắc Ninh, Cao Bằng, Lạng Sơn, Phú Thọ, Tuyên Quang, and Hà Nội. The resolution passed on 12 Jun 2025. New provincial governments started work on 1 Jul 2025 (S11). The park’s own about page says management moved to the Thái Nguyên People’s Committee after that merger (S1).
Kind: area
Corridor: northern-midlands
Base a traveler sleeps in: Stilt-house homestays in Pác Ngòi and Bó Lù, on the lake. Older pages still say xã Nam Mẫu. The 2026 festival report says xã Ba Bể. Not a hotel brand. Chợ Rã is the road town on the north edge, not the picture.
Checked: 21 Oct 2026

The park was under the Cao Bằng People’s Committee from 1992 to 1997, then the agriculture ministry from 1997 to 2003, then Bắc Kạn from 2003 until 1 Jul 2025 (S1). That early window is why the lake is sometimes hung on Cao Bằng. It is not part of the `cao-bang` page. Cao Bằng was not merged (S11).

## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: Hồ Ba Bể in the morning, green water between limestone, forest on the walls, a boat or a line of stilt houses. Not a bus yard and not a provincial square (S1, S2, S13).
- Where they sleep, as an area: Tay stilt houses in Pác Ngòi, on the Lèng at the lake, and in Bó Lù, where the festival ground is (S13, S14, S15). The district page counted nearly 60 households in Nam Mẫu registered as homestays (S14). VnExpress says a room that looks at the lake is one of those houses, and that fuller facilities sit 1–2 km off the shore at the park gate (S18). No hotel brand as a pick.
- Where the evening is: The stilt-house floor in Pác Ngòi. VOV describes then singing, đàn tính, and rượu men lá after the meal (S13). A bar street was not found. A weekend night market at Pác Ngòi was reported once, in September 2019, as a first trial (S21).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): The lake from the shore (S2); the mouth of Puông, about 1 km down the Năng from bến Buốc Lốm (S3); the stilt line at Pác Ngòi (S13); đảo An Mạ, the temple island about 30 m out of the water (S2); Thác Đầu Đẳng, about 3 km west of the lake on the park’s location page (S4).
- Hero must include: the water, the limestone on at least one side, and a boat or the stilt roofs, in color, subject through the center. Morning mist can stay if the shore is still readable.
- A crop that would fail: fog with no shore; a slice of water with no cliff; a portrait of one house that loses the lake; a bat close-up; a fish on a plate with no landscape.

## Attractions (Sight, area of interests, zone, hub, etc.)

### Ba Be National Park
- Vietnamese name: Vườn quốc gia Ba Bể
- Kind: zone
- Area: Xã Ba Bể, tỉnh Thái Nguyên, on the current park pages (S1, S2). UNESCO’s 2017 summary still says five communes in Ba Be district, Bac Kan: Nam Mẫu, Khang Ninh, Cao Thượng, Quảng Khê, Cao Trĩ (S12). The about page says the north edge is xã Chợ Rã, the south xã Nam Cường, the east xã Đồng Phúc, and the west xã Na Hang in Tuyên Quang (S1)
- What the visitor does: Uses the park as the whole trip: the lake, the Năng, the caves, the fall, and the villages. It is not a museum with one gate. Decision 83/TTg of 10 Nov 1992 established it from the former forbidden forest of the lake (S1, S10). A 1995 freshwater-lakes conference in the United States named the lake one of 20 special freshwater lakes to protect (S1). The locations index phrases that as one of the 20 largest natural freshwater lakes in the world (S2). Keep the conference wording. ASEAN recognition is 2003 on the park about page and 2004 on the biodiversity desk and on UNESCO (S1, S10, S12). Ramsar designation is 2 Feb 2011 on the park page; UNESCO calls it site 1938, the third Ramsar site in Viet Nam (S1, S12). The rsis.ramsar.org page did not load. In 2012 the Prime Minister ranked the lake a special national scenic site, Decision 1419/QĐ-TTg (S1). UNESCO’s tentative list, submitted 31 May 2017, is “Ba Be – Na Hang”, not an inscription. A 2006 nomination was deferred in 2007 (S12)
- Time: Two nights if the boat day and one walk are both the point — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED as a required minimum
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. No park tariff was on the pages read. Operator sheets disagree with each other and were not adopted
- From the base: The base is inside the park
- When to go: Year round, with the climate split in `getting-there.md`. March and April are when the park says fish run the fall (S4). Long Tong is the 10th of the first lunar month (S9, S16)
- Mistake to avoid: Treating the lake as a section of Cao Bang, or as a day trip that starts and ends in Hanoi
- Local interest: The lake morning; limestone forest
- Suggested slug: `ba-be-national-park`
- Suggested dot: 5 — the frame of the trip, not a single ticket window
- Photo must show: karst, forest, and water in one frame. A logo or a gate sign fails
- Sources: S1, S2, S10, S11, S12

Area figures, print both. Both desks say 10,048 ha. The park about page, noting “theo đơn vị hành chính cũ”, splits that into strict protection 4,010.70 ha, ecological restoration 5,375.60 ha, admin-service 57.40 ha, and an inner buffer of 604.0 ha, plus an outer buffer of 25,309 ha (S1). The biodiversity desk, in a page first dated 31 Dec 2023, splits the same 10,048 ha into 3,846 ha strict, 6,162 ha restoration, and 40 ha admin (S10).

Species figures, print both. The park’s 2012 survey counted 909 terrestrial plant species, 316 terrestrial vertebrates (40 mammals, 233 birds, 27 reptiles), 16 amphibians, and about 107 fish from the 2006 nomination dossier (S1). The biodiversity desk counts 1,268 higher plants, 182 orchids, 470 vertebrates, and 106 fish, of which 20 have not been seen since the 1970s (S10). UNESCO’s narrative says “over 1,800 living species” in a mixed flora-and-fauna sentence (S12). Do not add these into one total.

### Ba Be Lake
- Vietnamese name: Hồ Ba Bể. Tay: Slam Pe, the widenings Pé Lèng, Pé Lù, and Pé Lầm (also written Pe Lam, Pe Lu, Pe Leng)
- Kind: sight
- Area: Centre of the park. The biodiversity desk says the water is one lake running nearly 8 km north–south, with many small inlets, not three separate lakes (S10). The park about page still says three lakes joined (S1)
- What the visitor does: Rides a boat. The usual circuit is the open water, An Mạ, Ao Tiên, Puông, and Đầu Đẳng, in combinations the boatman sells. VnExpress names two piers, the lake and the Năng (S18). The park names bến Buốc Lốm for the cave (S3). Islands the about page names: Bà Góa and An Mã (S1)
- Time: A long morning to most of a day for the cave and the fall together. VnExpress speaks of trips of 2–8 hours (S18). A park duration was not found — Check on arrival
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED as a park price. See the boat activity in `events.md` for a news-desk range that is not a tariff
- From the base: Walk to the village landing, or ride from Buốc Lốm if the night was not in Pác Ngòi (S3, S18)
- When to go: Morning, for still water and the mist VOV describes (S13). Fish running the fall is March and April (S4)
- Mistake to avoid: Budgeting the cave, the fall, Hua Mạ, and a trek as one hour. Also swimming in Ao Tiên, which the park post forbids (S8)
- Local interest: The lake morning
- Suggested slug: `ba-be-lake`
- Suggested dot: 5 — the reason for the night
- Photo must show: the water through the center, limestone along at least one side, and a boat or stilt roofs. A crop of empty mist, or of sky, fails
- Sources: S1, S2, S3, S10, S12, S18

Lake measurements, print every desk. Do not average them.

- Park about page: 150 m above sea level, 7.5 km long, 0.2–1 km wide, 450 ha, depth 17–23 m, deepest 29 m, annual level change 2.8 m, about 90 million m³, formed about 10,000 years ago (S1)
- Biodiversity desk: about 178 m, length to 8 km, width 500 m, surface 300–500 ha by season, average depth 17–23 m, deepest about 35 m (S10)
- UNESCO tentative text: 150 m, 450 ha, average depth 17–23 m, deepest 35 m, about 90 million m³ (S12). The same text’s deepest figure disagrees with the park’s 29 m
- Lao Động, citing the Thai Nguyen portal: about 500 ha, 178 m, depth 17–29 m (S19)
- VnExpress: 145 m, longer than 8 km, widest about 3 km, about 500 ha, average depth 20 m, deepest 35 m (S18)

The about page’s coordinate box is 22°06'12"–22°08'14" N, 105°09'07"–105°12'22" E (S1). UNESCO’s box for the whole tentative property is N 22°14'–22°35', E 104°17'–105°35' (S12). They do not describe the same outline. Do not invent a map pin.

### Puong Cave
- Vietnamese name: Động Puông
- Kind: sight
- Area: On the Năng, under the limestone of Lũng Nham, northeast of the lake. Address line on the park page: xã Ba Bể, tỉnh Thái Nguyên (S3). The older national tourism note says huyện Ba Bể, tỉnh Bắc Kạn, 5 km from thị trấn Chợ Rã (S20)
- What the visitor does: Sits in the boat while it goes through. The park tells the Tài Ngào story: the Năng was blocked, a giant cut the mountain, and the rock he threw back became Đầu Đẳng (S3). Stalactites over the water. The park counts 23 bat species, hàng vạn animals. The national tourism note says hàng chục vạn (S3, S20). Print both. Do not turn the bats into a handling session
- Time: The through-ride is part of the boat circuit. A separate “about two hours” on a newspaper interview was not a park clock — Check on arrival
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. No cave ticket was on the park page
- From the base: About 1 km down the Năng from bến Buốc Lốm (S3). From a Pác Ngòi night, the boatman joins the circuit
- When to go: With the morning boat. Inside, it is dark either way
- Mistake to avoid: Standing up for a photograph under the bats, or flashing a light at them because the park says footsteps, torchlight, and talking wake the colony (S3)
- Local interest: The river cave and the fall
- Suggested slug: `puong-cave`
- Suggested dot: 4 — the stretch of the boat day people remember, not a separate ticketed museum
- Photo must show: the mouth with the river and a boat, or the boat under the stalactites with enough ceiling to show the height. A black frame with no boat fails. A portrait of one stalactite fails
- Sources: S3, S20

Dimensions, print both. Park location page: 300 m long, ceiling 30–40 m, average width about 30 m (S3). The national tourism note does not repeat those metres (S20).

### Dau Dang Waterfall
- Vietnamese name: Thác Đầu Đẳng
- Kind: sight
- Area: About 3 km west of the lake, where the park page puts the Thai Nguyen–Tuyen Quang line (S4). The older post on the same site still says the Bac Kan–Tuyen Quang line (S8). VnExpress puts the blockage at bản Húa Tạng, after the river has passed Lũng Nham and Puông (S18)
- What the visitor does: Lands from the boat and looks at the Năng dropping through stacked limestone. The park splits it into three sections: a steep first drop, a second reach that divides in two, and a third that slows among the rocks (S4). In March and April, after the first rains, the park says schools of fish run the fall, “cá vượt vũ môn”, and names cá chiên, some tens of kilograms (S4)
- Time: A stop on the long boat, not a second day. Minutes ashore — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- From the base: By boat toward the west end of the water (S4, S18)
- When to go: The fish run is March and April (S4). The fall itself is described as white all year (S4)
- Mistake to avoid: Reading “500 m” as a vertical drop. The park says the slope of the stacked rock is estimated at 500 m, and the height of the fall at about 53 m (S4)
- Local interest: The river cave and the fall
- Suggested slug: `dau-dang`
- Suggested dot: 4 — the end of the boat day, worth landing for
- Photo must show: white water, the stacked limestone, and forest in one frame. Foam with no rock fails
- Sources: S4, S8, S12, S18

Size, print every figure. Park location page: height about 53 m, three sections, slope estimated at 500 m, about 3 km west of the lake (S4). A post on the same site says the fall is about 2 km long (S8). UNESCO says 53 m and, in the same sentence, water “from the height of 85 meters” (S12). VnExpress says longer than 1,000 m, three tiers, each tier 3–4 m apart (S18). Do not collapse these into one height.

- When to go: Daytime. The legend says the cries were at dusk. That is a story, not a visiting hour
- Mistake to avoid: Folding it into the Puông ride. Different side of the lake, and a walk
- Local interest: Limestone forest
- Suggested slug: `hua-ma`

## Lifestyle

### A lake day
- Vietnamese name: Một ngày ở hồ
- Kind: lifestyle
- What a day here actually is: Wake in the stilt house while the lake is still. VOV describes early mist and the water “trong như ngọc” (S13). Breakfast is the house meal, not a cafe street. The middle of the day is the boat: open water, a landing at An Mạ or Ao Tiên, the ride through Puông, and Đầu Đẳng if the circuit is the long one (S3, S4, S8). VnExpress says the air is cool at dawn and at night even in summer, and that the hamlet has few shops (S18). The evening returns to the floor, the fish, and then singing if it is on (S13). The hour the village goes quiet was not printed
- When: One full day between two nights. The festival day replaces the ordinary morning when it falls on the 10th of the first lunar month (S9, S15)
- Area: Pác Ngòi or Bó Lù, then the water
- Suggested slug: `lake-day`
- Photo must show: the morning shore with a boat or a stilt house, mist allowed only if the cliff still reads. A timetable graphic fails
- Sources: S3, S4, S8, S13, S18
