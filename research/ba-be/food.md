# Food — Ba Be

Checked: 21 Oct 2026

Tay cooking on the lake, plus gifts the old district sells at two counters. No stall prices. No hotel restaurant as a pick.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Grilled lake fish
- Vietnamese name: Cá nướng
- What it is: Fish from the lake, grilled. VOV lists it first among Pác Ngòi dishes (S13). VnExpress says the fish is cleaned, seasoned, and grilled over coals, and that it is better hot (S18). A step-by-step drying method on commercial food pages was not adopted. The park names cá chiên at Đầu Đẳng, some fish tens of kilograms (S4). That is a species in the river, not a recipe
- How to eat it: Hot, with the rest of the homestay table (S18). Báo Xây dựng says it is served at Long Tong (S17)
- When to order it: The evening meal, and at the lunar new-year festival (S13, S17)
- Area, not a shop: Pác Ngòi and the festival ground at Bó Lù
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this lake. Do not reuse a coastal grilled-fish slug
- Sources: S4, S13, S17, S18

### Meat hung over the fire
- Vietnamese name: Thịt gác bếp
- What it is: VnExpress says Tay households hang pork over the kitchen fire so it keeps, and that the same preserving idea is how northwest households treat buffalo. It says the pork is for guests and for Tết (S18). The district OCOP list names thịt trâu khô separately, as a gift (S14). A national tourism article that a search snippet tied to buffalo was not reloaded (S22), so buffalo-as-the-lake-dish is not adopted. Pork over the fire is the sentence that did load
- How to eat it: With the meal for guests, or with local wine (S18)
- When to order it: When the house is feeding guests, and at Tết (S18)
- Area, not a shop: The stilt houses. Not a stall
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S14, S18

## Food streets and markets

### Pac Ngoi kitchens
- Vietnamese name: Bữa ăn nhà sàn Pác Ngòi
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats in the stilt house, not at a row of stalls. VOV’s list is cá nướng, tôm chua, măng vầu, xôi nếp nương, and lợn sữa quay, with rượu men lá (S13). VnExpress says the hamlet has few shops, so meals should be booked and water carried in (S18)
- When to go: Breakfast and the evening meal, arranged with the house. A public lunch hour — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area: Pác Ngòi, on the Lèng at the lake
- Suggested slug: `pac-ngoi-kitchens`
- Photo must show: a low table on a stilt-house floor, grilled fish and rice in the same frame, the lake or the cliff visible through the opening. A plated close-up with no house fails
- Sources: S13, S18

### OCOP counters at Cho Ra and Nam Mau
- Vietnamese name: Điểm giới thiệu OCOP tại thị trấn Chợ Rã và xã Nam Mẫu
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Buys gifts. The district says it built two counters, one in Chợ Rã town and one in Nam Mẫu, for OCOP goods and local specialties (S14). Named goods include bí xanh thơm, trà bí xanh, thịt trâu khô, miến dong, trà giảo cổ lam, chuối sấy, lạp sườn gác bếp, khẩu mẩy, rượu suối nguồn Nà Hai, and gạo Nếp Tài
- When to go: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. No opening hour was on the page
- Area: Chợ Rã, and Nam Mẫu near the homestays. Admin names on that page are pre-merger
- Suggested slug: `cho-ra-ocop`
- Photo must show: the counter with the goods and a person buying, not a logo and not a hotel lobby
- Sources: S14
