# Food — Can Tho

Slug: `can-tho`
Checked: 22 Oct 2026

This is Can Tho’s own list. The published place file points its food slug at Ho Chi Minh City. This pass does not edit that field. The later page needs Can Tho’s own guide. Dishes below are here only when a source ties them to Can Tho, or when a source puts a Mekong plate in a chapter that names Can Tho and says so in the entry. Ho Chi Minh City dishes are not borrowed. Stall prices are not copied.

A 40-to-50-entry guide can be built from this list plus the streets, and it is not padded up to that count. The opened pages support the plates and the streets below. Rooms that would fill the rest of a 40-to-50 guide were not on a page opened in full. See the gap note at the end.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Hu tieu
- Vietnamese name: Hủ tiếu
- What it is: Noodle soup from the breakfast boats. Vietnam Tourism’s floating-flavour page says the broth is pork bone and dried squid, with star anise and ginger, and that the bowl has chewy noodles and bean sprouts (S2). The towns page says tapioca noodles, sliced pork, and herbs (S4). Those are the publisher’s words. A second recipe was not opened.
- How to eat it: A bowl passed boat to boat at Cái Răng (S2). Also the dish the noodle village is built around (S1).
- When to order it: Dawn, on the water (S2, S6)
- Area, not a shop: Cái Răng boats; the noodle workshops toward Phong Điền
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none as a Ho Chi Minh City plate. The Mekong towns page already treats this bowl as Can Tho’s breakfast (S4).
- Sources: S1, S2, S4, S6

### Bun rieu

- What it is: Named with hủ tiếu and coffee as the breakfast on the boats, on the department listing (S6). A recipe was not on that page.
- How to eat it: On the boat at the market
- When to order it: Dawn (S6)
- Area, not a shop: Cái Răng
- Meal: breakfast

### Ca phe vot

- How to eat it: From the coffee boat at the market
- When to order it: With the dawn noodles (S2)

### Pineapple and market fruit
- Vietnamese name: Khóm (pineapple) and the fruit the boats carry
- What it is: Pineapple peeled on the boat, from the Đức Bridge area on the flavour page (S2). The towns page names pomelos, pineapples, and durians traded by the sack (S4). The dining chapter names rambutan, jackfruit, and mangosteen as market fruit in the delta chapter that uses Can Tho as a base (S3). The advice extract adds Phong Điền honey orange, dâu Hạ Châu, red-fleshed jackfruit, seedless durian, and Phong Điền cacao as fruit to buy (S21).
- How to eat it: Cut on the boat, or in a garden at Mỹ Khánh and Cồn Sơn (S1, S2)
- When to order it: Market morning, or the fruit months June–August (S1)
- Area, not a shop: Cái Răng boats; Phong Điền gardens; Cồn Sơn
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S2, S3, S4, S21

### Banh xeo
- Vietnamese name: Bánh xèo
- What it is: Vietnam Tourism’s place page lists “Can Tho crispy pancake” among the plates not to miss (S1). The dining chapter says the Mekong version is large, filled with pork, bean sprouts, and shrimp, and eaten in a lettuce leaf with herbs and nước chấm (S3). That chapter is the delta, with Can Tho named as a base. The place page is the line that ties the pancake to Can Tho by name. Cồn Sơn also serves bánh xèo among the cakes (S1).
- How to eat it: With the hands, in herbs, as the dining page says (S3)
- When to order it: A meal, not only breakfast. The sources do not print a clock.
- Area, not a shop: Can Tho, and the Cồn Sơn garden kitchens (S1)
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: the published dish slug `banh-xeo` is this plate. Do not describe it as a Ho Chi Minh City dish.
- Sources: S1, S3

### Nem nuong Cai Rang
- Vietnamese name: Nem nướng Cái Răng
- What it is: Skewers of lean pork grilled over charcoal, with peanut sauce, herbs, pickles, and bánh hỏi, on the four-day piece, eaten back in town after the market (S5). The place page’s “Phong Dien fine rice vermicelli (Banh hoi Phong Dien)” is the noodle that goes with this kind of plate (S1).
- How to eat it: With bánh hỏi, herbs, and the sauce (S5)
- When to order it: Lunch after the market, on that itinerary (S5)
- Area, not a shop: Cái Răng / back in town. Shop names found only in search extracts are under Notes.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: bánh hỏi
- Sources: S1, S5

### Banh hoi

- How to eat it: With the grilled pork (S5)
- When to order it: With nem nướng
- Area, not a shop: Phong Điền on the place page; with the Cái Răng pork at lunch (S1, S5)
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: nem nướng Cái Răng
- Sources: S1, S5

### Lau mam

- What it is: “Fish sauce hot pot” on the national place page’s Can Tho food list (S1). A recipe and a herb count were not on that page. A department news extract describes a Kinh–Hoa–Khmer hotpot of river fish, shrimp, squid, snail, and beef in mắm of cá linh, cá sặc, or cá chốt, with a long list of garden herbs (S31). The direct open of that news URL returned 404. The extract is not a second opened page. Use the national list as the tie, and do not print the herb inventory as confirmed.
- How to eat it: Shared, at a table. The page does not say it is a boat dish.
- When to order it: A main meal. Clock: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Can Tho, on the national list (S1)
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1

### Canh chua
- Vietnamese name: Canh chua
- What it is: Two wordings. The place page says “Can Tho sour soup with sesbania sesban flower and henicorhynchus” (bông điên điển and cá linh) (S1). The four-day piece, at dinner in downtown Can Tho, says canh chua native to the region, with fish or shrimp, pineapple, tomato, sprouts, and elephant-ear stems (S5). Record both. Do not merge them into one recipe.
- How to eat it: A dinner soup in town on the four-day piece (S5)
- When to order it: Dinner (S5). Flood months are when cá linh is the seasonal fish; a department cuisine card of 23 Sep 2025 says flood season brings cá linh and many ways of cooking it (S32). The card does not print a method.
- Area, not a shop: Downtown Can Tho (S5)
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S5, S32

### Banh cong
- Vietnamese name: Bánh cống
- What it is: The dining chapter says a savoury fritter of mung bean, taro, and rice flour, topped with a whole shrimp, popular in Can Tho and Soc Trang, eaten with herbs as an afternoon snack (S3).
- How to eat it: Two or three, with herbs, as that page says (S3)
- When to order it: Afternoon (S3)
- Area, not a shop: Can Tho and Soc Trang. The Soc Trang half is the neighbor, not this page’s city.
- Meal: sweet is wrong. It is a savoury snack. Meal: main, as a snack between meals.
- Neighbor dish to link: Soc Trang’s version, when that place exists. No Soc Trang slug is on the site. Do not link Ho Chi Minh City.
- Sources: S3

### Elephant ear fish
- Vietnamese name: Cá tai tượng
- What it is: The dining chapter calls elephant-ear fish a classic Mekong plate, fried whole and rolled with pickles, cucumber, and herbs in rice paper (S3). The chapter names Can Tho as a base. It does not say the dish was born in Can Tho. Keep that limit.
- How to eat it: Pulled off the upright fish and rolled (S3)
- When to order it: A main meal. Clock not printed.
- Area, not a shop: Mekong restaurants in the chapter. Not a named Can Tho street.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: Chau Doc is the other base in that chapter (S3). Slug `chau-doc` exists. Do not make this a Can Tho-only origin.
- Sources: S3

### Rice cakes
- Vietnamese name: Bánh dân gian. Cồn Sơn’s list on the national page: bánh bò lụi, bánh ít, bánh bột lọc, bánh lá mít, bánh da lợn, bánh đúc, bánh xèo, bánh khọt (S1). The dining chapter adds small rice cakes in banana leaves, sometimes coloured with pandan and sweetened with coconut or mung bean, as market sweets in the delta chapter (S3).
- How to eat it: At the garden, or from a market stall
- When to order it: With the islet or market visit. The folk-cake festival is a separate event.
- Area, not a shop: Cồn Sơn for the named cakes (S1). Markets for the wrapped cakes (S3).
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none. Ben Tre coconut candy is a different town’s product on that page (S3). Slug `ben-tre` exists. Do not move it onto this list as a Can Tho sweet.
- Sources: S1, S3

### Bun nuoc leo
- Vietnamese name: Bún nước lèo
- What it is: The dining page says the noodle hails from Soc Trang, 60 km southeast of Can Tho, with a fish-stock broth, and that a Vietnamese version uses pickled river fish, shrimp, roasted pork, crackling, fish balls, herbs, soy beans, and banana blossom (S3). The same page sends the reader to a named stall at 12 Đề Thám in Can Tho, selling since 2001. The stall is under Notes. The four-day piece eats this bowl in Tra Vinh, before the transfer to Can Tho (S5). The Mỹ Khánh article also names it (S29). It is served in Can Tho. It is not a Can Tho invention on these pages, and it is not a Ho Chi Minh City dish.
- How to eat it: A noodle bowl
- When to order it: A meal. Clock not printed for the Can Tho bowl.
- Area, not a shop: Đề Thám is the street the dining page names (S3). Mỹ Khánh is the garden mention (S29).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: Soc Trang, when a page exists. No slug now. Tra Vinh is a side-trip placeholder.
- Sources: S3, S5, S29

The plates are what those pages tie to a Can Tho table. They are not a ranked restaurant list.
- How to eat it: At a table, not on the wholesale boats
- When to order it: Dinner, on those pieces (S3, S5)
- Area, not a shop: The Hậu riverbank rooms. Do not publish the room names as picks.

- Neighbor dish to link: none

### De Tham
- Vietnamese name: Đường Đề Thám
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Vietnam Tourism says that in Can Tho, Đề Thám is known locally as food street, a primer for southern cooking, and that the move is to sit where it is busy (S3).
- When to go: Not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED for the hour.
- Area: Ninh Kiều. A street number for the row was not on that page. The bún nước lèo stall at number 12 is a clue under Notes, not the address of the whole street.
- Suggested slug: `de-tham`
- Photo must show: The eating street, stools, and bowls, subject through the center. unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S3

### Ninh Kieu night market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ đêm Ninh Kiều (chợ đêm Tây Đô)
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: The department listing says fruit, clothes, jewelry, handicrafts, and seafood, on a paved lane, with stalls setting up around 16:00 (S9). Hours on the listing: 17:00–22:00. Address on the listing: đường Hai Bà Trưng, phường Ninh Kiều. A footer line “98 Phan Đình Phùng” matches the tourism-centre block on other pages. Do not move the market to that number on this evidence.
- When to go: 17:00–22:00 on the listing (S9). The 2016 portal’s quay snacks are 16:00–19:00 and are the park stalls, which may be the same crowd or a different row (S17). Record both.
- Area: Hai Bà Trưng, Ninh Kiều
- Suggested slug: `ninh-kieu-night-market`
- Photo must show: The night stalls and the river or the quay in one frame if the camera can get both. A frame of only clothing racks fails the food use. unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S9, S17
