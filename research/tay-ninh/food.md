# Food — Tay Ninh

Slug: `tay-ninh`
Checked: 22 Oct 2026

The meal of this place is the Trảng Bàng rice-paper roll. Dishes of old Long An appear on the new province’s homepage. They are named so copy can tell them apart. They are not the night in Tân Ninh. No stall prices.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Bánh tráng phơi sương
- Vietnamese name: bánh tráng phơi sương Trảng Bàng
- What it is: A two-layer rice sheet, dried, toasted over a peanut-shell fire until small bubbles show and the sheet is still pale, then rested in dew (S21). The tourism page calls the dew step the one that decides the sheet (S17). The town page says the dew rest is one or two minutes, enough to soften the sheet without soaking it (S19). The 2024 article says the dew is near dawn and must stay short or the sheet goes damp (S21). Both times stay: one or two minutes, and “short, near dawn.”
- How to eat it: Rolled with boiled pork or boiled young beef, a handful of herbs, and a dipping sauce (S17). The town page names fish sauce with garlic and chili (S19). The rural-tourism page names tamarind fish sauce (S20). Print both sauces. Herbs on the tourism page include forest leaves: mặt trăng, trâm ổi, chùm mòi, quế vị, cóc, rau nhái, lộc vừng (S17). The older service article adds a longer garden-and-forest list and says the Gia Huỳnh sauces run sour and sweet (S18).
- When to order it: When you stop in Trảng Bàng. Dew-making is a dawn job (S19, S21). Eating is not limited to dawn on the pages opened.
- Area, not a shop: khu phố Gia Huỳnh for the meal, khu phố Lộc Du for the yards where sheets are made (S18). Ward line: phường Trảng Bàng after 1 Jul 2025 (S2). The articles still say thị trấn Trảng Bàng.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on a published neighbour. Bánh tráng in other cities is a different sheet. Do not link it to a Ho Chi Minh City dish.
- Sources: S2, S17, S18, S19, S20, S21

## Food streets and markets

### Khu phố Gia Huỳnh
- Vietnamese name: khu phố Gia Huỳnh
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Sits down for a rice-paper roll and a bowl of bánh canh (S18). The rural-tourism page also points at chợ Trảng Bàng and the eating strip along Quốc lộ 22B (S20).
- When to go: on the drive between Ho Chi Minh City and the city
- Area: Trảng Bàng, on Quốc lộ 22 toward Mộc Bài and Quốc lộ 22B toward the old town (S18)
- Suggested slug: gia-huynh
- Photo must show: low tables, a stack of sheets, herbs, and the lane. A single shop sign filling the frame does not pass.
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S18, S20

### Khu phố Lộc Du
- Vietnamese name: khu phố Lộc Du
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Watches sheets being poured, dried, and toasted, and can buy sheets to carry (S17, S18). It is a yard street, not a restaurant row.
- When to go: morning, if the point is the dew racks (S19, S21)
- Area: Trảng Bàng
- Suggested slug: loc-du
- Photo must show: racks of sheets, a yard, and a person at the work. A sealed souvenir packet does not pass.
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S17, S18, S19, S21

## Added 23 Oct 2026 for the food guide

The public list is every dish a publisher named. Streets stay streets. No stall price was printed. The older specialty URL S18 returned 404 on 23 Oct 2026. Facts already stored from 22 Oct stay. New facts below come from pages opened that day.

- Bánh canh Trảng Bàng: rice-flour strands, no tapioca, pork-bone broth, hock, lean pork, blood cake or fish cake (S36). The town page and S18 had not described the broth. Both stay.
- Muối tôm: salty, spicy, aromatic, with fruit, rice paper, and other dishes (S41). The mắm article lists muối ớt and muối tôm as two names (S38). A recipe that separates them was not on a page opened 23 Oct.
- Bò tơ Trảng Bàng: about 5 to 6 months, about 50–60 kg, sweet, soft, crisp skin. The described plate is the rice-paper roll. Also named, undescribed: bò nướng mọi, bò nướng sả, bò hấp gừng, bò nhúng giấm, bò nhúng mắm ruốc, lẩu đuôi bò, cháo bò, and unnamed offal (S39). Marked lunch. Hour Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Mãng cầu Bà Đen: 5,000 ha on the mountain board, chewy and deeply sweet, and lightly sweet and fragrant on S41. Decision 1804/QĐ-SHTT, certificate 00027 (S45) and certificate 6-00027 (S46), both 10 Aug 2011. OCOP 4 stars in 2021 on S45 only. Footer address is the mountain office, not an orchard.
- Bún Xiêm Lo: snakehead, turmeric, lemongrass, fish cake or pieces, thin noodles, muối ớt, rau kèo nèo, rau tai tượng, bean sprouts. Breakfast or lunch. Commune still unnamed (S37).
- Mắm chua: tép and cá cơm from Hồ Dầu Tiếng, or cá rễ tre, about 15 days (S38). Also named as Khmer small-fish and shrimp paste (S35).
- Tung Lò Mò, cà ri bò, bánh thuẫn: Cham Islam. Sausage is described. Curry and cake are names only (S35).
- Cá lóc nướng trui: rolled with seasonal forest leaves, placed with Trảng Bàng plates (S42) and also with the old Long An line (S3). Both stay.
- Hoa: hủ tiếu and mì hoành thánh in a clear, lightly sweet broth; há cảo, xíu mại, bánh bao; chè mè đen and sâm bổ lượng; caption also names bánh bao kim sa and chân gà hấp tàu xì (S35). Streets not printed.
- Khô cá lóc and four plates: gỏi xoài, chiên mắm me, gỏi lá sầu đâu, canh chua. Yards on kênh 79 and the named markets (S40). Homepage still groups it with old Long An (S3).
- Lẩu mắm: still described as Long An. Three mắm, noodles, greens (S43). Also names lạp xưởng tươi, rượu đế Gò Đen, mắm còng Cần Giuộc, and the rice, without recipes for the sausage or the crab paste.
- Gạo Nàng Thơm Chợ Đào: 170–185 days, one crop, fragrant, and the same article says the flavor has faded (S44).
- Rượu đế Gò Đen: a shop card at số 05, ấp 3B, xã Mỹ Yên, old xã Phước Lợi, huyện Bến Lức (S47). No strength.
- Thanh long Đức Hòa: named on the homepage only (S3).
- Vegetarian dishes at the Holy See: named, no dish (S41). Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Heritage year: 2015 (S41) and 2016 (S17). Both stay.
