# Food — Tay Ninh

Slug: `tay-ninh`
Checked: 22 Oct 2026

The meal of this place is the Trảng Bàng rice-paper roll. Dishes of old Long An appear on the new province’s homepage. They are named so copy can tell them apart. They are not the night in Tân Ninh. No stall prices.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Bánh tráng phơi sương
- Vietnamese name: bánh tráng phơi sương Trảng Bàng
- What it is: A two-layer rice sheet, dried, toasted over a peanut-shell fire until small bubbles show and the sheet is still pale, then rested in dew (S21). The tourism page calls the dew step the one that decides the sheet (S17). The town page says the dew rest is one or two minutes, enough to soften the sheet without soaking it (S19). The 2024 article says the dew is near dawn and must stay short or the sheet goes damp (S21). Both times stay: one or two minutes, and “short, near dawn.”
- How to eat it: Rolled with boiled pork or boiled young beef, a handful of herbs, and a dipping sauce (S17). The town page names fish sauce with garlic and chili (S19). The rural-tourism page names tamarind fish sauce (S20). Print both sauces. Herbs on the tourism page include forest leaves: mặt trăng, trâm ổi, chùm mòi, quế vị, cóc, rau nhái, lộc vừng (S17). The older service article adds a longer garden-and-forest list and says the Gia Huỳnh sauces run sour and sweet (S18).
- When to order it: When you stop in Trảng Bàng. Dew-making is a dawn job (S19, S21). Eating is not limited to dawn on the pages opened.
- Area, not a shop: khu phố Gia Huỳnh for the meal, khu phố Lộc Du for the yards where sheets are made (S18). Ward line: phường Trảng Bàng after 1 Jul 2025 (S2). The articles still say thị trấn Trảng Bàng.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on a published neighbour. Bánh tráng in other cities is a different sheet. Do not link it to a Ho Chi Minh City dish.
- Sources: S2, S17, S18, S19, S20, S21

### Bánh canh Trảng Bàng
- Vietnamese name: bánh canh Trảng Bàng
- What it is: A noodle bowl the town page and the tourism article put beside the rice-paper roll as the town’s other specialty (S18, S19). The broth, the cake, and a topping were not described on those pages.
- How to eat it: In a bowl, at the same stop as the rolls (S18). The rest: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to order it: At the Trảng Bàng stop, including the morning if that is when you pass (S18, S19).
- Area, not a shop: khu phố Gia Huỳnh (S18)
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S18, S19

## Food streets and markets

### Khu phố Gia Huỳnh
- Vietnamese name: khu phố Gia Huỳnh
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Sits down for a rice-paper roll and a bowl of bánh canh (S18). The rural-tourism page also points at chợ Trảng Bàng and the eating strip along Quốc lộ 22B (S20).
- When to go: on the drive between Ho Chi Minh City and the city
- Area: Trảng Bàng, on Quốc lộ 22 toward Mộc Bài and Quốc lộ 22B toward the old town (S18)
- Suggested slug: gia-huynh
- Photo must show: low tables, a stack of sheets, herbs, and the lane. A single shop sign filling the frame does not pass.
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S18, S20

### Khu phố Lộc Du
- Vietnamese name: khu phố Lộc Du
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Watches sheets being poured, dried, and toasted, and can buy sheets to carry (S17, S18). It is a yard street, not a restaurant row.
- When to go: morning, if the point is the dew racks (S19, S21)
- Area: Trảng Bàng
- Suggested slug: loc-du
- Photo must show: racks of sheets, a yard, and a person at the work. A sealed souvenir packet does not pass.
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S17, S18, S19, S21

### Chợ Trảng Bàng và Quốc lộ 22B
- Vietnamese name: chợ Trảng Bàng, quán ven Quốc lộ 22B
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Stops at the market and the highway eating strip the rural-tourism page names with the craft village (S20). Stalls were not listed.
- When to go: daytime on the highway
- Area: Trảng Bàng
- Suggested slug: cho-trang-bang
- Photo must show: the market aisle or the highway strip, with food in the foreground and the street behind it
- unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S20
