## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: a sampan on the Ngô Đồng, limestone towers, and rice on both banks. UNESCO describes forest-mantled towers up to 200 m, deep green forest, grey rock, blue-green water, and green and yellow rice, with visitors in traditional sampans rowed by local guides (S1). The department calls the boat on the Ngô Đồng the visit, and the old praise-name “Nam thiên đệ nhị động” (S2). The promotion page of 7 Jul 2025 says Tam Cốc means three caves — Hang Cả, Hang Hai, and Hang Ba — cut by the Ngô Đồng, and that the rice on both banks is especially fine in the ripe-rice months 5–6 (S5). The same center’s page of 23 May 2023 narrows that window to mid-May through early June, with green and gold in the same view, and says the boat has no roof (S6). Those two rice sentences are not one date. A night here is for waking up to that river, not for a second city.
- Where they sleep, as an area: the villages in the rice of Nam Hoa Lư. UNESCO’s buffer is mostly rural land with rice, and the property has about 14,000 residents, most of them families in subsistence agriculture. Occupied areas are small traditional villages, gardens, and paddies. UNESCO also says four small private tourist resorts sit inside the property and does not name them (S1). Do not name a hotel. The department’s distance is about 10 km from the provincial center (S2). The 14 Jul 2023 page lists resort stays as one of the visit types and does not name a street (S7). The 7 Jul 2025 page describes community tourism here: cycling the rice, making cakes, weaving, and cooking with local people (S5). A named homestay lane is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Where the evening is: the lanes among the paddies after the boat, not a published night street. No page opened for this pass names a bar street or a night market in Tam Coc. The 7 Jul 2025 page’s evening-shaped activities are the village ones: the bicycle, the cooking, the quiet (S5). Pier open and close times are Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. They are not on the 2026 price list (S3), the 14 Aug 2025 department page (S2), or the 7 Jul 2025 article (S5). A 4 Dec 2016 provincial tip says restaurants in Ninh Bình often run 07:00–22:00 and tells visitors not to go into empty places too late (S12). That is a provincial caution, not a Tam Coc street.
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): the sampan on the Ngô Đồng, and Bích Động about 2 km from the Tam Coc wharf. They are pins, not a ranked list. The support desk at the boating area is the third pin, for where the ticket is, not for the evening.
- Hero must include: one sampan on the Ngô Đồng, the limestone towers, and rice on the bank, in one daylight frame, subject through the center. The 23 May 2023 page says the boat has no roof, so the frame should not depend on a canopy (S6). A rower may be in the frame if the river, the towers, and the rice still read.
- A crop that would fail: empty sky, or a tower cut off at the top; a cave interior so dark that the river and the rice are gone; a close crop of a rower that loses the karst; a hotel pool or a resort front standing in for the river.

### The river at Tam Coc
- Vietnamese name: Sông Ngô Đồng, khu du lịch Tam Cốc – Bích Động
- Kind: scene pin for the place. The three caves are the published sight `three-caves`. This pass does not open that page.
- Area: phường Nam Hoa Lư on the 14 Aug 2025 page (S2). The 23 May 2023 page names the landing bến đò Văn Lâm (S6). The 15 Jan 2021 support page says only “Tam Coc Bich Dong Tourist Area, Nam Hoa Lu Ward” (S4). The 7 Jul 2025 page says “bến thuyền Tam Cốc” (S5). Văn Lâm is the 2023 name. Whether that name is still on the gate is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- What the visitor does: Rows the Ngô Đồng with a local rower, through Hang Cả, Hang Hai, and Hang Ba, with rice and karst on the banks (S2, S5). The department also lists Bích Động pagoda, Thái Vi temple, Tiên cave, Linh Cốc pagoda, and Thung Nắng inside the same tourist area (S2). Those are later sight entries, not this pin. The 4 Dec 2016 provincial tip says visitors wear life jackets and follow the rower through the caves (S12).
- Time: about 2 hours on the water, on both the 7 Jul 2025 page and the 23 May 2023 page (S5, S6). Pier hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Fee: The promotion center’s price list, updated 27 Feb 2026, prints one route, “Tam Cốc – Đền Thái Vi – Động Tiên – chùa Bích Động”, phone 19009157. Without the electric transfer: adults 250,000 VND, children 1–1.3 m 120,000 VND. With the electric transfer: adults 350,000 VND, children 1–1.3 m 190,000 VND (S3). It does not split a boat fare from an entrance fare, and it does not print a band for a child under 1 m. The 23 May 2023 page printed a different pair: 120,000 VND a person, then a boat at 150,000 VND a boat for 4 Vietnamese guests or for 2 foreign guests, and free under 3 years old (S6). Those are not the 2026 figures. Use the 27 Feb 2026 list, and do not carry the 2023 split forward. Anyone the 2026 list does not name: Check on arrival.
- From the base: the wharf is the start if you slept in the villages. From Ga Ninh Bình or the provincial center, the road is in `getting-there.md`. The department’s “about 10 km” is from the provincial center, not a measured ride from the station (S2).
- When to go: early, while the light is still soft, on the 23 May 2023 page, and bring a hat because the boat has no roof (S6). For the rice picture, the 7 Jul 2025 page says months 5–6, and the 23 May 2023 page says mid-May to early June (S5, S6). Print both. Do not promise gold on a date between them.
- Mistake to avoid: treating the 2023 boat-plus-entrance pair as the fare on the door, and treating a night street as if one had been published. Also staying on the water when the department has stopped the route. The 17 Jul 2024 notice tells site managers to stop a cave, river, or lake visit the moment the weather makes it unsafe, and to say so (S14).
- Local interest: the river, the rice, and the photograph of both
- Suggested slug: already the place `tam-coc`. Do not open a second place.
- Suggested dot: already published on the place — do not change
- Photo must show: the sampan, the towers, and the rice in one daylight frame
- Sources: S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6, S12, S14

The three caves are not given one length. The heritage department’s ranking essay: Hang Cả about 180 m long, about 30 m wide, ceiling above 5 m; Hang Hai about 90 m, ceiling about 3.5 m; Hang Ba more than 80 m, ceiling under 3 m, and often flooded when the rain is heavy, so the stalactites are worn smooth (S8). The promotion page of 7 Jul 2025: Hang Cả about 127 m, Hang Hai about 60 m, Hang Ba about 50 m (S5). The 23 May 2023 page repeats 127 / 60 / 50 and adds that Hang Hai is nearly 1 km from Hang Cả, and that the mouth of Hang Cả is over 20 m wide (S6). Do not collapse these into one set of meters. The sight entry belongs to the attractions lane. Nothing opened here says a passenger must duck.

### Bích Động, as a pin
- Vietnamese name: Chùa Bích Động
- Kind: scene pin. Not a separate public slug in this pass.
- Area: about 2 km from the Tam Coc wharf (S5). The heritage essay describes three levels, chùa Hạ, chùa Trung, and chùa Thượng, built into the rock (S8).
- What the visitor does: walks the stone steps between the three pagodas. Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Fee: inside the 2026 route ticket above if the visitor is on that route (S3). A standalone gate fee was not printed on the Tam Coc rows.
- Sources: S3, S5, S8

### Support desk at the boating area
- Vietnamese name: not printed. The English page titles it “Support center at Tam Coc Boating”.
- Kind: scene pin for the ticket, not a sight page.
- Area: Tam Coc Bich Dong Tourist Area, Nam Hoa Lu Ward. Telephone on the page updated 15 Jan 2021: +84 2296 270 373 (S4). The 27 Feb 2026 price list prints 19009157 for the same tourist area (S3). The two numbers are not the same. The 14 Jul 2023 page also prints a visitor hotline, 19000117 (S7). Do not pick one and drop the others.
- Sources: S3, S4, S7
