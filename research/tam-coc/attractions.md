# Attractions — Tam Coc

Slug: `tam-coc`
Checked: 23 Oct 2026

The search slug stays `tam-coc`. The base is thôn Văn Lâm and the sampan on sông Ngô Đồng. `ninh-binh` stays its own page. Tràng An, Hoa Lư, and Hang Múa are that page. Do not rebuild them here.

## Why a night here

### The night on the Ngô Đồng

- The picture they expect: Sampans on sông Ngô Đồng, with the karst on both sides and the rice at the water. UNESCO’s criterion (vii) for the whole Trang An property is towers up to 200 m, green and yellow paddies, and sampans rowed by local guides (S1, S2). The 2014 decision says “200m-high” towers. The current list page says “up to 200m”. Both stay. Tam Coc is the Ngô Đồng boat inside the Trang An–Tam Coc–Bich Dong scenic landscape, not the Tràng An sampan on sông Sào Khê.
- Where they sleep, as an area: Thôn Văn Lâm, along the rice roads by the pier. Vietnamese Wikipedia says lodging grew at the boat pier, in the tourism-village cluster, and on the road from Tam Coc to Bích Động (S7). No hotel brand. A room rate was not taken from a single commercial “from” figure. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Where the evening is: The fields and the lanes after the boat. One commercial sample puts a walk in the fields at 18:30 (S17). No opened page names a bar street.
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): The pier at Đình Các / bến thuyền Tam Cốc, where the 24 May 2026 procession started (S13). The three caves on the river (S5, S7). Chùa Bích Động, 2 km from the pier on the Wikipedia page (S7). Prices for the ticket are in the sight entries, not here as a ranking.
- Hero must include: The boats, the river, and the karst in one frame. The published file `public/photos/tam-coc.jpg` was opened on 23 Oct 2026. It shows several sampans, the river, and the karst towers together. It matches. Credit on the photo ledger: “travel oriented”, CC BY-SA 2.0, Wikimedia file Tam_Cốc-Bích_Động in Ninh Binh province 2.
- A crop that would fail: A frame of water only, a frame of sky only, or a portrait of one rower that cuts the karst off. A tight crop that keeps a single boat and loses the towers fails.

### The three caves
- Vietnamese name: Tam Cốc — hang Cả, hang Hai, hang Ba
- Kind: sight
- Area: Sông Ngô Đồng, from the central pier
- What the visitor does: Boards a sampan at the central pier and rides sông Ngô Đồng through three water caves. Tam Cốc means three caves. The heritage bureau also calls hang Cả hang Ngoài or hang Lớn, hang Hai hang Giữa or hang Trung, and hang Ba hang Bé (S5). The ride passes rice and karst and returns to the same pier (S7).
- Time: Khoảng 2 giờ out and back, Vietnamese Wikipedia only (S7). The ticket page prints no duration. A second clock was not opened.
- Fee: Included in the Tam Cốc–Bích Động ticket of 27 Feb 2026. Without the shuttle: adult 250,000 đồng, child 1–1.3 m 120,000 đồng. With the shuttle: adult 350,000 đồng, child 1–1.3 m 190,000 đồng. Hotline 19009157. Route named on that row: Tam Cốc, Đền Thái Vi, Động Tiên, chùa Bích Động (S3). A child under 1 m has no line on this row. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. An older tourism-center page, updated 23 May 2023, prints a different Tam Coc ticket: 120,000 đồng a person a turn, then a boat ticket of 150,000 đồng a trip for 4 domestic guests or 2 international guests, free for a child under 3 (S24). Daily hours were not printed on any of the three. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- From the base: The pier is in the village. Đình Các is the landing name on the gazetteer and on the Wikipedia route list (S7, S11).
- When to go: The 2026 ripe-rice festival used the end of May, opening morning 24 May (S13, S14). A standing month beyond that festival was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.

### Chùa Bích Động
- Vietnamese name: Chùa Bích Động
- Kind: sight
- Area: Núi Bích Động, thôn Đam Khê on the older pages. 2 km from the Tam Coc pier (S7).
- What the visitor does: Climbs a pagoda built in three halls up the hill, Hạ, Trung, and Thượng. The name means green cave. In front, a branch of sông Ngô Đồng and the rice (S5, S7). The dry cave holds the pagoda. The water cave through the hill is Xuyên Thủy Động, a separate entry.
- Time: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. No opened page printed a minute for the stair.
- Fee: Named on the combined ticket route (S3). No separate Bích Động price on that table. Do not invent one. Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- From the base: 2 km from the pier (S7). Bicycle or the shuttle on the 350,000 đồng ticket (S3, S7). A walk minute was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: With the boat, same day. Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Mistake to avoid: Calling it Nam thiên đệ nhất động. The pages that use the old ranking call Bích Động the second, Nam thiên đệ nhị động (S4, S5, S7).
- Local interest: The green cave pagoda
- Suggested slug: bich-dong
- Suggested dot: 4 — the pagoda is the walk the boat ticket already names
- Photo must show: The three halls on the limestone, with the river or the rice in the same frame. A portrait of one statue loses the hill. unpublished: no licensed photo in this dossier.
- Published 23 Oct 2026: licensed photos found: `File:Bich dong.jpg` and a Jakub Hałun frame. Page `bich-dong`. See `attractions/bich-dong.md`.
- Naming and founding, all kept: Heritage bureau: formerly Bích Sơn; founded in the Lê Trung Hưng period by the monks Trí Kiên and Trí Thể; in Giáp Ngọ (1774) Trịnh Sâm had Nguyễn Nghiễm supervise an eight-month carving of the two characters “Bích Động”, each character’s frame 1.5 m (S5). Vietnamese Wikipedia: Nguyễn Nghiễm named the cave in 1773; the pagoda dates from the early Hậu Lê and was enlarged under Lê Hiển Tông (1740–1786) into the three halls (S7). French Wikipedia: the pagoda complex was built in 1428 (S9). Do not pick a year or a person.
- Sources: S3, S4, S5, S7, S9
