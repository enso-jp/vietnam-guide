## What it is

- English name: Bích Động Pagoda
- Vietnamese name: Chùa Bích Động
- Kicker: Cave pagoda in three halls up a limestone hill
- Kind: sight

## What the visitor does

- The scene, in order: three halls in "tam tòa" order up the hill — chùa Hạ, chùa Trung, chùa Thượng; Buddha statues in the dark caves; towers on the rock faces (S5). Chùa Hạ: five bays on a high base, monolithic stone columns over 4 m (S38). Xuyên Thủy water cave under the halls (S7, S38).
- Name and history on the page (official first): formerly Bích Sơn; built up in the Lê Trung Hưng period by the monks Trí Kiên and Trí Thể; Giáp Ngọ 1774, Trịnh Sâm had Nguyễn Nghiễm oversee eight months of carving the characters "Bích Động", frame 1.5 m (S5). "Nam thiên đệ nhị động" (S5, S7). War against the French: Phan Đình Phùng arms workshop, rice store, leaflet printing, training ground (S5).
- Conflicts kept here, off the page: Vietnamese Wikipedia (S7) has Nguyễn Nghiễm naming the cave in 1773; chùa Bích Động article (S38) says the pagoda formed in 1428, monks arrived 1705, bell cast 1707, and the original name "Bạch Ngọc Thạch Sơn Đồng"; French Wikipedia (S9) also 1428. The page uses the heritage bureau.
- How long on site: UNSOURCED. Page: Check on arrival.
- From the base: 2 km from the pier (S7); bicycle or the shuttle ticket (S3, S7).
- Mistake: calling it the "first" cave; the ranking is the second (S4, S5, S7).

## Fee

- On the combined Tam Cốc–Bích Động row: adult 250,000 VND, child 1–1.3 m 120,000 VND; with shuttle 350,000 / 190,000 VND (S3, re-opened 23 Oct 2026). No separate pagoda price.
- Xuyên Thủy is not named on the row (S3). Left off the page as a fee.
