## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Cơm cháy
- Vietnamese name: Cơm cháy
- What it is: Crisp rice. The tourism department, 20 Dec 2016, describes Ninh Bình cơm cháy as cooked rice spread thin in a round, cooled, dried, fried, then broken up. The topping on that page is beef tenderloin and pork heart and kidney, with tomato, carrot, onion, and nấm hương, poured on so the rice crackles (S18). DulichToday, updated 25 Jan 2024, says that in restaurants at Tam Coc the same rice is eaten with thịt dê núi and with tim cật stir-fried with carrot, tomato, mushroom, and onion, and that the rice is nếp hương with ruốc (S17). The culture office, 20 Oct 2023, describes the Ninh Bình plate as beef, pork heart and kidney, tomato, and carrot, and places that meal in thành phố Ninh Bình or the old capital, not in a Tam Coc street (S19). Beef and shiitake stay. Goat and ruốc stay. The Tam Coc restaurant sentence and the city sentence both stay.
- How to eat it: Broken into the bowl, with the hot topping poured over, on the 2016 page (S18). Or with the goat and the offal stir-fry, on the Tam Coc restaurant sentence (S17).
- When to order it: With the goat plates, on the Tam Coc restaurant sentence (S17). A meal hour was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Restaurants at Tam Coc, no street named (S17). The official recipe pages do not name a street.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: The city cơm cháy on `ninh-binh`. Do not collapse the two recipes.
- Sources: S17, S18, S19

### Thịt dê núi
- Vietnamese name: Thịt dê núi
- What it is: Mountain goat. DulichToday says it is what people eat at Tam Coc and in Ninh Bình, from goats left on the hills rather than penned, and lists the table herbs as chanh, gừng, ớt, sả, riềng, lá sung, lá mơ, and green banana (S17). The culture office describes the same herding for Ninh Bình goat and places the cooking with người Hoa Lư and the city or the old capital (S19). Both placements stay. The office’s other preparations (nem dê, tiết canh dê, lẩu dê, dê xào lăn, tái dê) are neighbor dishes below. They were not printed as Tam Coc plates.
- How to eat it: With the herbs named above (S17).
- When to order it: With cơm cháy, on the Tam Coc restaurant sentence (S17). Hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Restaurants at Tam Coc, no street (S17).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: tái dê and the other goat plates on `ninh-binh`
- Sources: S17, S19
