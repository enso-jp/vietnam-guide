## Why a night here

### The valley night

- The picture they expect: Limestone towers in the river, and a sampan. UNESCO describes forested towers up to 200 m, water in the depressions, and visitors in sampans rowed by local guides (S1). The tourism page puts that landscape about 90 km southeast of Hà Nội and calls the boat water sông Sào Khê on the relics page (S3, S11). The public hero file is a temple gate at Phúc Sơn, Ninh Tiến. The karst is not in that frame (S33).
- Where they sleep, as an area: Rooms in the Hoa Lư valley, around the Tràng An wharf and the old capital, phường Tây Hoa Lư on the Bái Đính page and xã Trường Yên on the heritage page (S4, S6). Or rooms in the city if the train arrives late, at ga Ninh Bình (S16, S32). No hotel brand. Tam Coc hamlets are the other slug.
- Where the evening is: Phố cổ Hoa Lư, where the management board prints a free visit and a paid lantern boat (S5). The city pages opened do not name a bar street. Non Nước is the sunset the relics page names, on sông Vân (S11).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): The Tràng An wharf, https://trangandanhthang.vn/gia-ve-tham-quan-cac-khu-diem-du-lich/ (S5). Đền vua Đinh and đền vua Lê, https://dsvh.gov.vn/di-tich-lich-su-va-kien-truc-nghe-thuat-co-do-hoa-lu-2952 (S6). Ga Ninh Bình, the station front in S32. They are pins, not a ranked list.
- Hero must include: Limestone towers and the river in one frame, with at least one sampan, subject through the center. The frame that does this is S21. Rice can sit in the same picture when the paddies are in the valley. The towers have to stay whole.
- A crop that would fail: The Phúc Sơn gate (S33). A stone bridge and boats with no towers (S22). The Tam Cốc file (S34). Empty sky, fog with no cliff, a bell filling the frame (S29), or a tomb used as if it were the temple hall (S31).

The tourism overview is the merged province. Its 90 km of coast includes beaches it names at Kim Sơn, Thịnh Long, and Rạng Đông (S10). Thịnh Long and Rạng Đông are the Nam Định coast. This night is the karst, not that coast.

### Hoa Lu
- Vietnamese name: Cố đô Hoa Lư, đền vua Đinh, đền vua Lê
- Kind: sight
- Area: Xã Trường Yên, huyện Hoa Lư, tỉnh Ninh Bình on the heritage page (S6). The festival page uses the same commune (S7).
- What the visitor does: Walks the two temples and the mountain tombs. The heritage page says this ground was the capital of the Đinh, Tiền Lê, and Lý, and names six kings: Đinh Tiên Hoàng, Đinh Toàn, Lê Đại Hành, Lê Trung Tông, Lê Long Đĩnh, and Lý Thái Tổ (S6). The relics page says the capital is Đinh, Tiền Lê, and early Lý (S11). Đền Đinh stands on the old main hall, about 3 mẫu Bắc Bộ, with núi Mã Yên in front and núi Dù behind (S6). Đền Lê is a chữ Công plan. Tiền đường is 5 gian, Hậu Lê style. Thiêu hương is 2 gian, 2.8 m long and 6.15 m wide. Hậu cung is 5 gian, 14 m by 6 m. The middle bay holds the statue of Lê Đại Hành, the left bay Dương Vân Nga, the right bay Lê Long Đĩnh (S6). The tombs on núi Mã Yên carry steles dated Minh Mệnh 21 (1840) (S6). The page also lists chùa Nhất Trụ and a stone column dated 995, đình Yên Trạch, chùa Ngần, and smaller phủ. 678 objects (S6). Ranked a special national relic (S6, S11).
- Time: A pair of temples. A minute was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Fee: Board page. Adult 20,000 đồng. Child 6–15: 10,000 đồng. Age 60 and over, and disabled visitors: 10,000 đồng. Hotline 02293 620 099 (S5). Support phone 02293.621.890 (S9). Both numbers stay. Am Tiên is a separate line on the same page. Do not fold that 100,000 đồng into this 20,000 đồng.
- From the base: The valley. Minutes from the station: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: The festival window is in `events.md`. A daily gate hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Mistake to avoid: Printing only the 10th, or only the 8th through the 10th. Both are in the sources. Also avoid using the tomb photo as the temple hall.
- Local interest: The old capital
- Suggested slug: hoa-lu

### Am Tien
- Vietnamese name: Động Am Tiên, hang Rồng. The ticket line also says Tuyệt Tịnh Cốc (S5).
- Kind: sight
- Area: About 400 m from the east gate of đền Đinh Tiên Hoàng (S6). Same Trường Yên ground.
- What the visitor does: Climbs to a cave temple. The heritage page says Dương Vân Nga lived here late in life, and Nguyễn Minh Không preached here under the Lý. Most of the cave sits in a flooded valley. The mouth is on the cliff. 205 stone steps. The mouth is compared to a dragon, hence hang Rồng. The main cave is both a pagoda, for the Buddha and Nguyễn Minh Không, and a temple for Dương Vân Nga (S6).
- Time: The stair is 205 steps (S6). A minute was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.

- From the base: Walk from the temples if the 400 m holds (S6). Minutes: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: With Hoa Lư. A gate hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Mistake to avoid: Paying the temple ticket and assuming the cave is inside it. The board prints two lines (S5).
- Local interest: The old capital
- Suggested slug: am-tien
- Suggested dot: 3 — the cave beside the temples, not a separate day
- Photo must show: The cave mouth and the steps, or the flooded valley with the cliff, subject through the center. A statue crop fails. `unpublished: no licensed photo`
