### S1
- URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1438/
- Publisher: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, list entry for Trang An Landscape Complex, dossier 1438bis
- Accessed: 22 Oct 2026
- Supports: Mixed site. Inscribed 2014. Minor boundary modification inscribed 2016. Property 6,226 ha. Buffer 6,026 ha. Three protected areas: Hoa Lu Ancient Capital, Trang An-Tam Coc-Bich Dong Scenic Landscape, Hoa Lu Special-Use Forest. About 14,000 residents. Towers up to 200 m. Sampans rowed by local guides. Archaeology over more than 30,000 years. Karst dissection over five million years. Bai Dinh Pagoda complex is named inside the buffer zone as recently reconstructed. No cave count and no boat duration on this page.

### S2
- URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/6099/
- Publisher: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Decision 38 COM 8B.14, 2014
- Accessed: 22 Oct 2026
- Supports: The inscription decision’s provisional text. Property 6,172 ha. Buffer 6,079 ha. Same three protected areas, worded “Trang An-Tam Coc-Bich Dong Scenic Area” and “Hoa Lu Special-Use Primary Forest”. 14,000 residents. Towers described as 200 m high. These hectares are not the 2016 figures on S1. Both pairs stay.

### S3

- Publisher: Sở Du lịch Ninh Bình, page dated 14 Aug 2025
- Accessed: 22 Oct 2026
- Supports: About 90 km southeast of Hà Nội. About 12,252 ha. 26 provincial relics and 20 national relics inside that count, including two special national relics: Cố đô Hoa Lư, and danh lam thắng cảnh Tràng An–Tam Cốc–Bích Động. UNESCO mixed site, called the first mixed site of Vietnam and Southeast Asia. The page does not say that 12,252 is the sum of S1’s 6,226 and 6,026. The arithmetic is noted in `getting-there.md` and is not a sentence of this page.

### S4
- URL: https://sodulich.ninhbinh.gov.vn/vi/tai-nguyen-du-lich-van-hoa/chua-bai-dinh-21.html
- Publisher: Sở Du lịch Ninh Bình, page dated 15 Aug 2025
- Accessed: 22 Oct 2026
- Supports: Bái Đính inside the Tràng An complex, phường Tây Hoa Lư. About 20 km from the provincial centre. About 100 km from Hà Nội. Old pagoda and new complex. Nguyễn Minh Không, 1136, for the old foundation. New complex built and expanded from 2003, more than 1,000 ha, five levels on the main axis. The page’s record claims: largest gilt Buddha in Asia, longest arhat corridor in Asia, tallest Buddhist relic tower in Asia, largest bronze Maitreya in Southeast Asia. No ticket and no hour on this page.
