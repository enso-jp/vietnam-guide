## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Mountain goat
- Vietnamese name: Thịt dê núi, dê tái chanh
- What it is: Goat grazed on the limestone, the dish the culture page puts first with cơm cháy (S12). The page says the meat is firm because the animals climb. Dishes it lists: dê tái chanh, dê xào sả ớt, dê núi nhúng mẻ, dê nướng ngũ vị, dê hầm thuốc bắc, dồi dê chiên, cơm cháy sốt dê, cháo thịt dê, dê ủ trấu, cà ri dê (S12).
- How to eat it: The page says the usual way for dê tái chanh is with lá sung, dipped in nước tương (S12). That is the page’s instruction, not a tasting note added here.
- When to order it: The page does not print a meal hour. It says the shops sit on the roads and lanes toward the sights (S12). The hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: The roads out to Tràng An, Hoa Lư, and Bái Đính. No shop name.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Photo: S27 shows crackers, soup, and greens. Goat is not identifiable. `unpublished: no licensed photo` of dê tái chanh.
- Sources: S12, S27

### Crispy rice
- Vietnamese name: Cơm cháy
- What it is: The gift the culture page names. Crisp rice cakes. The page says the rice is gạo tám thơm from Hải Hậu, and it still calls Hải Hậu tỉnh Nam Định (S12). After the merger, that wording and the new province both exist. Do not rewrite Hải Hậu into a Ninh Bình village. The page says the rice is cooked in a thick iron pot, the edge scorched, crisp without being hard (S12). Eaten with ruốc, or as cơm cháy sốt dê (S12).
- How to eat it: Break the cake. With ruốc as a gift, or with the goat sauce at the table (S12). The opened plate is the dry cake (S26).
- When to order it: With the goat meal, or as the thing you carry home (S12). A shop hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: The same roads as the goat. Also a packaged gift, so it is not only a table.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Photo: S26, Nguyễn Thanh Quang, CC BY-SA 3.0. A plate of the crisp rice, close. A licensed photo of cơm cháy. A street table was not the frame.
- Sources: S12, S26

### Kim Son noodle soup
- Vietnamese name: Bún mọc Kim Sơn
- What it is: Noodles, pork balls, and broth. The page says the pork is leg meat, ground, seasoned with nước mắm, pepper, and bột ngọt, spread on banana leaf, then boiled 7 to 10 minutes. The balls are about the size of a child’s fist (S12).
- How to eat it: With seasonal herbs the page lists: hoa chuối, kinh giới, tía tô, húng quê, giá, rau răm, lime or kumquat, chili vinegar, pepper (S12).
- When to order it: Not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Kim Sơn (S12).
- Meal: main

## Food streets and markets

- Vietnamese name: Các tuyến đường ra điểm du lịch
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats goat. The culture page says every road, lane, and approach to the sights has dê núi shops (S12). It does not name one street.
- When to go: After the boat or the temples. An hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
