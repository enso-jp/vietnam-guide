## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Mountain goat
- Vietnamese name: Dê núi. The plate people mean is often dê tái chanh, plus dê nướng and dê hấp.
- What it is: Goat raised on the limestone, which the department says eats wild leaves. The meat is described as firm, fragrant, and sweet. Served with cơm cháy and a rich sauce (S18, S19).
- How to eat it: With the crispy rice and the sauce. The 22 May 2024 page also names dê quay, cháo dê, and lòng đắng (S19).
- When to order it: Lunch or dinner. The 20 Jan 2026 page frames it as a cold-weather meal (S18). The 4 Dec 2016 page says goat restaurants near the sights often run 07:00–22:00 (S11). That hour is old and is not one restaurant.
- Area, not a shop: Near the sights, and as a gift from Cho Rong, Cho Bau, or the central streets (S3, S11). No street name for a goat row.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S11, S18, S19

### Crispy rice
- Vietnamese name: Cơm cháy
- What it is: A crisp fried rice crust. The department serves it with the goat sauce (S18, S19). Vietnam Tourism describes the same crust as a snack or something dipped in a stew, and romanizes it “com chay” (S16). Use cơm cháy.
- How to eat it: Broken up, with the goat sauce, or carried home as a pack. The 2025 visitor guide lists it among gifts (S3).
- When to order it: With the goat meal, or from a market when leaving.
- Area, not a shop: The same gift markets. A producer’s address was not on these pages.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S3, S16, S18, S19

### Eel noodle soup
- Vietnamese name: The 20 Jan 2026 page says bún lươn in the introduction and miến lươn in the dish paragraph (S18). Both names are kept. They may be two bowls. This pass does not merge them.
- What it is: A hot bowl of eel broth with herbs and chili. The miến paragraph says glass noodles (S18).
- How to eat it: As a morning bowl on a cold day (S18).
- When to order it: Breakfast, on that page’s wording.
- Area, not a shop: Not given. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Meal: breakfast

- How to eat it: Hot.
- When to order it: The same winter page (S18).

### Ant-egg sticky rice
- Vietnamese name: Xôi trứng kiến
- What it is: Sticky rice with young ant eggs from the Nho Quan limestone. The eggs are washed, seasoned, and stir-fried, then mixed with the rice and fried shallot (S19).
- How to eat it: As a plate. The page does not give a time of day.
- When to order it: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: The ingredient is tied to Nho Quan, not to a stall (S19).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19

- What it is: A Kim Son salad of nhệch fish, sticky rice, vinegar, lemongrass, garlic, chili, and ginger. The page says the fish smell is taken out in several steps (S19).
- How to eat it: As a shared plate.
- When to order it: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: Kim Son. That is a day-trip distance, not the Tam Coc hamlets. Minutes: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.

### To Nhu pork-ball noodles
- Vietnamese name: Bún mọc Tố Như

- How to eat it: Noodles, broth, and herbs kept apart, then combined at the table.
- When to order it: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.

### Steamed rice rolls
- Vietnamese name: Bánh cuốn
- What it is: Fresh rice-paper rolls with diced pork and mushrooms, mint, and a mild fish sauce. Vietnam Tourism calls it a local breakfast (S16). The department’s gift list names bánh cuốn Phủ Lý, which is the former Ha Nam town, not this bowl (S3).
- How to eat it: In the morning, with the herbs and sauce.
- When to order it: Breakfast (S16).
- Area, not a shop: Not given. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: bánh cuốn Phủ Lý, no place slug yet (S3)
- Sources: S16
