## Nearest airport

- Name: Cảng hàng không Cà Mau. IATA: CAH.
- The airline ticket page prints the airport in phường 6, thành phố Cà Mau, about 10 km from the center, and 20–30 minutes in. Reference fares on that page: xe ôm 60,000 VND a ride, taxi 150,000 VND a trip. It says those fares are a reference and should be agreed before the ride (S16).
- The airline’s airport guide, titled as a 2025 update, prints a different pair: Số 93, đường Lý Thường Kiệt, phường 6, and 2 km to the center. Taxi 5–10 minutes and 70,000–100,000 VND. Xe ôm 5–10 minutes and 20,000–40,000 VND. It also prints a runway of 1,500 m by 30 m, a terminal of 2,233 m2, and ATR-72 or ATR-42 (S17).
- Record both distances, both clocks, and both ground fares. Do not pick one for a page.
- The same guide says civil use began in 2004, and that a Deputy Prime Minister’s decision of 16 October 2024 names ACV as investor for an upgrade of about 2,400 billion VND, with a new runway 2,400 m by 45 m for A320 and A321. It prints a 2030 aim of ICAO 4C and 1 million passengers a year, and a 2050 aim of 3 million (S17).
- VietnamPlus, 9 October 2026, quotes the airport director Phạm Thanh Lâm: commercial flights resume 29 November 2026 after more than a year stopped for the upgrade. The planned day is Vietnam Airlines, A321. Ho Chi Minh City to Cà Mau 12:50–13:55. The return 14:50–15:50. He gives a fare of 924,000–964,000 VND including the ticketing fee. The runway was still being finished that day. The same article prints the 2021–2030 plan: category 4C, 1 million passengers and 1,000 tons of cargo a year, and a 2050 aim of 3 million passengers and 3,000 tons. A Hanoi flight is a hope he states, not a timetable (S15).
- The airline ticket page, opened 22 October 2026, still lists Ho Chi Minh City–Cà Mau as the only direct route being flown, about 1 hour 10 minutes, one flight a day. Connections via Tân Sơn Nhất: Hanoi about 4 hours 40 minutes to 7 hours 40 minutes, four flights a day; Thanh Hóa about 4 hours 45 minutes, one flight a day. Sample fares on that page start at 955,181 VND, and a later answer on the same page says fares from 1,206,000 VND one way. It shows seats on dates that include 28 November 2026 (S16).

## Nearest rail

- No passenger station is open. Tuổi Trẻ, 10 October 2025, says the Mekong Delta has had no railway since the Sài Gòn–Mỹ Tho line stopped in 1958. The pre-feasibility it describes is 175.2 km from ga An Bình to ga Cần Thơ, through Ho Chi Minh City, Tây Ninh, Đồng Tháp, Vĩnh Long, and Cần Thơ. Twelve main stations. A hoped start at the end of 2027 and phase 1 basically finished in 2035. Cà Mau is not a station on that scheme (S22).
- The same article says residents, hearing of the Cần Thơ line, also hope for a later Cần Thơ–Cà Mau line. That sentence is a hope. It is not a decided alignment or a year (S22).
- Do not tell a traveler to take a train.

## From Cần Thơ

### Expressway
- Length: 110.85 km on the project board, as printed by VietnamPlus and by Tuổi Trẻ on 19 January 2026 (S18, S19). Pháp Luật / Tuổi Trẻ on 28 April 2026 prints “gần 111 km” of main line and “gần 26 km” of link roads (S20). Record both.
- The line opened for traffic at 11:00 on 19 January 2026. The Hậu Giang–Cà Mau section is 73.2 km. The Cần Thơ–Hậu Giang section had been open since 22 December 2025. Speed 80 km/h maximum and 60 km/h minimum. Motorbikes, bicycles, and people on foot are not allowed (S18, S19).
- Duration, 19 January 2026: the project board says the expressway cuts the Cần Thơ–Cà Mau ride to 1.5 hours, from 3 hours before. The Tuổi Trẻ headline that day says about 90 minutes. One driver that morning told the paper the ride took more than 1 hour 15 minutes (S19). Record all three. Do not pick one.
- The Hậu Giang–Cà Mau section was paused on 16 March 2026 for unfinished drains, slopes, and shoulders. The project board said the full line would reopen at 11:30 on 29 April 2026 (S20). A later closure, after that April notice, was not on the pages opened on 22 October 2026.
- Where it sits: the Cần Thơ end is at phường Cái Răng, off the Nam Sông Hậu / Quốc lộ 91B road, near Cảng Cái Cui. The Cà Mau end is nút giao IC12, xã Hồ Thị Kỷ, on the southern coastal corridor the notices call đường Hành lang ven biển phía Nam, or đường Xuyên Á (S18, S19, S20).
- The expressway does not reach Đất Mũi. The cape road is the one below.
- Toll: the January VietnamPlus piece says the line has non-stop toll gates. It does not print a toll. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED for the đồng amount.

## From the city to Đất Mũi

- The road is the last section of đường Hồ Chí Minh. Báo Đầu tư, 29 November 2023, prints the project length as 58.7 km, from cầu Ông Tình at Năm Căn to Khu du lịch Đất Mũi, xã Đất Mũi, huyện Ngọc Hiển. Phase 1 was finished in 2016. The alignment is drawn as cấp III đồng bằng, design speed 80 km/h, but the article says the cross-section still matches cấp V, about 40 km/h, and the surface is a sealed coat. Four bridges (Rạch Tàu, Rạch Vàm, Xóm Mũi, Rạch Bầu Nhỏ) are printed at a 60 km/h design. An upgrade to a real cấp III road, 11 m of asphalt on a 12 m bed, was proposed at about 4,204.1 billion đồng, with a hoped finish in 2027 if approved (S21). That 2027 date is the hope in a 2023 article. It is not a notice that the wide road is open.
- A Vietravel page says the cape is more than 100 km from the city, and that mũi Bãi Bùng is 34 km southwest of thị trấn Năm Căn (S28). A tour page prints 100–110 km and 2.5–3.5 hours by road, and “more than 50 km” on Quốc lộ 1 to Năm Căn plus “more than 50 km” on the Hồ Chí Minh road (S29). Those pairs are not the same as the 58.7 km project, which starts at Năm Căn, not at the city. Keep every figure. Do not add them into one official kilometer.
- A city-to-cape minute was not on the road-project page. The 2.5–3.5 hours is one tour page (S29).
- Motorbikes can use this road. They cannot use the expressway (S18).
- A canoe from Năm Căn is named on the tour page as another way through the mangrove. No official canoe timetable or fare was opened. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
