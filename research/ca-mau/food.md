## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Cua Cà Mau
- Vietnamese name: cua Cà Mau, cua thịt and cua gạch
- What it is: the mangrove crab. The Ngọc Hiển page says meat crab is firm and sweet, and roe crab is full and rich. The airline page says the same pair, firm meat and rich roe (S7, S16).
- How to eat it: steamed, rang me, or as lẩu cua, on the Ngọc Hiển page (S7).
- When to order it: a main meal on the cape day. A season for the best crab was not printed.
- Area, not a shop: Đất Mũi and the city, not a named restaurant.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this page. Do not import a Cần Thơ or Bến Tre crab recipe.
- Sources: S7, S16

### Ba khía muối Rạch Gốc
- Vietnamese name: ba khía, mắm ba khía Rạch Gốc
- What it is: a small crab from the mangrove. The Ngọc Hiển page says it eats fallen mắm fruit, so the meat is firm and the roe is scant. Salted ba khía is the form that page calls the famous one (S7). Người Lao Động, 29 December 2019, says the crabs gather in huge numbers around the tenth lunar month, which is why salting was how people kept them, and that the salting trade of Ngọc Hiển was recognized with the honey-keeping trade as national intangible heritage (S42). VOV says the same recognition for nghề muối ba khía and nghề gác kèo ong (S43).
- How to eat it: with hot rice, on the Ngọc Hiển page (S7). The page also names hấp, rang me, and nướng. A later commercial article describes pulling the shell and mixing with sugar, lime, garlic, chili, and rau răm (S44). That mix is one article, not the office page.
- When to order it: with rice, day or night. The gathering month on the 2019 piece is the tenth lunar month (S42). A 2026 solar date for that month: Check on arrival.
- Area, not a shop: Rạch Gốc and the Ngọc Hiển mangrove. Not a brand.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none. Other deltas have the crab. This page keeps the Rạch Gốc name.
- Sources: S7, S42, S43, S44

### Cá thòi lòi
- Vietnamese name: cá thòi lòi, also cá leo cây
- What it is: the mudskipper of the mangrove flats. The Ngọc Hiển page says it swims, runs on the water and the mud, and climbs. It says the fish is not bony and not strongly fishy (S7). VOV calls it a newer dish that visitors ask for (S43).
- How to eat it: grilled, in a hotpot, kho tiêu, or dried, on the Ngọc Hiển page (S7). The airline page repeats grill, kho tiêu, and hotpot (S16).
- When to order it: a main meal on the cape day.
- Area, not a shop: the mangrove flats of the cape.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S7, S16, S43

### Lẩu mắm U Minh
- Vietnamese name: lẩu mắm U Minh
- What it is: a fermented-fish hotpot. The Ngọc Hiển page says the Cà Mau pot uses mắm cá sặc and chopped lemongrass, with fish, shrimp, squid, and forest greens: đọt nhãn lồng, rau đắng, càng cua, bông súng, đọt choại (S7). The airline page repeats the sặc fish and the lemongrass (S16).
- How to eat it: shared, with the greens in the pot. A noodle from another town was not named.
- When to order it: a main meal on the U Minh day, or back in the city.
- Area, not a shop: U Minh, not a restaurant row.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: lẩu mắm elsewhere in the delta is a relative, not this pot. Do not paste a Châu Đốc recipe here.
- Sources: S7, S16

### Cá lóc nướng U Minh
- Vietnamese name: cá lóc đồng nướng
- What it is: snakehead from the melaleuca water. The national tourism news page says the fish is best from the tenth lunar month through the first lunar month, when the forest water is highest. It quotes the local order “nhất nướng, nhì nấu canh chua, tam kho, tứ luộc”, and says the grill is over straw or tram-wood charcoal, with rau cỏ the, bông súng, and đọt rau muống đồng (S45).
- How to eat it: grilled, with those greens. The other three methods are on the same page.
- When to order it: the lunar window above, if the cook is working. The next solar dates: Check on arrival.
- Area, not a shop: U Minh Hạ.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S45

### Mật ong U Minh
- Vietnamese name: mật ong rừng U Minh, mật ong hoa tràm
- What it is: honey from the melaleuca flowers, taken by gác kèo ong, the rafter set to draw the bees. The national tourism page calls U Minh Hạ the largest tram forest in the country and says the provincial science office registered a shared brand. It also says a Vietnam record for a bee colony was set at the 2022 Hương rừng U Minh festival (S45). The 2019 Người Lao Động piece is the heritage note for the trade (S42).
- How to eat it: by the spoon, or carried home. The pages do not print a café ritual.
- When to order it: the harvest month was not printed on the official pages opened. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Do not use a commercial page that reverses the wet and dry seasons.
- Area, not a shop: U Minh Hạ. A jar in the city market is the souvenir, not a picked stall.
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S42, S45

### Nghêu Khai Long
- Vietnamese name: nghêu Khai Long, nghêu hấp sả
- What it is: the clam of the Khai Long flat. The management board says the meat is thick and not sandy, and that the flat is busiest after Tết through the end of May and June (S10).
- How to eat it: the board’s pot is lemongrass and crushed ginger under the clams, chili if wanted, until the shells open, with muối tiêu chanh or nước mắm chua ngọt, eaten hot (S10).
- When to order it: that harvest window, if the boats are out (S10).
- Area, not a shop: Bãi Khai Long.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S10

### Gỏi nhộng ong
- Vietnamese name: gỏi nhộng ong, also nhộng ong trộn bắp chuối
- What it is: young bee pupae from the U Minh honey harvest. The national tourism article lists it with grilled snakehead, honey, lẩu mắm, and lươn um lá nhàu. It says the pupae were a forest dish and now appear on visitor meals (S45, search record 23 Oct 2026).
- How to eat it: boil the young comb so the wax melts, skim the pupae, then cook. The same article names chiên bột, mắm ong, and especially nhộng ong trộn bắp chuối. Those stay on this dish. They are other preparations of the same pupae.
- When to order it: on the U Minh day. A season was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: U Minh Hạ.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S45

### Lươn um lá nhàu
- Vietnamese name: lươn um lá nhàu
- What it is: freshwater eel braised with nhàu leaves and coconut. The same article says eel of 1–2 kg, wrist-thick, with yellow skin, is still found, and singles out this braise. Other pots on that article are cháo lươn bẹ môn, canh chua, kho mắm, and xào sả ớt (S45, search record 23 Oct 2026).
- How to eat it: clean the slime with wood ash, marinate with scallion, garlic, and salt, layer lemongrass, nhàu, eel, and more nhàu, braise in thin coconut water, finish with coconut cream. The dip is coconut cream, crushed roasted peanuts, tương hột, chili, and fish sauce. The article says the eel is sweet, the coconut is rich, and nhàu is sweet-bitter.
- When to order it: on the U Minh day. A season was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area, not a shop: U Minh Hạ.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S45

- What the visitor does: Moves among boats selling produce on the Gành Hào, in the center, as UNESCO describes it, or watches that market from the bank (S1). The city handbook calls it a place to eat and to look, and does not name stalls (S13).

### Rạch Gốc
- Vietnamese name: Rạch Gốc
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats ba khía where the crab is named, with rice. The culture office sits at khóm 8, thị trấn Rạch Gốc, on the 2024 page (S7). A market hall number and an open hour were not on that page.
- When to go: with the cape day. Hour: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area: Rạch Gốc, the old Ngọc Hiển town
- Suggested slug: rach-goc
- Photo must show: the town waterway and a plate of ba khía with rice, the town still in the frame.
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S7
