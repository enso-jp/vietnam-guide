## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Folded grilled fish
- Vietnamese name: Pa pỉnh tộp
- What it is: Thái grilled fish, folded. The culture desk says “pa” is fish and “pa pỉnh tộp” is the folded grill. Black Thái cooks use a fish of 1–1.5 kg, open it along the back, salt it, and stuff it with mắc khén, roasted fresh chili, spring onion, herbs, and coriander, then clamp it with bamboo and grill it (S17). Vietnam News repeats the 1–1.5 kg fish and the same stuffing, and names chẩm chéo as a separate sauce beside the fish (S18). The culture desk does not use the words chẩm chéo in that recipe. Keep the stuffing on the culture page and the sauce name on the news page. Do not merge them into one line
- How to eat it: From the grill, with the herbs still green. A city instruction beyond those two pages — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- When to order it: The Thái meal, midday or evening. A stall hour — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: The Thái table in the basin, and bản Phiêng Lơi when a house is serving (S18). Not a named restaurant
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site as its own dish file. Do not borrow a Mộc Châu plate
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S17, S18

### Upland sticky rice
- Vietnamese name: Xôi nếp nương Điện Biên
- What it is: Upland sticky rice. The culture desk says the grains are steamed twice in a wooden steamer, and that leaves, tubers, and fruit give red, purple, yellow, and green (S17). It does not say five colors and it does not name the plants. The dye plants — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. `xoi-ngu-sac` is the Ba Be tray. That dish page says upland sticky rice is a different bowl. Do not file this rice as that slug
- How to eat it: With the grilled fish or grilled pork, or as a packet of rice carried on the road. The culture desk says a ball of it leaves the hand clean (S17). The packet is not cơm lam
- When to order it: With the main meal, or as something to carry. A breakfast stall that sells only this — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: The Thái table. Not a shop name

### Kitchen-smoked buffalo
- Vietnamese name: Thịt trâu gác bếp on S40. S18 uses the English “kitchen-smoked buffalo meat” and does not print a Vietnamese name
- What it is: Buffalo dried over a fire. S18: free-range hill buffalo, soaked with mắc khén, salt, lemongrass leaves, chilli, sugar, and garlic for two hours, hung on bamboo skewers over a wood fire for about 15 hours, brown-red. - How to eat it: Warm, or resteamed on the S40 account. A shop — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED

- Area, not a shop: No street on S18 or S40

## Food streets and markets

### Muong Thanh walking street
- Vietnamese name: Phố đi bộ Mường Thanh
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Walks the river street for a local snack if a booth is open. The city paper on 23 Mar 2026 says most booths were not trading yet, outside Ban flower week, and that a few drink stalls at the head of the street were the stop (S19). The festival press note planned a food zone called “Hương sắc Điện Biên” on the walking-street ground during 6–12 Mar 2026 (S14). That zone was a festival layout, not a proven nightly market
- When to go: Evening, and weekend daytime on one trader’s clock in the city paper (S19). A bylaw hour — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area: Beside the Nậm Rốm, phường Điện Biên Phủ
- Suggested slug: muong-thanh-walking-street
- Photo must show: Stalls or the drink tables, the street, and the river in one frame
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S14, S19

### Central Market I
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Trung tâm I
- Kind: food-street

A later city-paper piece, republished, places it in phường Tân Thanh, says it opened in 1992, and counts 150 kiosks (S30). Tân Thanh is inside phường Điện Biên Phủ from 1 Jul 2025 (S22). - Area: Old phường Tân Thanh, now phường Điện Biên Phủ
