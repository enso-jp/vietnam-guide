## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Banh da cua
- Vietnamese name: Bánh đa cua (also called canh bánh đa on the 2021 article)
- What it is: Red rice crackers and crab, either đồng or bể. A full bowl on the 2023 map adds bề bề, chả lá lốt, shrimp, and fish cake in a crab broth (S11). The 2021 article adds giò, chả quế, peeled tôm rảo, chí chương (the Hoa name for chili sauce), chopped herbs, fried shallot, and lime or kumquat. The crackers come from Dư Hàng Kênh. They are described as thin, soft, and chewy (S12).
- How to eat it: In a bowl, at a pavement table. The 2021 article says the sign is often a small card tied to a tree (S12).
- When to order it: The 2023 map puts it at lunch and says it is sold all day (S11). The 2021 article calls it the common breakfast and the common evening meal (S12). Both stay.
- Area, not a shop: Across the center. Streets named below, not a single counter.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none on another slug. Chả rươi is the former Hải Dương bowl, not this one (S19).
- Sources: S11, S12, S19

### Bun ca cay
- Vietnamese name: Bún cá cay
- What it is: A hot fish noodle bowl. The map says a full bowl has close to ten toppings from the fish, including offal and fish maw, plus banana blossom and dọc mùng, in a light broth with a chili note (S11).
- How to eat it: As a morning bowl.
- When to order it: Breakfast, on that map (S11).
- Area, not a shop: The map points at Lê Lợi and Lê Lai among other counters. Counters are notes, not picks.

### Banh mi cay
- Vietnamese name: Bánh mì cay
- What it is: A small spicy sandwich, eaten with chí chương. The training speech lists it among the city’s food marks (S19, S11). On 23 December 2012 the city portal describes a piece about two fingertips wide, with pate, red chili sauce, charcoal, then optional fat and seasoning, crisp and hot (S25). Ban Tuyên giáo và Dân vận also calls it a small crisp stick with pate and chí chương, and prints no date (S29).
- Price: Cổng du lịch Hải Phòng prints 2,500 đồng a piece on 24 July 2023 (S11). The city portal prints 5,000–7,000 đồng on 23 December 2012 (S25). Both stay. Do not pick one.
- How to eat it: To carry, with trà cúc.
- When to order it: Morning, and as something to take home, on the 2023 map (S11). Also the end of the working day in winter, on 23 December 2012 (S25).
- Area, not a shop: Lê Lợi, Minh Khai, and Phan Bội Châu (S11). Ngã ba Khánh Nạp on Hàng Kênh, Lê Lợi, and Đinh Tiên Hoàng (S25). Both stay.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link:
- Sources: S11, S19, S25, S29

### Tra cuc
- Vietnamese name: Trà cúc
- What it is: A drink of chrysanthemum, liquorice, and kumquat. The map calls the taste bitter, sweet, and astringent at once, and compares the role to Hà Nội’s trà chanh without making them the same drink (S11).
- How to eat it: In a glass, any time of day, often with bánh mì cay.
- Price: 20,000–25,000 đồng a glass on 24 July 2023 (S11). The hour is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to order it: Any time (S11).
- Area, not a shop: Phan Bội Châu, which the map calls phố trà cúc (S11).
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: Hanoi trà chanh is the comparison the map makes, not a dish to copy onto this page.
- Sources: S11
