# Attractions — Mui Ne

Slug: `mui-ne`
Checked: 23 Oct 2026

Province on the pages: Vietnam Tourism’s coast page now says the former Binh Thuan coast is part of Lam Dong (S5). The food page and Decision 1772 still say Binh Thuan (S3, S9). Vietnamese Wikipedia dates the provincial merger to Resolution 202/2025/QH15 of 12 Jun 2025, and the new Mui Ne ward to Resolution 1671/NQ-UBTVQH15 of 16 Jun 2025, merging the old Ham Tien ward, the old Mui Ne ward, and Thien Nghiep (S20). Population figures disagree and both stay: about 25,000, and 24,275 in 1999 on 35.41 km², for the older ward (S19); 50,166 people on 31 Dec 2024 across 118.59 km² for the merged ward (S20). GeoNames prints 50,166 on the populated-place record (S21).

## Why a night here

### The kite, the sand, and a thin dune on the skyline

- The picture they expect: A long beach, a kite over the water, and sand underfoot. Vietnam Tourism’s picture of the place is the fishing harbor at dawn, the red dunes at sunset, the white dunes at sunrise, and the wind on the beach (S1, S2, S4). The published hero is that beach: wet sand in the foreground, a person holding a yellow-and-black kite in the surf, the sea behind, and a low scrub-covered ridge on the horizon. The ridge reads as dune, and it is only a band under the sky.
- Where they sleep, as an area: The beach strip. Decision 1772 puts medium and small resorts, and the night, in the Mui Ne–Ham Tien sea zone of about 340 ha, and smaller rooms in the ward centers and the fishing village (S9). English Wikipedia says the built-up sand is Rang Beach in Ham Tien, often called Mui Ne Beach, west of the fishing ward, which has Ganh and Suoi Nuoc (S19). No hotel brand.
- Where the evening is: The sea wall of seafood grills by the fishing village, which Vietnam Tourism calls “bo ke” (S1, S3). Nguyễn Đình Chiểu is the restaurant road from the center of Mui Ne toward the village; Tuyên Quang is the other named street (S3). The 2018 plan names night activity in the Mui Ne sea zone and does not name a bar street (S9).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): The fishing harbor at dawn, https://vietnam.travel/things-to-do/wind-sand-and-sea-mui-nes-must-do-list (S1). The sea-wall grills on that same page (S1). Pit Stop Food Court, named on Nguyễn Đình Chiểu with no street number, as the court that also has a bar and a nightclub, https://www.vietnam.travel/vi/things-to-do/mui-ne-foodies-fantasy (S3). Address of the court: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. Pins, not a ranked bar list. Coco Beach and Flyboard Mui Ne are named on S1 and stay in `food.md` under notes. They are not picks.
- Hero must include: The sand, the water, and either the dune ridge or the kite line, subject through the center. If the frame is the kite, the whole kite and the wet sand stay in the frame, with the horizon ridge still visible. If the frame is the dune, the slope has to read as a dune, not a dirt path.
- A crop that would fail: A tight crop on the kitesurfer and the middle of the kite cuts the dune. On the published hero the dune is the far ridge, so any crop that sits on the person drops it. The same crop can also cut the kite tips. Empty sky, empty sand, or a slice of a torso fails. The published red-dune frame is a separate picture: orange sand, casuarinas, a walker, power lines, and a watermark, with no sea. A tight crop on that walker cuts the dune slope.

## Attractions (Sight, area of interests, zone, hub, etc.)

### Resort strip
- Vietnamese name: Not one name on the pages. English Wikipedia calls the built sand Rang Beach, in Ham Tien, and says foreigners call it Mui Ne Beach (S19). Decision 1772 calls the zone the Mui Ne sea tourism zone, Mui Ne and Ham Tien (S9).
- Kind: area
- Area: Ham Tien and the west side of the bay, the road toward the fishing ward
- What the visitor does: Sleeps on the sand, uses a day pass at a beachfront property, swims, or takes a kite lesson (S1, S4, S9). The kite window on the adventure page is 11:00–16:00, October to April (S4). The must-do page says the wind is gentler in the morning and stronger in the late afternoon (S1). The sports page says the breeze is year-round (S2). All three stay.
- Time: A beach day. A clock for the swim was not printed.
- Fee: The sand is not given a ticket on these pages. Day pass and lesson: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- From the base: This is the base if the night is on the strip (S9).
- When to go: Morning for a first kite lesson (S1). Late morning through afternoon for the thermal wind (S4).
- Mistake to avoid: Calling the whole strip the fishing ward. The ward’s own beaches on the English Wikipedia page are Ganh and Suoi Nuoc; Rang is Ham Tien (S19).
- Local interest: Beach, kite
- Suggested slug: resort-strip
- Suggested dot: 5 — the reason to stay the night
- Photo must show: Sand, surf, and a kite line in one frame, subject through the center, with the horizon ridge still in the frame if the lens can hold it. A tight crop cuts that ridge.
- Unpublished: the published hero is this beach. A second licensed frame of the resort road was not stored in this pass.
- Sources: S1, S2, S4, S9, S19

### Red dunes
- Vietnamese name: Đồi cát bay. The workshop report uses that name for the pink flying dunes (S10). Vietnam Tourism says red dunes (S1, S2).
- Kind: sight
- Area: Mui Ne ward, Phan Thiet, about 22 km from the city center on the 2019 ministry page (S10). Vietnam Tourism says just outside town (S1). Those origins are not the same sentence. Both stay.
- What the visitor does: Walks the orange slope and sandboards at sunset (S1, S2). The 2019 report says the field is about 36.8 ha and, since 2016, was assigned to the provincial youth outdoor center, overlapping the Ham Tien–Mui Ne tourism board and the ward (S10).
- Time: Sunset (S1). A minute count: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The ministry page names the manager and prints no tariff (S10).
- From the base: A ride from the strip. Minutes: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The 22 km figure is from the city center, not from a resort gate (S10).
- When to go: Late day, when Vietnam Tourism sends visitors for the orange slope (S1).
- Mistake to avoid: Treating this as the white field. The white dunes are Bàu Trắng, in Hoa Thang, and the drive figures disagree (S4, S9, S1).
- Local interest: Photography, dunes
- Suggested slug: red-dunes
- Suggested dot: 4 — the sunset picture, paired with a beach day
- Photo must show: The red slope filling the frame, a person for scale, subject through the center. A tight crop on the person cuts the dune. The published frame also shows power lines and a watermark and does not show the sea.
- Sources: S1, S2, S10

### White dunes
- Vietnamese name: Bàu Trắng. Also called the white sand dunes (S4, S5). Bàu Sen is named with it for protection and is not given a separate visit on these pages (S9).
- Kind: sight
- Area: Hoa Thang, Bac Binh, inside the national tourism zone, called the symbol of Mui Ne (S9). Not the fishing ward.
- What the visitor does: Climbs the pale slopes, looks at a lotus lake, rides an ATV or a jeep, or sandboards (S2, S4). The adventure page prints steep 30-metre dunes (S4). No other page opened here prints a height.
- Time: Be there 5:00–7:00. The same page says set an alarm for 4:30 if the plan is an ATV at sunrise (S4).
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. No tariff on the decision or the tourism pages (S4, S9).
- From the base: Distance disagrees and both stay. The must-do page prints a 25 km drive (S1). The adventure page prints a short drive from the main beach strip (S4). Minutes: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: Sunrise. Midday sand is scorching (S4).
- Mistake to avoid: Using the red-dune sunset as this trip. The white field is the early ride (S1, S4).
- Local interest: Photography, dunes
- Suggested slug: white-dunes
- Suggested dot: 4 — the dawn picture, a ride from the strip, not the swim
- Photo must show: The pale slope and the crest in one frame, people small on the sand, subject the dune. A tight crop on the people cuts the dune.
- Sources: S1, S2, S4, S5, S9

### Po Sah Inu towers
- Vietnamese name: Tháp Pô Sah Inư. Also Poshanu and Pho Hai Cham towers (S16).
- Kind: sight
- Area: Ba Nai Hill, Phu Hai ward, about 7 km northeast of Phan Thiet center (S16). This is the neighbor’s hill, on the road between the city and the strip, not the fishing ward. The catalogue address disagrees and both stay: Km9 Nguyễn Thông, Phường Phú Thuỷ, Lâm Đồng (S17); Phường Phú Hài, Phan Thiết, Bình Thuận (S18); the English intro leaves the address blank (S16).
- What the visitor does: Walks the three brick towers. The main tower holds a Linga-Yoni altar. A stone bull once in the middle tower is described as gone (S16). Vietnam Tourism calls them 1,200-year-old Hoa Lai national monuments (S5).
- Time: The English catalogue says no limit at the point; the older catalogue says 120 minutes. Both stay (S16, S18).
- Fee: 7,000–15,000 VND on S16, S17, and S18. None of the three splits adult and child. Do not pick one end of the range.
- Hours: 7:00–17:00 on S16 and S18. 7:00–18:00 on S17. Both stay.
- From the base: A stop on the way in from Phan Thiet. Minutes from the strip: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to go: Daytime inside the hours above. Katê morning is the ceremony day, not an ordinary visit (S12).
- Mistake to avoid: Filing the towers under the fishing ward. The pages place them in Phu Hai / Phu Thuy (S16, S17, S18).
- Local interest: Cham tower on the way in
- Suggested slug: po-sah-inu
- Suggested dot: 3 — a stop on the ride in, not the reason for the night
- Photo must show: The brick towers on the hill, full height, subject through the center. A tight crop cuts the crown.
- Unpublished: no licensed photo stored in this pass.
- Sources: S5, S11, S12, S16, S17, S18

## Lifestyle

### Dawn harbor, then the wind, then the wall
- Vietnamese name: Not a separate place name
- Kind: lifestyle
- What a day here actually is: Just after dawn, the fishing-village market, then cards and coffee (S1). The rest of the morning is the beach or a first kite lesson while the wind is gentler (S1). From 11:00 to 16:00 the adventure page puts the stronger kite wind on the strip (S4). Dinner is the sea wall (S1). The white-dune dawn is the other morning, and it replaces the harbor if the jeep leaves at 4:30 (S4).
- When: Dawn market; kite 11:00–16:00 on the adventure page, or morning for beginners on the must-do page. Both stay (S1, S4). The hour the strip goes quiet was not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Area: Fishing ward at dawn, Ham Tien sand in the day, sea wall at night
- Suggested slug: ordinary-day
- Photo must show: The same person-scale beach as the hero, sand and water, not a resort lobby
- Sources: S1, S3, S4
