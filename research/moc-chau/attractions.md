# Research dossier — Moc Chau

Slug: `moc-chau`
Province: Sơn La. 2026 news still prints tỉnh Sơn La and uses phường Mộc Châu and phường Thảo Nguyên (S9, S11). The 28 Oct 2024 open-data row and the older national district page still print huyện Mộc Châu and thị trấn (S2, S6). Both wordings stay. This pass did not open a merger resolution.
Kind: town
Corridor: northern-midlands
Base a traveler sleeps in: The farm-town strip on the plateau, and Thai stilt houses at Bản Áng. Not Mai Chau. Not Son La city unless that is already the night.
Checked: 21 Oct 2026

Moc Chau is the Sơn La plateau past the National Highway 6 pass. Mai Chau (`mai-chau`) is the valley before that pass. A Mai Chau rice-and-stilt photograph does not show this place.

## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: Tea rows on a high plateau, mist or low cloud, pasture, and in winter a white plum blossom over a valley. Vietnam Tourism calls it the Green Pearl, about 200 km from Hanoi, a plateau about 80 km long and 25 km wide, rising over 1,000 m (S1). The national district page puts the plateau at 1,050 m (S2). The heritage monograph puts the average plateau at 1,150–1,300 m and the limestone from 1,050 m (S7). A TITC feature says about 1,500 m (S5). Record all four. Do not pick one height for the page.
- Where they sleep, as an area: Small hotels and homestays in the farm town, and stilt-house stays in Thai villages. TITC names homestays and cooking with the family (S5). No hotel brand.
- Where the evening is: The walking street and night market around the Pha Luong light park (S8). Festival nights move to Quảng trường 08-5 in phường Mộc Châu (S9, S10).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): The walking street, Bao Son La, 27 Jan 2023, https://baosonla.vn/du-lich/trai-nghiem-pho-di-bo-va-cho-dem-moc-chau-jaYLhhTVR.html (S8). Square 08-5, VietnamPlus, https://www.vietnamplus.vn/moc-chau-san-sang-cho-tuan-van-hoa-du-lich-nam-2026-post1131600.vnp (S9). They are pins, not a ranked bar list.
- Hero must include: Contour tea rows, a valley or a cloud bank, and a path or a person through the center, in color. In blossom weeks the same rule is white flowers, the valley, and a path, not a branch filling the frame.
- A crop that would fail: Empty fog, a leaf macro, a costume portrait with no hill, a glass-bridge selfie with no plateau, or a Mai Chau rice valley.

### Dai Yem Waterfall
- Vietnamese name: Thác Dải Yếm. Local names in the monograph: Thác Ta Lét, thác Bản Vặt (S7). The national page also says thác Nàng (S4).
- Kind: sight
- Area: Bản Vặt, xã Mường Sang, on National Highway 43. The 2022 provincial recognition still uses that address (S6). Ward names after 2025 — Check on arrival
- What the visitor does: Walks down to a limestone fall in two parts, with a pool under the lower water. Vietnam Tourism says you hear it before you see it, and that a red Love Glass Bridge (Cầu kính Tình yêu) crosses above it (S1). The 2011 monograph describes stair paths and does not mention a glass bridge (S7). The bridge is that later tourism feature, not the monograph.
- Time: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED for a fixed visit length
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The 18 Jan 2022 decision 111/QĐ-UBND recognizes it as a provincial eco site managed by a company (S6). No tariff was on that row.
- From the base: The monograph gives two drives. Toward Son La, about 3 km on National Highway 6 to the Long Sap junction, then left about 5 km on National Highway 43. Toward Hanoi, about 1 km to that junction, then right about 5 km (S7). The national page says 3 km from the farm town, and also about 4 km from the Pa Hang junction (S4). Record both route descriptions.
- When to go: National page: fullest April–September, when it describes a 70 m-wide white sheet (S4). Monograph: May–September for the first fall’s lip, and April–September for the second (S7).
- Mistake to avoid: Publishing one height. Vietnam Tourism says the drop is over 100 m (S1). The monograph says the first fall drops about 70 m and is in three tiers, with a first tier about 40 m deep, about 70 m wide, and about 50 m high, and puts the two falls about 200 m apart (S7). The national page puts the two falls 150 m apart (S4). Record every figure. Do not pick one.
- Local interest: Limestone falls
- Suggested slug: `dai-yem-waterfall`
- Suggested dot: 5 — the waterfall the plateau is known for
- Photo must show: The white fall, the rock lip, and the pool or the forest in one color frame, subject through the center. A bridge selfie that crops out the water fails.
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S1, S4, S6, S7
