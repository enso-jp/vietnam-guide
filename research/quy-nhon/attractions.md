## Why a night here

### The crescent, then the bridge

- The picture they expect: Wake up on the city beach. A long crescent of yellow sand, water that the portal calls fairly clear and blue, and the buildings along the front, because the sand is in the center and a short walk from it (S3). The other picture, the one a tight crop ruins, is Thị Nại bridge after the lamps are on, a white span across the lagoon (S6, S7).
- Where they sleep, as an area: Beachfront rooms along that crescent. The quieter southern sand is Quy Hòa and the fishing village at Bãi Xếp, 13 km down the coast road (S12, S15). No hotel brand.
- Where the evening is: Xuân Diệu, the beach road, for seafood and a cafe on the sand (S24, S38). Nguyễn Tất Thành square for the walk after dark (S4, S24). The bridge when the lamps are lit (S6).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): The city beach, https://dulichquynhon.binhdinh.gov.vn/vi/bienquynhon (S3). Nguyễn Tất Thành square, 6.5 ha on the avenue of that name, https://dulichquynhon.binhdinh.gov.vn/vi/quangtruongnguyentatthanh (S4). Thị Nại bridge at night, https://dulichquynhon.binhdinh.gov.vn/vi/cauthinai (S6). Horse carts leave from Xuân Diệu near the square (S24). They are pins, not a bar list. The carriage fare is not printed.
- Hero must include: The surf, the yellow sand, and the buildings along Xuân Diệu in one frame, subject through the center. If the frame can hold the bridge, the full span and the water, not a lamp. A standing figure is the wrong hero. The 17 m Amitabha at Long Khánh loses the lotus if the crop takes only the face (S10).
- A crop that would fail: Empty sky, empty sand, a hotel sign, fog with no shoreline, or a slice of the bridge railing.
