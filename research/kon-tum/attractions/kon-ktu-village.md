## What the visitor does

- The scene, in order: Walk the lanes of stilt houses, the communal house in the middle, and the wooden church in the same yard. Weaving, gongs, and the xoang dance are village activities, not a daily included show (A1, A2, A3).
- How long on site: 2–3 hours on the Go Vietnam cluster page only (A4). One publisher. Public duration: Check on arrival.
- How long including the ride from the base: Minutes UNSOURCED — Check on arrival.
- Mistake that wastes the visit: Entering a house without an invitation. Confusing this communal house with the Kon Klor hall on the other bank.
- Notice, if one fact would waste the day: None that is sourced beyond the unsourced gate.

## Getting there

### From central Kon Tum

- Mode: Motorbike or taxi, toward the Kon Klor bridge, then along the river (A1).
- Time: UNSOURCED — Check on arrival.
- Fare, if any: UNSOURCED — Check on arrival. A4’s 30,000–100,000 VND taxi band is one publisher and covers the whole cluster, so it stays off the public fare.
- Source, or UNSOURCED: Distances disagree. Do not average.
  - About 5 km from the centre, across the Kon Klor bridge, then about 1 km more into the village (A4).
  - About 6 km from the centre (A2). About 6 km east (A3).
  - About 10 km east of central Kon Tum, and about 6 km along the riverside road after heading for the bridge (A1). Both figures are on A1.

### From Pleiku Airport (PXU)

- Mode: Bus or taxi into Kon Tum, then on to the village.
- Time: About an hour (S1, stored 21 Oct 2026, not re-fetched). A4 also says 1 to 1.5 hours by car and about 1.5 hours by bus.
- Fare, if any: Bus 80,000–120,000 VND on A4 only. Public fare: Check on arrival.
- Source, or UNSOURCED: Nearly 45 km (S1) and 51 km (S3), stored 21 Oct 2026. A4 says 50 km. All three stay.

## Season

- Sight-specific months, or leave to the place: A1 says the dry season, about November to April, when the road is easy and the river is calm for boating. That matches the place window. Leave bestMonths unset.
- One concrete climate fact, if a source gives it: November to April for the river road (A1).

## Other figures, not averaged

- Communal-house height: higher than 13 m (A2, A3). 22 m, and called the second-tallest in the Central Highlands after Plei Rông at 24 m (A4). Height figures both stay. The ranking claim stays out of public copy.
- Households and people: 92 households and more than 530 people (A1). More than 100 households (A2). 138 households and more than 736 people (A3). All stay. Do not pick one.
- Age: more than 300 years (A2, A3). A3 quotes an elder: Kon means village and K’tu means old. A2 says K’tu means nguyên sơ. The glosses disagree. Public copy uses the age, not the gloss.
- A3’s 1968 move of the old village, about 3 km, is one article. Left out of public copy.
- “Oldest and most beautiful” on A2 and A3. Skip the superlative.
