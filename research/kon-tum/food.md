## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Dry pho (two bowls)
- Vietnamese name: Phở khô (phở hai tô)
- What it is: Sun-dried rice noodles tossed with meat and fermented black soybean sauce, with hot broth in a second bowl. Chicken or beef. Go Vietnam traces the two-bowl service to Pleiku around 1959 and says it was recognized for Asian culinary value in 2012 (S10).
- How to eat it: Toss the dry bowl, then alternate bites with sips of broth (S10).
- When to order it: Morning, about 6:00 to 10:00 (S10).
- Area, not a shop: Kon Tum market and central streets, including Phan Chu Trinh, Phan Đình Phùng, and Tô Hiến Thành (S10). Do not publish the shop names on that article as picks.
- Price: about 20,000–33,000 VND a bowl, up to 35,000 VND for a large bowl, 2025 reference (S10). Single source for the price.
- Meal: breakfast
- Sources: S10

### Leaf salad
- Vietnamese name: Gỏi lá
- What it is: A platter of forest and garden leaves rolled into a funnel with pork, shrimp, and pork skin. Not established as a Ba Na dish. S14 does not say Ba Na.
- How to eat it: Funnel of a large leaf, then more leaves, pork, shrimp, pork skin, sauce, salt, and pepper. One bite (S14, S15, S16, S23, S24).
- Leaf counts, do not average: up to 40 (S16); 40–50 (S14); about 30, and 50–60 in the rains / about 30 in the dry season (S15); at least 30 and up to 70, with the dry season fullest and the rainy season about 30 (S24); 30–40 (S25). S15 and S24 disagree on which season has more leaves.
- Fillings disagree: S16 includes carp and mẹ; S14 includes carp and satay; S15 has dry-fried shrimp and no carp; S23 has boiled shrimp and wine-lees sauce; S25 adds rice paper.
- When to order it: Year-round (S15). Lunch or dinner.
- Area, not a shop: Trần Cao Vân (S15, S23, S24). S14 does not name the street.
- Price, do not average: 100,000–150,000 VND for about 3–4 people (S15); about 300,000–400,000 VND for 6–8 people (S23); 350,000–600,000 VND for 4–6 people (S25). A single-person price is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Meal: main
- Sources: S14, S15, S16, S23, S24, S25

### Highland red noodles
- Vietnamese name: Bún đỏ cao nguyên
- What it is: Field crab, cake balls, and boiled quail eggs. S16 says fish-cake balls and a tomato-and-cashew broth. S14 does not print tomato or cashew, and adds crispy pork fat (S14, S16).
- How to eat it: With rau cần đước, bean sprouts, and scallion oil or scallion fat (S14, S16).
- When to order it: VietnamNet says a cool highland afternoon (S14).
- Area, not a shop: Kon Tum. No street printed.
- Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Meal: main
- Sources: S14, S16

### Weaver-ant fish
- Vietnamese name: Cá gỏi kiến vàng
- What it is: Rơ Măm minced stream fish set under a weaver-ant nest so the ants’ secretion works on the meat, then ant eggs (S14, S16).
- How to eat it: With forest leaves or sung leaves, after about 30 minutes (S16) or half an hour (S14).
- Area, not a shop: Rơ Măm communities. No city street printed. Do not add Sa Thầy from a commercial blog.
- Price: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Meal: main
- Sources: S14, S16

### Bamboo-tube rice
- Vietnamese name: Cơm lam
- What it is: Glutinous rice grilled in bamboo. Ba Na and Xơ Đăng, often at festivals, with grilled chicken (S18). Cluster: Khẩu Cẩm rice, stream water, one hour on charcoal, with black sesame salt and stir-fried chicken (S4).
- How to eat it: Split the tube, keep the membrane, eat hot (S18).
- Price, do not average: 30,000–50,000 VND a tube (S4); 20,000–30,000 VND a tube (S19); common highland 2025 range about 10,000–25,000 VND depending on size and region, with Pleiku and the Buôn Ma Thuột road named, not a Kon Tum stall (S18).
- Area, not a shop: Ba Na hamlets around Kon Tum, Kon K’Tu, festivals. Măng Đen for the pairing (S19, S25).
- Meal: main
- Sources: S4, S18, S19, S25

### Grilled village chicken
- Vietnamese name: Gà nướng
- What it is: Small free-range bird grilled whole, eaten with cơm lam. Ba Na and Xơ Đăng (S19). Cluster marinade is jar wine, garlic, lemongrass, lime leaves, and chili salt, 30–40 minutes, Kon K’Tu (S4). The dedicated article uses lemongrass, wild chili, salt, garlic, shallots, sometimes forest honey, and a dip of green-chili salt, wild-pepper salt, or muối é (S19).
- Price, do not average: 280,000–400,000 VND for a 1.2 kg bird, enough for 4 (S4); about 220,000–280,000 VND a bird and 150,000–250,000 VND for a set (S19, 2025 reference); Măng Đen Sedang set with é leaf and forest honey 350,000–600,000 VND for 4 (S25).
- Area, not a shop: Kon Tum by the Đăk Bla, Kon K’Tu, Măng Đen. Do not publish Cô Sinh, Phố Núi, or Cơm Lam Dakbla as picks (S19).
- Meal: main
- Sources: S4, S17, S19, S25

### Jar wine
- Vietnamese name: Rượu cần (rượu ghè)
- What it is: Undistilled grain and forest-leaf yeast in a jar, drunk through straws. Xơ Đăng, Ba Na, Gia Rai, Giẻ Triêng, Brâu, and others (S20). Cluster: Khẩu Cẩm, 6–12 months, 8–10 straws (S4).
- How to eat it: Communal, at the nhà rông, with gongs and xoang. Water is poured in and topped up (S20). Festival and ceremony drink, not breakfast.
- Price, do not average: 100,000–200,000 VND per 5-litre jar (S4); about 175,000 VND for a 4-litre container from one maker (S20); 350,000–600,000 VND for a Sedang jar for 6–10, and 3–7 percent alcohol on that article only (S25).
- Area, not a shop: Communal houses, festivals, district markets. Do not publish maker names as picks.
- Meal: drink
- Sources: S4, S20, S25

### Highland coffee
- Vietnamese name: Cà phê
- What it is: Vietnam Tourism calls Kon Tum the coffee hub and the Central Highlands bean Robusta. Dak Ha, just north of downtown, over 9,000 hectares / over 22,000 acres. Cà phê sữa đá is a phin drip with condensed milk and ice (S11). Go Vietnam’s cluster article calls the town cup arabica (S4). The Măng Đen article also says arabica at 1,200 m (S25). Do not collapse the bean to one variety.
- How to eat it: At a café, morning or after the river walk. S11 describes cafés overlooking the Đăk Bla. S11 does not name Trần Phú.
- Price, do not average: S11 prints no cup price. S4 prints 25,000–40,000 VND a cup and 200,000–400,000 VND/kg roasted ground beans. S25 prints 30,000–60,000 VND in Măng Đen.
- Area, not a shop: City cafés and Dak Ha. Do not publish Indochine, Eva, or the other café names on S11 as picks.
- Meal: drink
- Sources: S4, S11, S25

### Cassava-leaf soup
- Vietnamese name: Canh lá mì
- What it is: Young cassava leaves pounded and simmered with bitter eggplant. Jrai, Ba Na, Xơ Đăng, and Ê Đê, including the former Kon Tum area (S21). The city festival list names lá mì and does not print a recipe (S17).
- How to eat it: Hot, with rice and grilled dried fish (S21).
- Price: Bowl in Kon Tum is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. S21’s leaf prices are highland-market bunches, and its shops are Pleiku and Buôn Ma Thuột.
- Meal: soup
- Sources: S17, S21
