# Food — Son La

Checked: 22 Oct 2026

The city table is a Thái hearth: stream fish, smoked meat, bamboo-tube rice, jar wine. Mộc Châu yogurt, hau plum, and plateau tea stay on `moc-chau`. No stall prices. Where a dish already has a site slug, link it. Do not file a second dish.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Grilled stream fish
- Vietnamese name: Pa pỉnh tộp
- What it is: Thái grilled stream fish. The museum handbook says the fish is split along the back, stuffed with ginger, lemongrass, and mắc khén, then grilled and eaten with sticky rice (S6). The national tourism desk names the same dish at Bản Mòng, beside chẩm chéo, the dip of roasted salt, grilled chili, mắc khén, garlic, and herbs (S18)
- How to eat it: From the grill, with sticky rice and chẩm chéo. A city recipe card beyond those two descriptions — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- When to order it: The Bản Mòng meal, midday or evening (S17, S18). Stall hours in town — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: Bản Mòng, and the town table. Not a named restaurant
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: `pa-pinh-top` on `moc-chau`. Do not file another slug
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S6, S17, S18

### Hearth-smoked buffalo
- Vietnamese name: Thịt trâu gác bếp
- What it is: Buffalo dried over the hearth. The handbook says the meat is seasoned with forest chili, pepper, and mắc khén, black outside and still red inside (S6). Bản Mòng’s menus call the same family thịt hun khói (S17, S18)
- How to eat it: With rice and rượu cần, at the stilt-house meal or packed from a market. A drying time — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- When to order it: With the Bản Mòng meal, or as something to carry. The handbook lists it as a gift because it keeps (S6)
- Area, not a shop: Bản Mòng, and the highland goods at the town market. No stall is a pick
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: `thit-hun-khoi` on `moc-chau`. Do not file another slug
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S6, S17, S18

### Bamboo-tube rice
- Vietnamese name: Cơm lam
- What it is: Sticky rice cooked in a bamboo tube, closed with banana leaf or fresh leaf, then heated on a wood fire (S6). Bản Mòng serves it with the grilled fish (S17, S18)
- How to eat it: From the tube, with the fish or the smoked meat
- When to order it: The village meal. A breakfast stall that sells only this — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: Bản Mòng, and the town table
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: `com-lam` on `moc-chau`. The plate already filed for that dish was not taken here. Do not file a second cơm lam
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S6, S17, S18

### Nam pia
- Vietnamese name: Nậm pịa
- What it is: A Thái offal dish. The handbook says it is set from cow or goat blood, with tail, stomach, and the base of the heart, plus the thick liquid from the small intestine, cooked until it tightens. Eaten with banana flower and mint, and with rượu (S6)
- How to eat it: Hot, from the pot, with the herbs. The handbook says it is an acquired taste
- When to order it: A home or hamlet meal. A shop that posts it every day — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: The Thái table in this province. The handbook does not pin a street
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none. This slug is not on `moc-chau`
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S6

### Dao sour pork
- Vietnamese name: Thịt chua người Dao
- What it is: Dao belly pork, scored, salted, packed with leftover rice, and soured in a jar. Eaten with lá lốt. The handbook ties it to feast days and to guests (S6)
- How to eat it: From the jar, with the herb. A souring time — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- When to order it: The handbook says Tết and guest meals, not a daily stall (S6)
- Area, not a shop: Dao households. No ward was named
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S6

### Calf quick-fried in oil
- Vietnamese name: Bê chao
- What it is: Sliced calf, seasoned with ginger, lemongrass, and pepper, passed through hot oil (S6). That is one sentence in the 2020 handbook. A second method was not found
- How to eat it: As that hot plate. Do not invent a dipping sauce
- When to order it: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area, not a shop: The provincial table in the handbook. Not pinned to a city street
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: `be-chao` on `moc-chau`, where the method was left open. This handbook sentence is the only method recorded here. Do not file a second slug
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S6

### Jar wine
- Vietnamese name: Rượu cần
- What it is: The jar on the Bản Mòng floor, drunk while the xòe is danced (S18). The handbook’s opening also puts the visitor in that wine (S6)
- How to eat it: From the jar, through the pipes, with the meal. A strength or a price — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- When to order it: The evening at the stilt house, not as a morning drink
- Area, not a shop: Bản Mòng
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: `ruou-can` on `moc-chau`. Do not file another slug
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S6, S18

### Forest greens and ban flower
- Vietnamese name: Rau rừng, hoa ban xào măng
- What it is: Forest vegetables, and ban flower stir-fried with sour bamboo, named on the national tourism desk’s Bản Mòng meal (S18). The commune page says rau rừng with the grill (S17)
- How to eat it: Beside the fish and the rice. A market bunch of bitter bamboo, măng đắng, is a gift line in the handbook and that line is tied to Mộc Châu shopping, not to a city stall (S6). Do not move the Mộc Châu gift counter onto this page
- When to order it: With the Bản Mòng meal
- Area, not a shop: Bản Mòng
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: `rau-rung` on `moc-chau` if copy needs the greens. Hoa ban xào is not a separate slug here
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S6, S17, S18

## Food streets and markets

### Central market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Trung tâm
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Buys the morning meal and the packed smoked meat in the city’s main market. Sở Công Thương, updated 10 Jul 2024, lists it at tổ 12, phường Chiềng Lề, 8,940 m², hạng 2, a solid hall, meeting daily, under a market board (S20). - When to go: Daily, on that 2024 row. The clock — Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- Area: Old Chiềng Lề, now phường Tô Hiệu
- Suggested slug: cho-trung-tam
- Photo must show: The market hall and the street in front, with stalls through the center. An empty gate fails

### Saturday stalls on Hoang Quoc Viet
- Vietnamese name: Hàng quán phố đi bộ Hoàng Quốc Việt
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eats from the stalls on the two Saturday nights a month when the street is closed to traffic. Báo Sơn La names ẩm thực among the stalls (S7). VOV in May 2026 shows the same night as a place parents worry about unlabeled sweets (S9). It is not a daily market
- When to go: 19:30–23:00 on a scheduled Saturday (S7, S8). Other nights — the street is an ordinary road
- Area: Hoàng Quốc Việt, phường Chiềng Cơi and phường Quyết Thắng
- Suggested slug: hoang-quoc-viet-stalls
- Photo must show: Night stalls along the walking street, with people and the road. Not the Mộc Châu night market
- Unpublished: no licensed photo
- Sources: S7, S8, S9

Chợ đầu mối nông sản Chiềng Cơi sits at bản Mé Ban, 1,370 m², hạng 3, which is the turn where the walking street ends (S7, S20). It is a wholesale produce market. It is not the visitor meal. No page.
