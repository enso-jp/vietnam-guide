# Research dossier — Gia Lai

Slug: `gia-lai`
Province: Gia Lai (merged province; former Bình Định and former Gia Lai). Provincial administrative center: Quy Nhơn. Pleiku is the highland city travelers use.
Kind: area
Corridor: highlands-coast
Base a traveler sleeps in: Pleiku, around Biển Hồ or the city center. Not a resort brand.
Checked: 21 Oct 2026

## Why a night here

- The picture they expect: morning mist on Biển Hồ (Hồ T’Nưng), a volcanic lake about 7 km north of Pleiku, with tea hills and pine on the drive toward Chu Đăng Ya. The provincial scenic list describes Biển Hồ as a trace of three extinct volcanoes (S5). Chu Đăng Ya, about 30 km northeast, is an inactive crater farmers plant with pumpkin, taro, and corn, bright with wild sunflowers in the dry season (S6, S7).
- Where they sleep, as an area: Pleiku city hotels and guesthouses, or lodgings closer to Biển Hồ for an early lake morning. The merger left Quy Nhơn as the provincial capital on the coast; that is a different climate and a different hotel decision.
- Where the evening is: Dien Hong food street in the Nguyen Thien Thuat–Tran Phu area of Dien Hong ward, opened as a formal food-street project in April 2026 after decades of night-market use on the same ground (S8). Weekend gong performances are also promoted for Saturday evenings at Dai Doan Ket Square and Nguyen Tat Thanh Square (S9).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): Dai Doan Ket Square for scheduled gong nights during festival weeks and regular weekend programmes (S9, S10). Minh Thanh Pagoda on the hill above Pleiku, recognizable by its tall golden tower (S2). They orient the evening; they are not a ranked bar list.
- Hero must include: Biển Hồ’s water, forested shores, and enough sky that a landscape card still reads as a lake, subject through the center. A portrait crop that loses the shoreline fails.
- A crop that would fail: only mist with no water; only a pagoda tower with no hill context; wild sunflowers without the crater bowl.

### Chu Đăng Ya volcano
- Vietnamese name: Núi lửa Chư Đăng Ya
- Kind: sight
- Area: Chư Đăng Ya commune, Chư Păh
- What the visitor does: Climbs from Ploi lagri village to the rim of an inactive volcano about 30 km northeast of Pleiku and 500 m above sea level on the national tourism article (S6, S7). The crater floor is farmland. The national article names late October to December for wild sunflowers on the slopes and notes an annual sunflower-related festival period since 2018 (S7). Steep, slippery sections are called out in the Vietnamese national tourism walk-up article (S11).
- Time: Half a day with driving from Pleiku
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED
- From the base: Road from Pleiku through the Biển Hồ scenic corridor (S6)
- When to go: Dry-season months for the climb and sunflowers (S2, S7)
- Mistake to avoid: Wet-season shoes on the rim paths
- Local interest: mountains, photography, rice
- Suggested slug: `chu-dang-ya`
- Suggested dot: 5 — worth building a day around in autumn
- Photo must show: the crater bowl or sunflower slopes with the volcano rim visible
- Sources: S6, S7, S11

## Lifestyle

### A Pleiku highland day
- Vietnamese name: Một ngày ở Pleiku
- Kind: lifestyle
- What a day here actually is: Coffee at a city cafe, then a morning run to Biển Hồ and the tea dam road. Midday two-bowl pho on Nguyen Van Troi or at the night market strip later. Afternoon drive to Chu Đăng Ya if the road is dry. Evening on Dien Hong food street or a Saturday gong square. The Vietnam Tourism feature names November–April as the kinder travel months and ties February–April to coffee flowers in the hills (S2). No source in this pass named a single breakfast street separate from pho shops and the food street.
- When: Dry-season mornings for the lake and volcano
- Area: Pleiku base, northern lake loop, northeast volcano
- Suggested slug: do not publish a lifestyle page
- Photo must show: lake morning or coffee country, not the coastal capital
- Sources: S2, S5, S8
