## Why a night here

### The west-coast sand, the surf, and the trees on the shore

- The picture they expect: Wake up on the west-coast sand. Vietnam Tourism’s island page says Long Beach has the swimming, the food, and the sunset over the ocean, and that this west coast is one of the only places in Vietnam for that sunset (S2). The day page calls Long Beach the heart of the island’s social life and says dinner and cocktails are served along it (S3). The bureau places Bãi Trường on the southwest shore, about 20 km from Mũi Dinh Cậu to Khóe Tàu Rũ, a gently sloping yellow sand, clear green water, and both sunrise and sunset (S10). The airport is not the picture. A pearl farm is not the picture. The north is the quieter sand, not the strip people book for the night (S2, S3).
- Where they sleep, as an area: Rooms along Long Beach, the west-coast resort strip (S2, S3). The day page says the national park’s foothills keep high-rise resorts off Gành Dầu, so the north is the other bed (S3). The bureau says the district has allowed a future eco-tourism project, Thuận An, of more than 20 ha on the north of Bãi Trường in xã Dương Tơ (S10). That sentence is a permission, not an open hotel. A hotel brand is not a pick. No property page was opened.
- Where the evening is: Long Beach, for food, cocktails, live music, DJs, and fire shows (S2, S3). In town, Dinh Cậu at sunset, then the squid boats’ lights on the water after the beach empties (S8). The night market near Dương Đông, where the stall grills the catch (S1).
- Scene landmarks (few, sourced, no prices): Dinh Cậu, https://vietnamtourism.vn/index.php/tourism/items/1968 (S8). The Phu Quoc Night Market, named on https://vietnam.travel/places-to-go/mekong-delta/phu-quoc with no street number (S1). The pearl page calls a night market chợ đêm Dinh Cậu (S16). ### Phu Quoc National Park
- Vietnamese name: Vườn quốc gia Phú Quốc (S12, S18)
- Kind: sight
- Area: Northeast of the island, in the old communes Bãi Thơm, Cửa Dương, and Hàm Ninh, on the tourism page (S12). The office page says the park lies entirely on the old thành phố Phú Quốc (S18).
- What the visitor does: Walks forest trails to streams, viewpoints, and quiet beaches. Vietnam Tourism calls a short version a half-day trek or a motorbike ride in the north (S1). The tourism page describes a 4-hour tour to Núi Chúa, 565 m, called the island’s roof, on the Hàm Ninh range. It names suối Tranh, suối Đá Bàn, and suối Đá Ngọn, and says Đá Ngọn falls in seven tiers. It names bãi Dương and bãi Thơm as quiet beaches, and the dishes rượu sim, cùi biên mai nướng, and cá suối (S12). The nature page sends a gentle hike to Suối Tranh for a swim, and a harder hike on the Hàm Ninh mountain trail (S4).
- Founded: Decision 91/2001/QĐ-TTg, 8 June 2001, upgrading a nature reserve, on the office page and on the NBCA page (S18, S20). UNESCO recognition as part of the Kien Giang Biosphere Reserve in 2006 on the office page (S18). The NBCA page dates that recognition 27 October 2006 (S20, S21). UNESCO’s own page says year of nomination 2006 and does not print 27 October (S19). The date and the year both stay.
- Area, all kept, do not pick one:
  - Over 31,422 ha, split into 8,786 ha strict protection, 22,603 ha ecological restoration, and 33 ha administration, on the tourism page and the NBCA page (S12, S20).
  - 31,422 ha, of which 12,794 ha is forest, on the district page, plus 929 plant species (S9).
  - 36,262 ha on the office page: 29,596 ha special-use forest and 6,666 ha protection forest. The same page says forested land is about 97 percent of the park and about 63 percent of Phú Quốc’s natural area (S18).
- Coral, all kept:
  - District page: 89 hard coral species, 19 soft, 62 seaweed species (S9).
  - Tourism page and NBCA park page: nearly 100 hard, nearly 20 soft, 62 seaweed (S12, S20).
  - UNESCO, for the whole reserve, not the park alone: 89 hard, 19 soft, 125 reef fishes, 132 molluscs, 32 echinoderms, 62 seaweed, plus seagrass used by turtles and dugong (S19).
  - NBCA biosphere page: 700 ha of coral, 87 species, and 12,000 ha of seagrass, 10 species (S21).
- Animals on the tourism page: 30 mammal species, 200 bird species, 50 reptile species, with the Red Book and IUCN counts printed there (S12). The NBCA park page repeats that list (S20). The biosphere page’s larger counts are for the whole reserve (S21). Do not swap them.
- Time: Half a day on Vietnam Tourism (S1). Four hours for the Núi Chúa tour on the tourism page (S12). Both stay.
- Fee: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. The office page says the park collects an ecotourism fee and prints no number (S18).
- Hours: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- From the base: Dương Đông – Hàm Ninh road, then about 10 km north, on the tourism page (S12). The office page prints 120 km from Rạch Giá and 45 km from Hà Tiên, which is the island’s distance, not the trailhead (S18).
- When to go: The dry window in `getting-there.md`. A trail closure calendar was not opened.
- Mistake to avoid: Quoting one hectare figure. Quoting the UNESCO coordinate as the park gate. Calling the park a UNESCO World Heritage site. The whisper page says “UNESCO-recognised National Park” (S6). UNESCO’s own page lists a biosphere reserve, not a World Heritage inscription (S19). Both sentences stay.
- Local interest: The northern forest
