## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Fish sauce
- Vietnamese name: Nước mắm Phú Quốc
- What it is: Anchovy fish sauce fermented in wooden vats. The association’s protected profile: dark cockroach-wing colour, a light aroma without a fishy or ammonia smell, salty and deeply sweet with a natural fat, nitrogen 20–43 gN/lít (S22). The tourism page says the first draw is about a year, the colour of the pure sauce is dark brown, clear, and thick, and it is salty, gently sweet, and fragrant of cá cơm sọc tiêu (S14). Vietnam Tourism says the taste can be briny and rounded, with sweetness, a mineral note, or caramel, and that it should smell strong but not stinky (S7).
- How to eat it: As the seasoning and the dipping sauce. The Vietnam Tourism page, speaking of the country, says a diluted sauce with vinegar and water goes with fried spring rolls, a teaspoon with chili and garlic goes into fishy soups, pure sauce goes with steamed vegetables, and a blend with sugar and lime goes with fresh rolls (S7). On the island, taste it at a barrel house (S1, S14).
- When to order it: Any meal. The fishery on the tourism page runs April–November (S14). That is the catch, not a restaurant hour.
- Area, not a shop: The barrel houses. Historically along the Dương Đông river (S14).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none opened
- Figures that disagree, all kept: about 6 million litres a year and trên 25ºC on the district page (S9). Over 10 million litres a year, and one cá cơm than workshop at 43 percent after 15 months, on the tourism page (S14). Association range 20–43 gN/lít, and “up to 40°N” in that page’s introduction (S22). Salt ratio 3 tons of fish to 1 ton of salt on the tourism page (S14). Salt ratio 2.5–3 fish to 1 salt on the association page (S22). Age about one year (S14), 12 months (S22), 15 months for that one workshop (S14).
- Sea, both kept: Gulf of Thailand, Kiên Giang and Cà Mau waters, on the association page (S22). “Clear Andaman Sea” on the Vietnam Tourism fish-sauce page (S7).
- Sources: S1, S7, S9, S14, S22

### Black pepper
- Vietnamese name: Hồ tiêu. Vietnam Tourism says black pepper (S1).
- What it is: Peppercorns from island gardens. The nature page calls them aromatic and spicy (S4). The whisper page says a floral aroma and intense heat (S6).
- How to eat it: Taste it at a garden, and take some home. The nature page also says Hàm Ninh’s boiled crab is dipped in muối tiêu chanh (S4, S15).
- When to order it: With the garden visit, in the morning on the whisper page’s pairing (S6). A harvest month was not printed.
- Area, not a shop: Inland gardens. No commune was printed.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S1, S4, S6, S15

### Bun quay
- Vietnamese name: Bún quây. The page also writes Bun Quay and “stirring vermicelli”. It says the dish arrived from central Vietnam in 1955 as ground shrimp and was called bún tôm until 1996 (S5). The day page writes bún quây (S3).
- What it is: Rice-flour noodles with a soup the cooks stir, plus fish, shrimp, squid, herbs, and onion. Each person mixes a dipping sauce of chili, sugar, fish sauce, and citrus (S5).
- How to eat it: Mix the sauce, then take the noodles and the seafood at the stand (S5).
- When to order it: Traditionally morning. The same page says it is now served through the day and into the evening (S5). This is the breakfast that was sourced. The day page sends the bowl to Long Beach or Dương Đông (S3).
- Area, not a shop: Dương Đông, on the street across from the harbour, and Long Beach (S3, S5). Two rooms are named in the notes. The page says the island now has over ten rooms (S5).
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S3, S5
