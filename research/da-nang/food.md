# Comprehensive list of local food to try

Checked: 20 Oct 2026; food streets and markets added 21 Oct 2026. No stall prices. Shop names on tourism pages are clues only and are marked so they are not published as picks.

### Mi Quang
- Vietnamese name: Mì Quảng
- What it is: Wide rice noodles with a shallow, turmeric-tinted broth, peanuts, herbs, and a toasted rice cracker. Toppings vary: shrimp, pork, chicken, or a quail egg. It is the bowl Da Nang points visitors to.
- How to eat it: Toss it. Break in the cracker. Do not drink it like pho. The broth is meant to stay shallow.
- When to order it: Lunch. The local handbook says people also eat it at other meals. Start at lunch.
- Area, not a shop: Cho Con, or Cho Han if you are already on the river. A Vietnam Tourism page names a shop. Do not publish as a pick.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: cao lau in Hoi An, which is a different noodle and a different town
- Sources: S19, S20, S21

### Bun cha ca
- Vietnamese name: Bún chả cá
- What it is: Fish cakes in a light broth. Vietnam Tourism describes the cake as mackerel and other fish, and the broth as fish bone with pumpkin, tomato, and pineapple.
- How to eat it: Lime, chili, and herbs at the table.
- When to order it: Morning, in the city, not as a beach breakfast.
- Area, not a shop: City morning counters. Named shops on the tourism page — do not publish as a pick.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19

### Banh xeo
- Vietnamese name: Bánh xèo
- What it is: A crisp rice-flour crepe, smaller than the southern version, with shrimp or pork and bean sprouts.
- How to eat it: Tear it, wrap the piece with herbs, and dip. Do not cut it with a fork.
- When to order it: A snack or a shared plate at the market, from late morning.
- Area, not a shop: Cho Han or Cho Con
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19, S21

### Pork in rice paper
- Vietnamese name: Bánh tráng cuốn thịt heo
- What it is: Thin slices of pork rolled in rice paper with herbs.
- How to eat it: Roll, then dip. The local dip is the point. Ask what is in the bowl if you do not eat fermented fish.
- When to order it: Lunch, with other plates
- Area, not a shop: Market counters and city lunch rooms. No shop named here.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S20, S21

### Seafood on the beach
- Vietnamese name: Hải sản
- What it is: Grilled fish, shellfish, and prawns landed for the beach restaurants.
- How to eat it: Point at what is fresh, ask for it grilled, and ask the price before it is cooked. If a number is not written up, it is not a known price.
- When to order it: Dinner, if you slept at My Khe
- Area, not a shop: The streets just inland of My Khe, and the shore road Vo Nguyen Giap. Vietnam Tourism also points at the Son Tra shore. Do not name a grill.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19, S21

### Nam O fish salad
- Vietnamese name: Gỏi cá Nam Ô
- What it is: A raw fish salad associated with Nam O, on the north shore toward Hai Van, dressed with fish broth and herbs.
- How to eat it: As a shared plate. It is not a market-breakfast bowl.
- When to order it: Lunch, if the ride north is already the plan
- Area, not a shop: Nam O. No restaurant named.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S20, S21

### Che
- Vietnamese name: Chè
- What it is: Sweet soups. Vietnam Tourism lists them among Da Nang sweets.
- How to eat it: With a spoon, in the afternoon
- When to order it: After lunch, at a market
- Area, not a shop: Cho Con
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19, S21

### Coffee
- Vietnamese name: Cà phê
- What it is: The usual iced coffee with condensed milk, and the stronger city cafes.
- How to eat it: Afternoon, sitting down. It is not the breakfast that teaches the city.
- When to order it: After the museum or after the beach
- Area, not a shop: Anywhere. Do not name a roaster.
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19

## Food streets and markets

Food-street lane checked 21 Oct 2026. Areas and markets, not a stall list. No stall prices.

### Huynh Thuc Khang food street
- Vietnamese name: Phố ẩm thực Huỳnh Thúc Kháng
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Walk the short central street of everyday kitchens. The city paper calls it a popular local-food street opened as a food street in December 2014. Dishes on the page include mi Quang, banh xeo, pork in rice paper, banh canh, bun mam, and morning plates. The street is narrow and suited to walk-up tables, not coach groups.
- When to go: Busiest for breakfast, about 06:30–09:00. The paper says the street runs through the day; breakfast is the crowded hour. Exact stall hours — Check on arrival.
- Area: Hai Chau center. The paper places it at the junction with Nguyen Van Linh, near Phan Chau Trinh and Hoang Dieu, Nam Duong ward (then Hai Chau).
- Fee: Free to walk. Stall prices — not recorded here.
- Suggested slug: huynh-thuc-khang
- Suggested dot: 4 — the city breakfast street a planning day can name without a restaurant list
- Photo must show: the street of low shopfronts with morning stools and steam, subject through the center. Not a single shop sign as the hero.
- Sources: S26

### Con Market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Cồn
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Eat in the indoor food court and the outdoor street-food yard: mi Quang, banh beo, banh xeo, banh canh, snails, che, and pork rice-paper rolls. The city tourism portal calls it a snack paradise and one of the largest city markets. Vietnam Tourism points visitors here for morning-to-dusk bowls.
- When to go: Morning into late afternoon. The city portal’s reference window is about 06:00–19:00; stalls vary. Check on arrival.
- Area: Hai Chau. Vietnam Tourism places it on Ong Ich Khiem. The English Fantasticity page says the crossroads of Quang Trung and Ong Ich Khiem. The Vietnamese Fantasticity market list gives 290 Hung Vuong, Hai Chau ward. Treat those as published addresses for the same market and confirm the door on arrival.
- Fee: Free to enter the market halls. Stall prices — not recorded here.
- Suggested slug: cho-con
- Suggested dot: 4 — the market lunch that teaches the city’s bowls
- Photo must show: indoor or outdoor food stalls with bowls and low stools, one frame. Not a dry-goods aisle alone.
- Sources: S21, S27, S28

### Han Market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Hàn
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Walk the river-side market for local food and specialties (cha bo, fish sauce, dried seafood) and a morning bowl. The city portal frames Con as the snack paradise and Han as the specialties-and-souvenir market beside the Han. Vietnam Tourism dates the market to the 1940s and places it by the Han River swing bridge.
- When to go: Morning. Portal reference hours about 06:00–19:00; Check on arrival for the food counters.
- Area: Hai Chau, near the Han River swing bridge. Fantasticity gives 119 Tran Phu, Hai Chau ward. An older Fantasticity shopping page also names the block framed by Tran Phu, Bach Dang, Hung Vuong, and Nguyen Thai Hoc.
- Fee: Free to enter. Stall prices — not recorded here.
- Suggested slug: han-market
- Suggested dot: 3 — useful with Con Market, not a second full food day by itself
- Photo must show: the market hall or the river-side front, with food or specialty counters in frame
- Sources: S21, S28, S29

### Vo Nguyen Giap and Son Tra seafood shore
- Vietnamese name: Hải sản Võ Nguyên Giáp / Sơn Trà
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Point at fresh shellfish and fish at the stalls along Vo Nguyen Giap and the Son Tra coastal road, then have them cooked. Vietnam Tourism names these shores for the catch of the day. Ask the price before it is cooked if nothing is written up.
- When to go: Dinner, if you slept at My Khe or after a Son Tra ride
- Area: My Khe beach road (Vo Nguyen Giap) and the Son Tra shore road
- Fee: Free to walk. Grill prices — Check on arrival, ask before cooking
- Suggested slug: vo-nguyen-giap-seafood
- Suggested dot: 4 — the beach dinner street the city food pages already point to
- Photo must show: grilled seafood or iced shellfish stalls with the beach road or shore in the same frame. Not a hotel lobby.
- Sources: S19, S21

### Son Tra night market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ đêm Sơn Trà
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Walk the night stalls for street food, fresh seafood, and souvenirs after dark. The city tourism portal places it near Dragon Bridge and My Khe, and points visitors there after the beach or the dragon show.
- When to go: Evening. Portal reference hours 17:00–23:00; busiest about 19:00–21:00 on that page. Check on arrival.
- Area: Vo Van Kiet, An Hai ward, at the foot of Dragon Bridge (portal wording)
- Fee: Free to walk. Stall prices — not recorded here.
- Suggested slug: son-tra-night-market
- Suggested dot: 3 — a night food walk when Helio is closed or you are already on the bridge
- Photo must show: night stalls and lights with the bridge or river edge nearby. Not an empty car park.
- Sources: S28

### Helio night market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ đêm Helio
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Vietnam Tourism still names Helio among the city’s night markets. The city tourism portal’s September 2025 notice said Helio Night Market, Helio Play, and Helio Kids closed from 5 Sep 2025 for renovation, with a hoped return at the end of that year. No reopening notice was found on that portal on 21 Oct 2026. Do not plan dinner here until the door is open again.
- When to go: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED for a current open night
- Area: Duong 2/9, Hoa Cuong Bac, Hai Chau (portal address for Helio Center)
- Fee: Portal previously listed free entry. Confirm if it reopens.
- Suggested slug: helio-night-market
- Suggested dot: do not publish a pick while the suspension notice is the last official word
- Photo must show: only if it is open again — night food halls with people at tables. Do not use a closed-gate frame as a food hero.
- Sources: S21, S30
