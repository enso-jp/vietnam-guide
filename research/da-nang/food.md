# Comprehensive list of local food to try

Checked: 21 Oct 2026. No stall prices. Shop names on tourism pages are clues only and are marked so they are not published as picks. Food streets were added this pass. The dish list is still not every bowl the city portal names.

### Mi Quang
- Vietnamese name: Mì Quảng
- What it is: Wide rice noodles with a shallow, turmeric-tinted broth, peanuts, herbs, and a toasted rice cracker. Toppings vary: shrimp, pork, chicken, or a quail egg. It is the bowl Da Nang points visitors to.
- How to eat it: Toss it. Break in the cracker. Do not drink it like pho. The broth is meant to stay shallow.
- When to order it: Lunch. The local handbook says people also eat it at other meals. Start at lunch.
- Area, not a shop: Cho Con, or Cho Han if you are already on the river. A Vietnam Tourism page names a shop. Do not publish as a pick.
- Also on the table: An 18 May 2020 city page says one counter on Lê Hồng Phong offered about ten bowls: chicken, beef, shrimp and pork, frog, eel, jellyfish, snakehead, spare rib, egg, and vegetarian. It does not name the shop. Do not publish a shop.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: cao lau in Hoi An, which is a different noodle and a different town
- Sources: S19, S20, S21

### Bun cha ca
- Vietnamese name: Bún chả cá
- What it is: Fish cakes in a light broth. Vietnam Tourism describes the cake as mackerel and other fish, and the broth as fish bone with pumpkin, tomato, and pineapple.
- How to eat it: Lime, chili, and herbs at the table.
- When to order it: Morning, in the city, not as a beach breakfast.
- Area, not a shop: City morning counters. Named shops on the tourism page — do not publish as a pick.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19

### Banh xeo
- Vietnamese name: Bánh xèo
- What it is: A crisp rice-flour crepe, smaller than the southern version, with shrimp or pork and bean sprouts.
- How to eat it: Tear it, wrap the piece with herbs, and dip. Do not cut it with a fork.
- When to order it: A snack or a shared plate at the market, from late morning.
- Area, not a shop: Cho Han or Cho Con
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19, S21

### Pork in rice paper
- Vietnamese name: Bánh tráng cuốn thịt heo
- What it is: Thin slices of pork rolled in rice paper with herbs.
- How to eat it: Roll, then dip. The local dip is the point. Ask what is in the bowl if you do not eat fermented fish.
- When to order it: Lunch, with other plates
- Area, not a shop: Market counters and city lunch rooms. No shop named here.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S20, S21

### Seafood on the beach
- Vietnamese name: Hải sản
- What it is: Grilled fish, shellfish, and prawns landed for the beach restaurants.
- How to eat it: Point at what is fresh, ask for it grilled, and ask the price before it is cooked. If a number is not written up, it is not a known price.
- When to order it: Dinner, if you slept at My Khe
- Area, not a shop: The shore roads Hoàng Sa, Võ Nguyên Giáp, and Trường Sa, which the city portal names as one seafood strip. Vietnam Tourism also points at the Son Tra shore. Do not name a grill.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S19, S21

### Nam O fish salad
- Vietnamese name: Gỏi cá Nam Ô
- What it is: A raw fish salad associated with Nam O, on the north shore toward Hai Van, dressed with fish broth and herbs. An 18 May 2020 city page says the fish is herring (cá trích) and ties the dish to the Nam Ô fish-sauce village.
- How to eat it: As a shared plate. It is not a market-breakfast bowl.
- When to order it: Lunch, if the ride north is already the plan
- Area, not a shop: Nam O. No restaurant named.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S20, S21

## Food streets and markets

The city portal treats two streets as the pair: Huỳnh Thúc Kháng in the morning, Phạm Hồng Thái at night (S30, S31). Chợ Hàn and Chợ Cồn are the two traditional markets the city picked, from 2022, as tourist-point markets (S32). Clocks and street numbers disagree across portal pages. Both figures are kept. No stall price is copied. A gate fee is written only where a page states one.

### Huynh Thuc Khang breakfast street
- Vietnamese name: Phố điểm tâm Huỳnh Thúc Kháng
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Walk the street and pick a morning bowl. The 19 Jun 2019 roundup lists xôi, bánh canh, bánh cuốn, bánh bèo, bánh xèo, mì Quảng, cao lầu, bò kho, ốp la, cháo lòng, and cháo trắng with kho cá cơm. The 18 May 2020 page lists bánh canh, bánh bèo, xôi, bò kho, bánh mì ốp la, cháo trắng with cá kho, and cơm gà, and says the street is crowded from 06:00 to 08:00. The 19 Jun 2019 page says the street was recognized in late 2014, with 45 shops. The decision number is not on that page.
- When to go: Morning. Busy 06:00–08:00 (S30). The same portal’s 2019 roundup prints 06:00–12:00 (S26).
- Area: Đường Huỳnh Thúc Kháng, Đà Nẵng. No house number on the street entries. Hai Châu is the usual reading; the portal pages above do not print a ward.
- Fee: No gate fee is stated. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- From the base: Walk if you slept in the river wards. From Mỹ Khê the ride is Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- Mistake to avoid: Treating one door on the street as the dish. The street is the destination. Cao lầu on this list is still the Hoi An noodle.
- Local interest: Breakfast
- Suggested slug: huynh-thuc-khang
- Suggested dot: 4 — the breakfast street the city portal names, not a day by itself

### Han Market
- Vietnamese name: Chợ Hàn
- Kind: food-street
- What the visitor does: Shop the hall and eat in the food zone the market company built in from Bạch Đằng. The 28 Sep 2024 market page says the hall was rebuilt in 1990 on two floors and about 3,000 m², and that it sells fresh seafood, fruit, fish sauce, and dried central-coast food as well as clothes and áo dài. The 14 Mar 2025 ministry reprint says that in season and on holidays the market sees about 4,000–6,000 visits a day. This is a shopping market that also feeds you. Chợ Cồn is the louder food market.
- When to go: The 9 Oct 2023 markets page prints 06:00–19:00. The 28 Sep 2024 market page does not restate a clock. Morning is the food and the fresh seafood. The ministry page does not give a separate food-zone hour.
- Area: Near the Han River swing bridge. The 2024 page outlines the block as Trần Phú, Bạch Đằng, Hùng Vương, and Nguyễn Thái Học. The 2023 page says 119 Trần Phú and outlines Trần Phú, Hùng Vương, Bạch Đằng, and Nguyễn Văn Linh. The fourth street disagrees. Use the 2024 page for the block and the 2023 page for the door number and the clock.
- Fee: No gate fee is stated. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- From the base: Walk from the river. The Cham Museum is the nearby sight already on the site.
- Mistake to avoid: Expecting the Cồn food court. Hàn is the dried-food and souvenir hall, with a food zone off Bạch Đằng.
- Local interest: Markets
- Suggested slug: han-market
- Suggested dot: 4 — the river market, a stop inside a downtown morning
- Photo must show: the hall from the Bạch Đằng side, dried goods or the food aisle through the center. A frame that is only the swing bridge loses the market.
- Sources: S27, S28, S32
