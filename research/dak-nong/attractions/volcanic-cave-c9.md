## What it is

## What the visitor does

- The scene, in order: A permitted visit to the lava tube near Chu Bluk, in Buon Choah, Krong No. The local name is Hang Doi. The tube is about 730 m northwest of the Chu Bluk crater. Length 217 m. Depth 22.6 m. The northern downstream mouth is a wide round collapse, with ferns (S11).
- How long on site: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- How long from Gia Nghia: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED. No kilometre count was printed on S11.
- Mistake that wastes the visit: Walking in without an authorized guide. After a death in cave C7 on 19 Mar 2025, Krong No banned self-guided entry to the volcanic caves. The culture department said caves such as C7 are not yet opened as a tourism product (S12).

## Where the cave system figures disagree

- Handbook: the system was found in 2017, more than 50 caves, nearly 10,000 m, formed more than 140 million years ago, with prehistoric traces about 6,000–7,000 years old (S1).
- UNESCO: Nam Blang lava field discovered in 2007, Southeast Asia’s largest, over 10 km, about 50 caves, used as shelter about 6,000–10,000 years ago. The geopark’s volcanism is the last 16.5 million years, not a 140-million-year tube (S5).
- Print both discovery years, both prehistoric ranges, and do not use the handbook’s 140 million years as the age of this lava. C9’s own page is the 217 m tube (S11).
