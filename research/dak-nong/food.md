# Food — Dak Nong

Checked: 21 Oct 2026

Highland meals are tied to festivals, buon kitchens, and river fish. There is no sourced food-street row with primary hours on the provincial portal. Do not invent a market night.

## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Com lam
- Vietnamese name: Cơm lam
- What it is: Sticky rice cooked in bamboo tubes over coals, a staple at M'Nong and Ma gatherings (S1, S6).
- How to eat it: Break the tube, peel the charred shell, and eat with grilled meat or fish — gesture from handbook description (S1).
- When to order it: At festivals and buon meals — Check on arrival for everyday stalls.
- Area, not a shop: Gia Nghia and highland buons
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none on this site yet
- Sources: S1, S6

### Grilled catfish Serepok
- Vietnamese name: Cá lăng nướng sông Sêrêpok
- What it is: Red-tailed catfish from the Serepok through Krong No and Cu Jut, grilled in forest leaves (S8).
- How to eat it: With com lam and forest-pepper salt — Check on arrival for the exact dip recipe.
- When to order it: Lunch or dinner near the river corridors
- Area, not a shop: Krong No and Cu Jut districts
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S8

### Ruou can
- Vietnamese name: Rượu cần
- What it is: Fermented rice wine drunk through a shared bamboo straw at ceremonies, not for everyday use (S9).
- How to eat it: Communal jar at festivals — not a bar crawl.
- When to order it: Ceremonies and guest welcomes — Check on arrival.
- Area, not a shop: Highland communities across the province
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: `ca-phe` exists site-wide for coffee country
- Sources: S9

### Canh thut
- Vietnamese name: Canh thụt
- What it is: Bamboo-shoot and forest-vegetable soup associated with M'Nong kitchens (S1).
- How to eat it: Check on arrival — UNSOURCED for the serving gesture.
