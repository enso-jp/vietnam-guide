## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Bánh tráng Phú Hòa Đông
- Vietnamese name: bánh tráng. The recognized craft is nghề sản xuất bánh tráng (S15).
- What it is: rice paper made at xã Phú Hòa Đông. The 14 Oct 2024 decision names the craft and the old commune. It does not describe a taste (S15). The national tourism page quotes a maker: the main ingredient is rice flour; in the past a little sticky-rice flour; some kilns now use wheat flour as well; coconut water, salt, sugar, and sesame are added depending on the batch (S16). The industry magazine describes the older mix as rice flour, water, and salt, poured thin, lifted with a bamboo tube, and dried in the sun (S17). Both mixes stay.
- How to eat it: the decision and the two articles do not print a serving. The sheets are dried and stacked. The magazine says a stack is counted to 100 (S17). A plate, a sauce, and a meal around it were not printed. Check on arrival — UNSOURCED.
- When to order it: the magazine says a household starts at 2:00 (S17). The tourism page says the lanes are busy before Tết, when a quoted household goes from about 18–20 kg of rice toward about 25 kg (S16). Those are production hours, not a restaurant hour.
- Area, not a shop: xã Phú Hòa Đông, on the river. “Khoảng hơn 40km” from the city centre (S16). “Khoảng 40km” (S17). Both stay. Clause 128 keeps the name xã Phú Hòa Đông and adds the old xã Tân Thạnh Tây and xã Tân Thạnh Đông (S14).
- Counts, both stay: about 100 kilns, about 40 tons a day, about two-thirds export, about 6,000 workers (S16). HTV prints 62 establishments, of which 10 enterprises, 1 cooperative, and 41 business households, and also 20 households still working by hand, plus one firm at 15–20 tons a day (S18). Do not average them.
- Age, one page: “hơn 80 năm” (S17). The decision does not print a start year (S15).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none. This paper is not a Ho Chi Minh City dish page.
- Sources: S14, S15, S16, S17, S18
