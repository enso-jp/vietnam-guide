## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Eel porridge
- Vietnamese name: Cháo lươn Vinh
- What it is: A thin porridge. The city page says the rice is cooked as whole grains, not pounded, with a little sticky rice so it stays silky, and that the eel is seasoned and stir-fried and then folded in, not boiled to pieces in the pot (S10). It contrasts the bowl with cháo vịt Vân Đình and cháo dê Ninh Bình, which it calls thicker (S10).
- How to eat it: Mix the eel through the porridge. Pepper, chili, and herbs go on at the table. The page says bánh mì or bánh mướt on the side (S10).
- When to order it: The city page says the bowl shows itself in cool weather (S10). The 24-hour page treats it as the bowl that means you have arrived, any meal (S11).
- Area, not a shop: The center. Đường Hồ Sỹ Dương is the night stretch a Nghệ An news page names for this bowl (S20).
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: None on this site. Súp lươn is the sister bowl below, not a neighbor city.
- Sources: S10, S11, S20

### Eel soup
- Vietnamese name: Súp lươn
- What it is: The other eel bowl. A 2025 news page says the eel is small field eel, soaked in rice water, blanched, pulled off the bone, stir-fried with shallot, turmeric, and chili, and the bones are crushed for the broth (S12). The city page lists it beside cháo lươn (S10).
- How to eat it: With bánh mì or bánh mướt (S10, S12).
- When to order it: Morning on the 2025 page, which starts the day with this bowl (S12). Also at night where the porridge street is (S11).
- Area, not a shop: The center.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: None.
- Sources: S10, S11, S12

### Steamed rice sheets
- Vietnamese name: Bánh mướt
- What it is: The city page describes bánh mướt Diễn Châu as finger-length rolls, soft and translucent, rice flour only, fried shallot inside (S10). In Vinh it is a breakfast, eaten with giò lụa or chả (S11). Diễn Châu is another commune of Nghệ An, not this slug. The sheet is eaten here.
- How to eat it: With nước mắm, lime, and fresh chili, or beside grilled meat when it is a lunch (S10). At breakfast, with the sausage or pork loaf (S11).
- When to order it: Morning (S11).
- Area, not a shop: The center. A Diễn Châu village kitchen was not given a street in Vinh.
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: None. There is no `dien-chau` slug.
- Sources: S10, S11

### Jumping squid
- Vietnamese name: Mực nhảy (mực một nắng)
- What it is: The beach specialty. The national beach page names mực nhảy and mực câu (S3). The city food page says to ask for charcoal, and for mực một nắng when you want the dried-one-day squid of this coast (S11). A record claim for mực nháy Cửa Lò is the city food-culture page’s wording (S19). It stays that page’s claim.
- How to eat it: Grilled, at night, on the sidewalk in town or by the beach (S11, S12). The 2025 page also names it steamed.
- When to order it: Evening, with the beach day (S11, S12).
- Area, not a shop: Cửa Lò, and the night sidewalks in town. Not a restaurant name.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: None.
- Sources: S3, S11, S12, S19
