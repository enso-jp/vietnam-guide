## Comprehensive list of local food to try

### Coconut candy
- Vietnamese name: Kẹo dừa
- What it is: Soft candy. Vietnam Tourism calls the plain piece a blend of coconut milk and sugar, and says more than 300 coconut-candy factories are in the area (S29). The provincial portal, 25 November 2024, prints coconut milk, maltose from sprouted glutinous rice, and yellow cane sugar, more than 180 workshops, and tens of thousands of tons a year, and names later flavors: sầu riêng, đậu phộng, cacao, lá dứa (S33). Elders on that article place the craft in the 1930s at Mỏ Cày, now Mỏ Cày Nam, under the old name kẹo Mỏ Cày (S33). Both counts stand. The rural page shows the boil, the cut, and the wrap at Cồn Phụng and does not print a count (S6).
- How to eat it: Warm, at the kiln, or wrapped to take home. The portal sends the town boat to a kiln for a hot piece (S8).
- When to order it: On the boat day, at the workshop, not as a restaurant course (S6, S8).
- Area, not a shop: Cồn Phụng, and the kilns the town boat stops at. No shop is the pick.
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S6, S8, S29, S33

### My Long rice paper
- Vietnamese name: Bánh tráng Mỹ Lồng
- What it is: Rice paper from sticky rice and coconut milk, plus sugar, salt, sesame, and sometimes milk or egg (S9). Listed as national intangible heritage with Sơn Đốc by Quyết định số 4069/QĐ-BVHTTDL on 30/10/2018 (S9). Two origin stories are on that page: about 100 years, from the elders, and a dry-ration story from the Nguyễn–Trịnh years (S9). Keep both.
- How to eat it: Grilled, as a snack, or carried home. The portal says the old use was the Tết altar (S9).
- When to order it: At the village any day. The yards run longer near Tết (S9).
- Area, not a shop: Mỹ Lồng, old xã Mỹ Thạnh, Giồng Trôm (S9). The 2025 clause renames that merge xã Lương Phú and does not erase the craft name (S2).
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S2, S9

### Son Doc cracker
- Vietnamese name: Bánh phồng Sơn Đốc
- What it is: A glutinous-rice cracker with coconut milk that puffs to 3–4 times its size on the fire (S9). Same 2018 heritage decision (S9). More than 100 years on that page, first as a Tết sweet (S9).
- How to eat it: Grilled over charcoal, with tea. The portal mentions milk, egg, durian, and jackfruit versions in recent years (S9).
- When to order it: All year, and near Tết when the pounding runs late (S9).
- Area, not a shop: Sơn Đốc, xã Hưng Nhượng, Giồng Trôm. The portal quotes 51 households and about 25 million crackers a year (S9). One page.
- Meal: sweet
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S9

### Hu tieu with pate
- Vietnamese name: Hủ tiếu patê
- What it is: The town breakfast noodle the portal says is the one to eat in the city, with a pork-pâté slice it claims is local (S8). The bowl is the delta rice noodle. The neighbor page is hủ tiếu Mỹ Tho.
- How to eat it: A morning bowl, before the museum, is the portal’s patê bowl (S8). The 9 June 2021 article describes the slice as a house chả of da đầu heo, mỡ heo, thịt nạc heo, and tiêu hạt, dai giòn and beo béo, and says the counter has made it for more than 20 years (S30). The same article names hủ tiếu hoành thánh, hải sản, and xương thịt at that counter. Those are not the patê bowl. Vietnam Tourism describes a dinner bowl of the same noodle with pork, shrimp, a quail egg, lime, and cilantro (S29). Keep both plates. Do not fold the patê into the quail-egg bowl.
- When to order it: Breakfast (S8).
- Area, not a shop: Đường 30 Tháng 4. One shop address is a locator in the notes, not a pick (S8).
- Meal: breakfast
- Neighbor dish to link: `hu-tieu-my-tho`
- Sources: S8, S29, S30

### Coconut heart
- Vietnamese name: Củ hủ dừa
- What it is: A light salad of coconut heart, named by Vietnam Tourism with the coconut rice and the coconut-braised shrimp (S29). The provincial portal, 4 June 2021, names the same heart as gỏi củ hủ dừa tôm thịt: củ hủ dừa, tôm sú, thịt ba chỉ, rau răm, đậu phộng rang, hành tím phi, and cà rốt, in a lime-and-sugar dressing (S32). The article says the heart is the youngest shoot, white and mildly sweet, and that a tree is cut to take it, and that some palms are grown for the heart (S32). It says this salad and cơm hấp trong trái dừa were on a Vietkings list of 100 dishes for 2011–2016 (S32). That listing is the portal’s account. Keep Vietnam Tourism’s “light salad” beside the prawn-and-pork gỏi. They are one dish.
- How to eat it: At lunch, with those coconut plates (S29). A recipe was not printed.
- When to order it: Lunch on the coconut day (S29).
- Area, not a shop: The coconut groves and the islet lunch. No shop.
- Meal: main
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S29, S32

### Honey tea
- Vietnamese name: Trà mật ong
- What it is: Tea mixed with honey, and on the town boat “trà mật ong phấn hoa” the guest mixes (S8). The rural page says honey tea in a coconut-leaf house at Cồn Phụng (S6). A flower source for the Cồn Quy longan honey is the orchard page’s longan, not a second tea recipe (S7).
- How to eat it: In the garden, sitting down, not as a cafe order in town (S6, S8).
- When to order it: Mid-morning on the boat (S8).
- Area, not a shop: The orchard stop, Cồn Phụng or the town-boat garden (S6, S8)
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S6, S7, S8

### Young coconut
- Vietnamese name: Dừa tươi
- What it is: Drinking coconut, the fruit the province is named for. The portal serves it on the firefly boat with fruit (S8). The 25 November 2024 candy article also says a glass of fresh coconut water in the garden (S33). The rural page’s crop figures disagree and are kept in interests, not as a price (S7, S12, S22). S34’s “over 200,000 ha” is recorded in interests and is not a price.
- How to eat it: Cut open, with a straw, on the boat or in a yard (S8).
- When to order it: Any stop on the water (S8).
- Area, not a shop: The boat and the orchards
- Meal: drink
- Neighbor dish to link: none
- Sources: S8, S33
